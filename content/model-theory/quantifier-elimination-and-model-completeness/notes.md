## History

Elimination of quantifiers is older than model theory. In the 1920s C. H. Langford used it to show that the theory of dense linear orders without endpoints is complete. Mojżesz Presburger, in 1929, eliminated quantifiers from the additive theory of the integers once congruences are allowed, and so proved that this theory is complete and decidable; the [$\Z$-groups](/VKC9CR37FT/) are his theory written with the predicates $D_n$. The method was then a tool for proving completeness and decidability, one theory at a time.

Alfred Tarski proved [quantifier elimination for real closed fields](/FSJ9NGRRPA/) and with it a decision procedure for elementary algebra and geometry. His monograph *A Decision Method for Elementary Algebra and Geometry* appeared in 1948, and a second edition followed in 1951. Abraham Seidenberg gave a different proof in 1954. The geometric form, that projections of semialgebraic sets are semialgebraic, is the Tarski–Seidenberg theorem. The analogue for algebraically closed fields, Chevalley's theorem that projections of constructible sets are constructible, comes from algebraic geometry.

Abraham Robinson brought the subject into algebra. In *On the Metamathematics of Algebra* (1951) and *Complete Theories* (1956) he introduced [model completeness](/YR6HCMABTE/) and the model completion, proved [his test](/QHEADWYQ64/), and showed that algebraically closed fields and real closed fields are the model completions of fields and of ordered fields. He used the model completeness of real closed fields to give a new proof of Artin's solution of Hilbert's seventeenth problem. W. R. Scott had studied [algebraically closed groups](/5GS2AE651W/) in 1951, an early case of [existentially closed models](/6M98MCW575/). Per Lindström gave a test by categoricity: a $\forall\exists$ theory in a countable signature with only infinite models that is categorical in some infinite cardinal is model complete. Paul Eklof and Gabriel Sabbagh proved that an [inductive](/G8TRE2KSRV/) theory has a model companion exactly when its existentially closed models form an elementary class. Around 1970 Robinson adapted Cohen's forcing to model theory, in a finite and an infinite version, to study theories such as groups that have no model companion.

The search for efficient elimination went on in parallel. Michael Fischer and Michael Rabin showed in 1974 that every decision procedure for Presburger arithmetic takes doubly exponential time in the worst case. George Collins published [cylindrical algebraic decomposition](/GE8FCGKPTR/) in 1975, the first practical algorithm for real quantifier elimination. James Davenport and Joos Heintz showed in 1988 that real quantifier elimination is doubly exponential in the worst case. For valued fields, James Ax and Simon Kochen, and independently Yuri Ershov, proved in the 1960s the transfer principle that [relative elimination](/WMC3J66FAY/) now expresses, and Angus Macintyre proved quantifier elimination for the $p$-adic numbers in 1976, with predicates for $n$-th powers.

## Further reading

- David Marker, *Model Theory: An Introduction* (2002). Chapter 3 proves the tests and treats algebraically closed and real closed fields with full algebraic detail.
- Wilfrid Hodges, *A Shorter Model Theory* (1997). Good on existentially closed models and model companions.
- C. C. Chang and H. J. Keisler, *Model Theory*, third edition (1990). The classical account of model completeness, model completions and Robinson's test.
- Joram Hirschfeld and William H. Wheeler, *Forcing, Arithmetic, Division Rings* (1975). A monograph on Robinson's finite and infinite forcing and existentially closed structures.
- Saugata Basu, Richard Pollack and Marie-Françoise Roy, *Algorithms in Real Algebraic Geometry* (2003). Cylindrical algebraic decomposition and the complexity of real quantifier elimination.
