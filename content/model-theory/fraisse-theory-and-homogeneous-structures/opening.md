The age of a structure is the class of finitely generated structures that [embed](/SCRB74SWQN/) into it. The age of $(\Q, <)$ is the class of finite linear orders, and the age of a graph is a class of finite graphs. Roland Fraïssé asked in 1954 which classes are ages of especially symmetric countable structures, and his answer is a recipe for building them. Let $\mathcal{K}$ be a class of finitely generated structures with countably many isomorphism types. Suppose that it is closed under [substructures](/676H7EGTKJ/), that any two members embed into a third, and that it has the [amalgamation property](/XSCSX39GEZ/): two members that share a common part fit into a third member along that part. Then there is a countable structure whose age is exactly $\mathcal{K}$ and which is ultrahomogeneous, meaning that every isomorphism between finitely generated substructures extends to an automorphism. It is unique up to isomorphism, by a [back-and-forth](/6F2DB4HTJR/) argument, and it is called the [Fraïssé limit](/HJVRBDQSGG/) of $\mathcal{K}$.

The examples are basic objects of mathematics. Finite linear orders give $(\Q, <)$. Finite graphs give the [Rado graph](/RMXR0FEF54/), the countable random graph. Finite Boolean algebras give the countable atomless Boolean algebra, and finite graphs with no triangle give a triangle-free Henson graph. In a finite relational language the limit has an $\aleph_0$-categorical theory with quantifier elimination, so Fraïssé theory is also a source of examples for Chapters 4.6 and 4.8. The method reaches beyond logic. Lachlan and Woodrow classified the countable ultrahomogeneous graphs in 1980. The automorphism group of a countable structure is a closed subgroup of the symmetric group of $\N$, and Kechris, Pestov and Todorcevic showed in 2005 that the topological dynamics of the automorphism group of a Fraïssé limit matches a Ramsey property of its age.
