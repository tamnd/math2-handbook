[Model theory](/HFTBG5PMXC/) studies mathematical structures through the first-order sentences that are true in them. A [structure](/983GZBQYT4/) is a set with functions, relations and constants, such as a group, a field, an ordered set or a graph, and a [model](/MVCH6N9SNW/) of a theory is a structure in which every sentence of the theory is true. The subject asks which classes of structures a theory can describe, how many models of each size it has, which structures no sentence can tell apart, and which sets a formula can define inside one structure. Hodges called it algebraic geometry minus fields, because it treats the [definable sets](/CY1TXHF6KQ/) of a structure as algebraic geometry treats the zero sets of polynomials.

The results reach well beyond logic. The [Ax–Grothendieck theorem](/M2XAN2HGZH/), that an injective polynomial map $\C^n \to \C^n$ is surjective, follows from the finite fields by a transfer principle. The [Ax–Kochen theorem](/PHT3TJCS90/) transfers statements between $p$-adic fields and fields of power series by ultraproducts. Hrushovski's proof of the Mordell–Lang conjecture for function fields and Pila's proof of cases of the André–Oort conjecture use geometric stability and o-minimality. [Nonstandard analysis](/WM62GKCBWN/) gives rigorous infinitesimals, and [finite model theory](/4XRVJE0KAN/) ties logic to computational complexity. Volume 2 holds the syntax of first-order logic and its completeness and compactness theorems; this volume starts from them and studies the structures.

## What the volume teaches

- **A structure has a theory, and a theory has models.** The [theory of a structure](/X9HV9C6X4F/) is the set of sentences it satisfies; structures with the same theory are [elementarily equivalent](/P3B1V182N4/).
- **First-order logic cannot fix the size of an infinite structure.** By the [Löwenheim–Skolem theorem](/484M0TZ1S7/), a theory with an infinite model has models of every infinite cardinality at least the size of its language, and the [Tarski–Vaught test](/60SA99BER7/) builds the small ones.
- **Ultraproducts combine many structures into one.** By [Łoś's theorem](/AGYBSTD7BG/), a sentence holds in an [ultraproduct](/AX9Q86GB8M/) exactly when it holds in almost all factors.
- **Games measure what a sentence can say.** By the [Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé theorem](/72BMYXGFA8/), two structures agree on sentences of quantifier rank $n$ exactly when Duplicator wins the $n$-round game.
- **Types record what a tuple can say.** A [type](/9V71Q3SD1G/) is the set of formulas a tuple satisfies. Every type is realized in an elementary extension, countably many non-isolated types can be omitted by the [omitting types theorem](/7MFGBVDVG9/), and [saturated models](/RMTZ5J64FX/) realize as many types as possible.
- **Countable models are counted by types.** By the [Ryll-Nardzewski theorem](/1513J40TKA/), a complete countable theory with infinite models has one countable model up to isomorphism exactly when it has finitely many $n$-types for each $n$.
- **Finite pieces build homogeneous limits.** By [Fraïssé's theorem](/V5CKNFP3HC/), a suitable class of finitely generated structures is the age of exactly one countable [ultrahomogeneous structure](/H8VM37SSAX/), such as the [Rado graph](/RMXR0FEF54/).
- **Definable sets carry the geometry.** [Quantifier elimination](/7FDDXV3JK7/) for algebraically closed and real closed fields describes their definable sets, and the later chapters classify theories by how tame these sets are.
- **Form of axioms matches closure of models.** By the [Łoś–Tarski theorem](/9RN0KYXNT1/), the models of a theory are closed under substructures exactly when it has universal axioms, and by [Beth's theorem](/RJBDSA2ETP/) an implicit definition is explicit.
- **Structures live inside other structures.** An [interpretation](/7R0SJPH37J/) builds one structure from definable sets and quotients of another, and [imaginary elements](/Q0HM88QQKN/) make those quotients into elements.
- **Indiscernibles give models with prescribed shape.** [Ehrenfeucht–Mostowski models](/TSGB29FJM0/) are generated by [indiscernible sequences](/4016QKAHFB/) and inherit automorphisms from their index order.

## The chapters

**Chapter 4.1, Basic notions of model theory**, fixes the vocabulary: [substructures](/676H7EGTKJ/), [embeddings](/SCRB74SWQN/), [reducts and expansions](/EY20BF96SJ/), [complete theories](/655K8Y7VS8/), [elementary classes](/MEC3ZMC4F1/), definable sets and [properties that are not first-order](/T1S9T0RKHJ/).

**Chapter 4.2, Elementary extensions and the Löwenheim–Skolem theorems**, compares a structure with its extensions. [Elementary substructures](/8WZF9SBP39/), the [diagram lemmas](/EP5E317TNW/), [Skolem functions](/NKQY9NB955/) and the [elementary chain theorem](/NHCRZ74JHH/) are the tools of later constructions.

**Chapter 4.3, Ultraproducts and product constructions**, starts from [reduced products](/F4WRD396PB/). Łoś's theorem gives [compactness via ultraproducts](/EY09JT7270/), the [Keisler–Shelah theorem](/A37HPCKCE2/) describes elementary equivalence by ultrapowers, and the [Feferman–Vaught theorem](/RYMZZ2AWFT/) computes the theory of a product.

**Chapter 4.4, Back-and-forth methods and Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé games**, proves isomorphism and equivalence step by step: the [back-and-forth method](/6F2DB4HTJR/), the [Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game](/2RE7YAZK8R/), [Karp's theorem](/KAWWZ2DF0G/) and [non-definability proofs by games](/VT2PSXKM50/).

**Chapter 4.5, Types and saturated models**, describes models by the types they realize: the [Stone space of types](/VDY4PG5WHN/), [isolated types](/A0VNTN725W/), [algebraic and definable closure](/DKAGC639W9/), saturated and [homogeneous](/4NJS7W57VK/) models, and the [monster model](/EYTTQZ0E1S/).

**Chapter 4.6, Countable models and categoricity**, counts models: [categorical theories](/1JJCGNX240/) and the [Łoś–Vaught test](/ZR7Y5C235P/), [prime models](/S8W40PYNHX/), [$\omega$-categorical theories](/A22K1X8GKD/), [Vaught's never-two theorem](/E5W3RRSY5Z/) and the [Vaught conjecture](/YVBHE2SEE6/).

**Chapter 4.7, Fraïssé theory and homogeneous structures**, builds countable structures from finite pieces through the [amalgamation property](/XSCSX39GEZ/) and the [Fraïssé limit](/HJVRBDQSGG/), then studies the automorphism groups, with the [small index property](/RZAF6NVEXC/), [Ramsey classes](/8XS8FCTF8T/) and the [Kechris–Pestov–Todorcevic correspondence](/96Q4P7PZVY/).

**Chapter 4.8, Quantifier elimination and model completeness**, gives the [tests for quantifier elimination](/Q1CGE4DWXQ/) and applies them to [algebraically closed fields](/2NBBJADY5N/), [real closed fields](/FSJ9NGRRPA/), [Z-groups](/VKC9CR37FT/) and [divisible torsion-free abelian groups](/BFK4T1WK38/), with [cylindrical algebraic decomposition](/GE8FCGKPTR/) as the effective form. [Model complete theories](/YR6HCMABTE/), [Robinson's test](/QHEADWYQ64/), [existentially closed models](/6M98MCW575/) and [model companions](/68GG7QKN7C/) follow; the [theory of groups has no model companion](/5GS2AE651W/), and [Robinson forcing](/2TYY0ZP7YQ/) treats such cases.

**Chapter 4.9, Preservation and definability theorems**, matches closure properties of classes with syntactic forms of axioms: the general pattern of [preservation theorems](/T69D2NS1HP/), the Łoś–Tarski theorem, the [Chang–Łoś–Suszko theorem](/RK7NK2P75J/) for unions of chains, [Lyndon's theorem](/8DJSRWME2X/) for homomorphisms, and [universal Horn classes](/9A4CYWXPEA/). Beth's theorem, [Svenonius's theorem](/JNXY3WRYFY/) and the [Chang–Makkai theorem](/MFR9Z5PPH4/) turn implicit definitions into explicit ones, and [pseudo-elementary classes](/YEC1Q3A490/) close the chapter.

**Chapter 4.10, Interpretations and imaginaries**, builds structures inside structures. Interpretations transfer decidability and stability, [bi-interpretability](/KHZ1E7RMMQ/) is the notion of sameness, and [elimination of imaginaries](/6M2R0ER3KV/) says when classes of definable equivalence relations have codes among ordinary tuples. [Hyperimaginaries](/NQPCDVV3NB/) and the [reconstruction theorem](/AM0523XJ9D/) end it.

**Chapter 4.11, Indiscernibles and two-cardinal theorems**, proves the [existence of indiscernibles](/RG6D2FAS14/) by Ramsey's theorem and builds Ehrenfeucht–Mostowski models from them. [Vaught's two-cardinal theorem](/XT227BYP7B/) moves any gap between $\abs{M}$ and $\abs{P^M}$ down to $(\aleph_1, \aleph_0)$, [Chang's theorem](/4STP69KESQ/) moves it up under cardinal arithmetic, and the [Hanf number](/A9XBRF3A7Z/) measures how far a logic controls the sizes of models.

The remaining chapters (4.12–4.27) have no entries yet; the book prints their openings only. They treat strongly minimal sets and Morley's categoricity theorem (4.12); stable theories and forking (4.13); classification theory beyond stability (4.14); NIP theories (4.15); o-minimality (4.16); geometric stability and groups of finite Morley rank (4.17); the model theory of fields (4.18) and of valued fields (4.19); applications to Diophantine geometry (4.20); definability in number theory (4.21); models of arithmetic (4.22); nonstandard analysis (4.23); infinitary logics and Lindström's theorem (4.24); continuous logic (4.25); modules and other special theories (4.26); and finite model theory (4.27).

## How to read this volume

For a first course, read these entries in order: [theory of a structure](/X9HV9C6X4F/), [elementary equivalence](/P3B1V182N4/), [definable set](/CY1TXHF6KQ/), [properties that are not first-order](/T1S9T0RKHJ/), [elementary substructure](/8WZF9SBP39/), the Tarski–Vaught test, the Löwenheim–Skolem theorem, Łoś's theorem, the back-and-forth method, the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé theorem, [type](/9V71Q3SD1G/), the omitting types theorem, [saturated model](/RMTZ5J64FX/), the Łoś–Vaught test, the Ryll-Nardzewski theorem, [quantifier elimination](/7FDDXV3JK7/), the tests for quantifier elimination, quantifier elimination for algebraically closed fields, [model complete theory](/YR6HCMABTE/) and the Łoś–Tarski theorem. Do the ◦ and • exercises of Chapters 4.1 to 4.6 and 4.8.

After this course, three lines lead on. Pure model theory runs through Chapter 4.7, the definability theorems of Chapter 4.9, [interpretations](/7R0SJPH37J/) and elimination of imaginaries in Chapter 4.10, and indiscernibles and Ehrenfeucht–Mostowski models in Chapter 4.11, which prepare Chapters 4.12, 4.13 and 4.15. Tame geometry and algebra continue from Chapter 4.8 into Chapters 4.16, 4.18 and 4.20. Logic and arithmetic run through Chapters 4.22, 4.24 and 4.27.

Each entry stands on its own; its facts box lists what it needs, with the number of the entry that explains it.

## What you need first

You need first-order logic from Volume 2: [first-order logic](/086QKPF0A6/), [signatures](/WDVVEYD6AT/) and [formulas](/HYJ10AF6RT/) in Chapter 2.4; [satisfaction](/2RBQPV02AS/) in Chapter 2.5; and [Gödel's completeness theorem](/WQ1ZE255SF/) and the [compactness theorem](/1NR3HAWG9D/) in Chapter 2.7. You should know the definitions of a [group](/28PKCPYA2V/), a [field](/0QA8CHH8MG/) and a [vector space](/000AYPA9AJ/), as in Chapter 3.5. Several proofs use [cardinal numbers](/0EBACQFAZW/), [Zorn's lemma](/04RXTQA5ZA/) and [ultrafilters](/J64Y3JC0RE/) from set theory.

## The exercises

Each chapter with entries ends with exercises in three bands. Exercises marked ◦ test close reading, such as checking whether a map is an embedding; those marked • use an idea in a new place, such as proving that a class is not elementary; those marked ★ are harder, and some ask for a known theorem in your own words. Many have hints in the appendix *Hints to the exercises*.
