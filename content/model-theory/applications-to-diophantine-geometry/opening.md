Diophantine geometry asks which points with rational, integer or torsion coordinates lie on an algebraic variety. Its finiteness theorems, such as Faltings's theorem that a curve of genus at least $2$ over a number field has finitely many [rational points](/NCG0RCPTH1/), are proved with heights and arithmetic. Since the 1990s model theory has given proofs of several such theorems, often in new settings and with uniform bounds, and it has supplied a language for the conjectures that unify them.

The first large application was Ehud Hrushovski's proof of the Mordell–Lang conjecture for function fields, in all characteristics. In its basic form the conjecture says that a subvariety of a semiabelian variety meets a subgroup of finite rank in a finite union of cosets of subgroups. Hrushovski worked inside [differentially closed fields](/KPCN3KHG50/) in characteristic $0$ and [separably closed fields](/8B57B7EGK4/) in characteristic $p$, and he used the trichotomy for Zariski geometries to show that the relevant definable sets are either locally modular or controlled by the field of constants, which is algebraically closed. He gave a proof of the Manin–Mumford conjecture on torsion points by similar methods.

The second line runs through o-minimality. The Pila–Wilkie theorem says that a set definable in an [o-minimal](/DTWHAW1N0V/) expansion of the real field has few rational points of height at most $T$ outside its algebraic part, the union of its connected semialgebraic subsets of positive dimension: for each $\varepsilon > 0$, at most $c\,T^{\varepsilon}$ of them, where $c$ depends on the set and on $\varepsilon$. Pila and Zannier made this bound the core of a new proof of Manin–Mumford, and Pila used it to prove the André–Oort conjecture for products of modular curves. Functional transcendence results of Ax–Schanuel type, the analogues over function fields of [Schanuel's conjecture](/65PV4VFP9Y/), supply the other half of such proofs, and the Zilber–Pink conjecture states the common pattern.
