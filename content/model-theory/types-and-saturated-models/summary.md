## What to remember

- **[Types](/9V71Q3SD1G/):** an $n$-type over $A \subseteq M$ is a set $p(\bar{x})$ of $L_A$-formulas with $\operatorname{Th}_A(\mathcal{M}) \cup p$ satisfiable. It is complete when it contains each formula or its negation. By [realizing types](/8R31F7BQGY/), any family of finitely satisfiable types is realized in one elementary extension of size at most $\abs{M} + \abs{L} + \abs{I} + \aleph_0$.
- **[Stone spaces](/VDY4PG5WHN/):** $S_n(A)$ with the basic sets $[\varphi]$ is compact, Hausdorff and totally disconnected, and the sets $[\varphi]$ are its clopen sets. For dense orders $\abs{S_n(\emptyset)}$ is the number of weak orders on $n$ points: $1, 3, 13, 75, 541$. Over a model $M$, a type is isolated exactly when it is realized in $M$.
- **[Isolated types](/A0VNTN725W/) and [omitting types](/7MFGBVDVG9/):** an isolated type of a complete theory is realized in every model. In a countable language, countably many non-isolated types are omitted together in some countable model. Both countability hypotheses are needed.
- **[Closures](/DKAGC639W9/):** $\operatorname{dcl}(A) \subseteq \operatorname{acl}(A)$. In an algebraically closed field $\operatorname{acl}(A)$ is the algebraic closure of the field generated by $A$, and $\operatorname{dcl}(A)$ is its perfect closure, which is the generated field itself in characteristic $0$. In a dense order both equal $A$.
- **[Saturation](/RMTZ5J64FX/):** $\kappa$-saturated means that every type over fewer than $\kappa$ elements is realized, and saturated means $\abs{M}$-saturated. $(\Q, <)$ is saturated, while $(\R, <)$ is $\aleph_0$-saturated but omits the type of a point just above $0$ over $\Q$. Saturated models of the same theory and size are isomorphic, by the [uniqueness theorem](/DZ65A6X12W/).
- **[Existence](/GSQ833HAZT/):** every $\mathcal{M}$ has a $\kappa^+$-saturated elementary extension of size at most $(\abs{M} + 2^{\abs{L}})^\kappa$. A saturated model of size $\kappa$ exists when $\kappa$ is uncountable, $\kappa^{<\kappa} = \kappa$ and $\abs{L} < \kappa$, or when $T$ is stable in a regular $\kappa \ge \abs{L}$. A saturated dense order of size $\aleph_1$ exists exactly under CH, and none exists in singular sizes.
- **The two halves:** a countable structure is saturated exactly when it is [homogeneous](/4NJS7W57VK/) and [universal](/F30ZVR4AD5/), and a $\kappa$-saturated structure is $\kappa^+$-universal. [Special models](/CBNPB0QZGQ/) exist in every strong limit $\lambda > \abs{L}$ and are saturated when $\lambda$ is regular. Countable [recursively saturated models](/ZGEXNRHC9M/) exist for every theory in a computable language.
- **[Monster model](/EYTTQZ0E1S/):** a $\bar\kappa$-saturated, strongly $\bar\kappa$-homogeneous model in which every set of interest is small. Over a small $A$, [types are orbits](/7WH24MZZQ8/) of $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{M}/A)$, $\operatorname{dcl}(A)$ is the set of fixed points, and $\operatorname{acl}(A)$ is the union of the finite orbits.
- **[Rigidity](/EPNZKJQHWJ/):** $(\N, <)$, every well-order and the field $\R$ are rigid, while $(\Z, <)$ and $\C$ are not. No infinite saturated structure is rigid.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $(\Q, <)$ | yes | yes | yes | yes | no |
| $(\R, <)$ | yes | no | no | no | no |

## Common errors

- **Testing a type against the structure instead of its theory.** The formulas $0 < x$ and $x < 1$ form a type in $(\Q, <)$ but not in $(\Z, <)$, because $\operatorname{Th}_{\set{0, 1}}(\Z, <)$ says that nothing lies between $0$ and $1$.
- **Asking for types over the whole model.** No infinite model realizes the type $\set{x \ne a : a \in M}$ over $M$, so no infinite $\mathcal{M}$ is $\abs{M}^+$-saturated. The parameter set must be smaller than the model.
- **Dropping countability in the omitting types theorem.** With uncountably many constants a non-isolated type can be realized in every model, and uncountably many non-isolated types in a countable language need not be omitted together.
- **Reading homogeneous as rich.** $(\Z, <)$ and $\overline{\Q}$ are homogeneous and omit types over finite sets.
- **Assuming types are orbits everywhere.** In $(\R, <)$ the elements $0$ and $-1$ have the same type over the positive rationals, but an automorphism fixing every positive rational fixes $0$. In the model $\omega \times \Z$ of $\operatorname{Th}(\Z, <)$, all elements have one type over $\emptyset$, and elements of different copies of $\Z$ are in different orbits.
- **Expecting a countable saturated model in a countable language.** The condition $\kappa^{<\kappa} = \kappa$ holds for $\kappa = \aleph_0$, but a countable saturated model exists only when the theory has countably many types, as in [countable saturated models of small theories](/7GN6XF590M/).
