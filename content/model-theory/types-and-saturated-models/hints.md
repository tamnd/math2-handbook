10. A $2$-type over a finite $A$ is a weak order on $\set{x_1, x_2} \cup A$ that extends the given order of $A$. Over $\set{0}$ every weak order on three points occurs, and there are $13$. Over $\set{0, 1}$ count the weak orders on four points with $0 < 1$: by symmetry this is $(75 - 13)/2 = 31$.

14. After quantifier elimination a formula over $A$ is a Boolean combination of conditions $n x < a$ and $n x = a$ with $n \in \Z$ and $a$ in the group generated by $A$. A finite definable set therefore consists of points $a/n$, so $\operatorname{acl}(A) = \operatorname{dcl}(A)$ is the $\Q$-linear span of $A$. The answers are $\set{0}$, $\Q$, $\Q + \Q\sqrt{2}$ and no.

17. Take a countable elementary substructure $D \cong \Q$. For each of the $2^{\aleph_0}$ cuts $(X, Y)$ of $D$ in which $X$ has no greatest and $Y$ no least element, the type $\set{a < x : a \in X} \cup \set{x < b : b \in Y}$ is a finitely satisfiable type over a countable set. Different cuts are separated by an element of $D$, so their realizations are different.

19. An automorphism permutes the copies of $\Z$ in an order-preserving way, and $\omega$ is rigid, so it maps each copy onto itself by a translation. The orbits are the copies $\set{n} \times \Z$. The map $(1, 0) \mapsto (0, 0)$ is partial elementary, but no extension can send $(0, 0)$ anywhere, since its image would have to lie in a copy before the first.

21. In an $\aleph_1$-saturated extension the order $L$ of the copies of $\Z$ has an element between any countable $X < Y$, with $X$ or $Y$ allowed to be empty: realize $\set{x > a + n, \, x < b - n}$ over representatives, using $x > n$ for $n \in \N$ when $X$ is empty. Such an $L$ contains a copy of $\Q$ and fills each of its cuts, as in Exercise 17. Part 1 of the existence theorem with $\kappa = \aleph_0$ gives an extension of size $(\aleph_0 + 2^{\aleph_0})^{\aleph_0} = 2^{\aleph_0}$.

23. For (iii) to (i), a type over a finite $A$ either places $x$ at a finite distance from an element of $A$, or places it infinitely far from $A$ in one gap between the copies that $A$ meets, and density of $\Q$ fills each gap. (i) to (ii) is immediate. For (ii) to (iii), realize the computable types $\set{\delta_n(a, x) \wedge a < x : n \in \N}$, the same type below $a$, and $\set{\delta_n(a, x) \wedge \delta_n(x, b) \wedge a < x < b : n \in \N}$ for $a$ and $b$ in different copies; they show that $L$ is dense without endpoints, and Cantor's theorem gives $L \cong \Q$.

24. In an infinite $\mathcal{M}$ the set of formulas $\neg\varphi(x)$, for $\varphi$ an $L$-formula with finitely many solutions, is finitely satisfiable, so some $a$ has a non-algebraic type over $\emptyset$. Then $\operatorname{tp}(a) \cup \set{x \ne a}$ is realized by some $b \ne a$, and strong homogeneity gives an automorphism with $a \mapsto b$. Saturated structures are strongly homogeneous. For the example take $(\N, <)$, where $n$ is the element with exactly $n$ predecessors.

25. If $b \notin \operatorname{dcl}(A)$, compactness makes $\operatorname{tp}(b/A) \cup \set{x \ne b}$ consistent, since otherwise one formula of $\operatorname{tp}(b/A)$ would define $b$; a realization $c$ has the type of $b$ over $A$, and [types are orbits](/7WH24MZZQ8/) gives $\sigma$ with $\sigma(b) = c$. If $b \notin \operatorname{acl}(A)$, the type $\operatorname{tp}(b/A) \cup \set{x \ne c : c \in C}$ is consistent for each small $C$, so $b$ has infinitely many conjugates over $A$. The converse directions hold because automorphisms over $A$ preserve each formula over $A$.
