In the order $(\Z, <)$ name the elements $0$ and $3$. The formulas $0 < x$ and $x < 3$ describe a possible element, and $1$ and $2$ realize it. Now take instead the formula $3 < x$ together with the formulas "at least $n$ elements lie between $3$ and $x$", one for each $n$. Every finite part of this list is satisfied by a large integer, so the list is consistent with everything that $(\Z, <)$ says about $0$ and $3$, yet no integer satisfies all of it. By [compactness](/1NR3HAWG9D/) an [elementary extension](/CGH8C8EA9N/) of $(\Z, <)$ has such an element, infinitely far above $3$. In $(\Q, <)$ the same game never fails: every consistent list of formulas about finitely many rationals asks for one of the named points or for a point in an open interval, and the interval has one.

The second list is a [type](/9V71Q3SD1G/), $(\Z, <)$ omits it, and $(\Q, <)$ is [saturated](/RMTZ5J64FX/) because it omits no type over a finite set. The chapter studies types as the first-order descriptions of elements, the spaces they form, and the models that realize as many of them as possible.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in six groups, read in order.

1. **Types and type spaces.** A [type](/9V71Q3SD1G/) over $A$ is a set of formulas consistent with $\operatorname{Th}_A(\mathcal{M})$. The complete types form the [Stone space of types](/VDY4PG5WHN/) $S_n(A)$, a compact totally disconnected space. [Realizing types](/8R31F7BQGY/) shows that any family of finitely satisfiable types is realized in one elementary extension of controlled size.
2. **Isolated and omitted types.** An [isolated type](/A0VNTN725W/) is implied by one formula and is realized in every model. The [omitting types theorem](/7MFGBVDVG9/) builds, in a countable language, a countable model that omits countably many non-isolated types.
3. **Algebraic and definable closure.** [Algebraic and definable closure](/DKAGC639W9/) collect the elements that a formula over $A$ pins down to a finite set or to one point.
4. **Saturated models.** A [saturated model](/RMTZ5J64FX/) realizes every type over a smaller set of its elements. The [existence of saturated models](/GSQ833HAZT/) needs $\kappa^{<\kappa} = \kappa$ or stability, and for dense orders it is tied to the continuum hypothesis.
5. **Homogeneity, universality and weaker saturation.** A [homogeneous model](/4NJS7W57VK/) extends partial elementary maps, and a [universal model](/F30ZVR4AD5/) contains every smaller model of its theory. [Special models](/CBNPB0QZGQ/) and [recursively saturated models](/ZGEXNRHC9M/) replace saturated models when these may not exist.
6. **The monster model and automorphisms.** The [monster model](/EYTTQZ0E1S/) is the fixed large universe of later chapters. A [rigid structure](/EPNZKJQHWJ/) has no automorphism except the identity, and [types as orbits](/7WH24MZZQ8/) says when automorphisms see exactly what types see.

## A first reading

A short course takes these entries in order.

- [Type](/9V71Q3SD1G/), with its examples in $(\Q, <)$ and $(\Z, <)$.
- [Stone space of types](/VDY4PG5WHN/), for the topology and the count of types of a dense order.
- [Realizing types](/8R31F7BQGY/), the compactness argument behind every later construction.
- [Isolated type](/A0VNTN725W/) and the statement of the [omitting types theorem](/7MFGBVDVG9/).
- [Algebraic and definable closure](/DKAGC639W9/), with the examples in algebraically closed fields.
- [Saturated model](/RMTZ5J64FX/), and its example that $(\Q, <)$ is saturated.
- [Homogeneous model](/4NJS7W57VK/) and [universal model](/F30ZVR4AD5/), the two halves of saturation.
- [Monster model](/EYTTQZ0E1S/) and [types as orbits](/7WH24MZZQ8/), the setting of stability theory.

At first the proofs of the omitting types theorem can wait, and parts 2 to 5 of the existence theorem can be read for their statements only; they need cardinal arithmetic and, in part 3, [stable theories](/T7JW5QY6KR/). [Special models](/CBNPB0QZGQ/) and [recursively saturated models](/ZGEXNRHC9M/) are needed only where saturated models are not available, and [rigid structures](/EPNZKJQHWJ/) are a short side topic. The [uniqueness of saturated models](/DZ65A6X12W/) is used throughout and is worth reading alongside the existence theorem.

## Before you start

The chapter assumes the following.

- [Completeness, compactness and interpolation](/FBWW4J1XWG/), for the [compactness theorem](/1NR3HAWG9D/) and [Lindenbaum's lemma](/SC0BSJ3GT6/).
- [Elementary extensions and the Löwenheim–Skolem theorems](/2RE7GWWXM5/), for [elementary embeddings](/CGH8C8EA9N/), the [elementary chain theorem](/NHCRZ74JHH/) and [partial elementary maps](/XTNYY0WRQ5/).
- The [back-and-forth method](/6F2DB4HTJR/) of [Chapter 4.4](/N64R1MGDBN/), which drives the uniqueness and homogeneity proofs.
- [Boolean algebras in logic](/KG7730QZMQ/) and [Stone spaces](/ZV38HA5PAQ/), for the topology on $S_n(A)$.
- [Cardinal arithmetic](/SEMEEYYWT0/) and [regular cardinals](/13ZV2F8JAR/), for the existence of saturated and special models.
- Some [quantifier elimination](/7FDDXV3JK7/), since the examples compute types in dense orders and algebraically closed fields through it.
