7. In a model the orbits $\set{x, f(x)}$ have exactly two elements and partition the domain, so a finite model has even size. An infinite set $X$ has the same size as $X \times \set{0, 1}$, which carries the involution $(x, i) \mapsto (x, 1 - i)$. If the finite sets of even size were a $\mathrm{PC}_\Delta$ class, it would be closed under ultraproducts, but an ultraproduct of sets of sizes $2, 4, 6, \dots$ by a nonprincipal ultrafilter is infinite.

9. Let $\sigma$ be $\forall x \forall y \forall z \forall w \, \neg(E(x, y) \land E(x, z) \land E(x, w) \land y \ne z \land y \ne w \land z \ne w)$. A vertex with three neighbours spans, with them, a graph on four vertices in which it is adjacent to the other three, and the three others carry $0$, $1$, $2$ or $3$ edges. So the minimal forbidden graphs are the claw $K_{1, 3}$, the paw, the diamond $K_4$ minus an edge, and $K_4$; every graph on three vertices is allowed.

10. Forbid each cycle $C_n$, $n \ge 3$, by a universal sentence with distinct variables $x_1, \dots, x_n$. A finite set of these axioms forbids only cycles up to some length $N$, and $C_{N + 1}$ satisfies it, so by compactness no finite set of sentences works. For the last part, the direct product of the path $a - b - c$ with itself contains the $4$-cycle $(a, b), (b, a), (c, b), (b, c)$.

11. If the edges of $F$ hold among $x_1, \dots, x_n$ with repetitions allowed, the map $i \mapsto x_i$ is a homomorphism from $F$ to $G$, and a $k$-colouring of $G$ would pull back to one of $F$. Each $\forall \bar x \, \neg \bigwedge_{ij \in E(F)} E(x_i, x_j)$ is a universal Horn sentence with no unnegated atom. For a product, colour each tuple by the colour of its coordinate in one fixed factor.

13. Let $x * y = 2 x y$. The map $x \mapsto 2 x$ preserves $+$ and $0$ and carries $*$ to $\cdot$, so $(\R, +, 0, *)$ is a model of $T$ with the same reduct as $(\R, +, 0, \cdot)$. The graphs of $\cdot$ and $*$ differ, since $1 * 1 = 2$.

15. For $P_1$ the images are the intervals $\set{q : a < q < b}$ with $a < b$ rational: there are $\aleph_0$ of them, and $P_1$ is definable with the parameters $0$ and $1$. For $P_2$ the images are the sets $\set{q : a < q < s}$ with $a$ rational and $s > a$ irrational, since an automorphism of $(\Q, <)$ can send the cut at $\pi$ to any irrational cut: there are $2^{\aleph_0}$, so $P_2$ is not definable with parameters.

18. Closure under subgroups and isomorphisms is clear. An ultraproduct of bi-ordered groups $(G_i, <_i)$ is a bi-ordered group by [Łoś's theorem](/AGYBSTD7BG/), and its reduct is the ultraproduct of the $G_i$. Then apply the [characterization of universal classes](/JPGDSW2TAP/).

19. Suppose the connected graphs are the reducts of the models of $T^+$. Add constants $c, d$ and, for each $n$, the sentence saying that no path of length at most $n$ joins $c$ and $d$. Paths of length $m$, expanded to models of $T^+$ with $c$ and $d$ at the ends, show that every finite part is satisfiable. A model of the whole set has a connected reduct in which $c$ and $d$ are not joined.

20. A homomorphism maps an odd closed walk to an odd closed walk, and a graph with an odd closed walk has an odd cycle. The odd cycles $C_{2n + 1}$ are not bipartite, but by Łoś's theorem their ultraproduct by a nonprincipal ultrafilter has no cycle of any odd length $k$, since $C_{2n+1}$ has none once $2n + 1 > k$. Lyndon's theorem is about elementary classes, and this class is not one.

21. Let $\Psi$ be the set of existential positive consequences of $\varphi$, and let $B \models \Psi$. The set $\set{\varphi} \cup \set{\neg \psi : \psi \text{ existential positive}, \ B \not\models \psi}$ is consistent, since otherwise a finite disjunction of such $\psi$ would lie in $\Psi$. A model $A$ of it satisfies only existential positive sentences true in $B$, so the positive diagram of $A$ is consistent with the elementary diagram of $B$, which gives a homomorphism from $A$ into an elementary extension $B^*$ of $B$. Then $B^* \models \varphi$, so $B \models \varphi$, and compactness gives one sentence of $\Psi$ that implies $\varphi$.
