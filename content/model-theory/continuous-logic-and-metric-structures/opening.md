Many structures of analysis are complete metric spaces with extra operations: [Banach spaces](/06GZ956657/), [Hilbert spaces](/HM2HHE3J51/), [C*-algebras](/M2WFY1D3SZ/), and the measure algebra of a probability space. Classical first-order logic handles them badly. Completeness is not first-order, and in the [ultraproduct](/AX9Q86GB8M/) of a family of normed spaces in the sense of Chapter 4.3, with norms in an ultrapower of $\R$, some elements have infinite norm and some nonzero elements have infinitesimal norm. Analysts had their own construction, the Banach space ultraproduct, which keeps the bounded sequences and identifies two of them when their distance tends to $0$ along the ultrafilter.

Continuous logic is the logic that fits this construction. A metric structure is a complete metric space of bounded diameter with uniformly continuous functions and real-valued predicates; a Banach space enters through its unit ball. A formula takes a value in $[0, 1]$ instead of a truth value, the connectives are continuous functions on $[0, 1]$, and $\sup$ and $\inf$ take the place of $\forall$ and $\exists$. The value $0$ plays the part of truth. With these choices [Łoś's theorem](/AGYBSTD7BG/) holds for metric ultraproducts, and with it the compactness theorem, a downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem with density character in place of cardinality, and much of stability theory. The present form of the logic is due to Ben Yaacov, Berenstein, Henson and Usvyatsov, after the continuous model theory of Chang and Keisler and Henson's work on Banach spaces.

The theory of infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces has quantifier elimination and is categorical in every infinite density character, as the theory of infinite-dimensional vector spaces over $\Q$ is categorical in uncountable cardinalities. The theory of atomless probability algebras also has quantifier elimination, and up to isomorphism its only separable model is the measure algebra of the unit interval. The model theory of operator algebras, developed by Farah, Hart and Sherman and others, studies ultrapowers of C*-algebras and von Neumann algebras with these tools.
