## History

The idea of taking sequences modulo a notion of "almost everywhere" is older than the general theorem. Thoralf Skolem built a nonstandard model of arithmetic in 1934 from definable sequences of natural numbers, by a construction close to an ultrapower. Edwin Hewitt used ultrapowers of the real field in 1948, in "Rings of real-valued continuous functions", and gave the name "hyper-real" to fields of this kind. Alfred Horn studied in 1951 the sentences that remain true in direct products, and the [Horn sentences](/8RRECJJM3G/) are named after that paper.

Jerzy Łoś defined ultraproducts of arbitrary structures and proved [his theorem](/AGYBSTD7BG/) in 1955. The construction spread quickly in the early 1960s. Frayne, Morel and Scott published "Reduced direct products" in *Fundamenta Mathematicae* in 1962, a systematic study of the construction. H. Jerome Keisler, in "Ultraproducts and elementary classes" (1961), characterized the elementary classes by ultraproducts and proved, under the generalized continuum hypothesis, that elementarily equivalent structures have isomorphic ultrapowers. Saharon Shelah removed the hypothesis in "Every two elementarily equivalent models have isomorphic ultrapowers", in the *Israel Journal of Mathematics* in 1971.

For products, Andrzej Mostowski treated direct powers in 1952. Solomon Feferman and Robert Vaught extended his method to products and generalized products in "The first order properties of products of algebraic systems", in *Fundamenta Mathematicae* in 1959. The decidability of [Skolem arithmetic](/X86G2FXTPC/), the theory of the positive integers under multiplication, follows from these results.

Abraham Robinson's [nonstandard analysis](/WM62GKCBWN/) of the 1960s gave ultrapowers of $\R$ a wide audience. James Ax used ultraproducts of finite fields in "The elementary theory of finite fields" (1968), where pseudofinite fields appear. Keisler introduced good ultrafilters to study saturation, and in 1967 he defined the order on theories now called [Keisler's order](/TD5D2G5PYP/). Kenneth Kunen proved in 1972 without extra hypotheses that good countably incomplete ultrafilters exist on every infinite cardinal. Maryanthe Malliaris and Shelah later settled many questions about Keisler's order, and their work linking it to the cardinals $\mathfrak{p}$ and $\mathfrak{t}$ appeared in 2016.

## Further reading

- C. C. Chang and H. J. Keisler, *Model Theory*, third edition (1990). Chapters 4 and 6 are the standard reference for reduced products, ultraproducts, good ultrafilters and the Keisler-Shelah theorem.
- J. L. Bell and A. B. Slomson, *Models and Ultraproducts: An Introduction* (1969). A whole book built around ultraproducts, from Boolean algebras to the characterization of elementary classes.
- Wilfrid Hodges, *Model Theory* (1993). Covers reduced products, the Feferman-Vaught theorem and products in more generality than most texts.
- David Marker, *Model Theory: An Introduction* (2002). A short, clear treatment of ultraproducts and Łoś's theorem within a first course.
- Robert Goldblatt, *Lectures on the Hyperreals: An Introduction to Nonstandard Analysis* (1998). Builds the hyperreals as an ultrapower and develops calculus on them.
- W. W. Comfort and S. Negrepontis, *The Theory of Ultrafilters* (1974). The set theory of ultrafilters, including good and regular ultrafilters.
