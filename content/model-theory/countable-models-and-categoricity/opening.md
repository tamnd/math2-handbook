By the [Löwenheim–Skolem theorem](/484M0TZ1S7/), a theory in a countable language with an infinite model has models of every infinite size, so it never fixes its infinite models up to isomorphism. The best it can do is to have one model in a given cardinality $\kappa$; such a theory is $\kappa$-categorical. The theory of dense linear orders without endpoints is $\aleph_0$-categorical: by Cantor's [back-and-forth method](/6F2DB4HTJR/), each of its countable models is isomorphic to $(\Q, <)$. The theory of algebraically closed fields of characteristic $0$ is categorical in every uncountable cardinal, since such a field is determined by its transcendence degree, but it has $\aleph_0$ countable models, one for each degree $0, 1, 2, \dots, \aleph_0$. The [Łoś–Vaught test](/ZR7Y5C235P/) turns categoricity into a proof method: a theory with no finite models that is categorical in some infinite $\kappa \ge \abs{L}$ is [complete](/655K8Y7VS8/).

For a complete theory $T$ in a countable language, the countable models are governed by the types of Chapter 4.5. The [Ryll-Nardzewski theorem](/1513J40TKA/) says that $T$ is $\aleph_0$-categorical exactly when it has only finitely many complete $n$-types for each $n$. A [prime model](/FJ79HREXG4/) embeds elementarily into every model of $T$, and in a countable language the prime models are the countable atomic ones, in which every tuple realizes an isolated type; the field of algebraic numbers is the prime model of the theory above. Vaught proved that the number of countable models is never exactly $2$, while Ehrenfeucht's theory of $(\Q, <)$ with constants $c_0 < c_1 < c_2 < \cdots$ has exactly $3$. [Vaught's conjecture](/YVBHE2SEE6/), that the number is always finite, $\aleph_0$ or $2^{\aleph_0}$, is still open.
