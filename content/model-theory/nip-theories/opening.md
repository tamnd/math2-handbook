A formula $\varphi(x, y)$ has the independence property in a theory $T$ when some model contains elements $a_0, a_1, a_2, \dots$ that it can cut in every possible way: for each set $S \subseteq \N$ there is a parameter $b_S$ with $\varphi(a_i, b_S)$ true exactly when $i \in S$. A theory is [NIP](/74VCDYQDGP/) when no formula has this property. The edge relation of the [Rado graph](/RMXR0FEF54/) has the independence property, by the extension property and compactness. In arithmetic, divisibility has it: take the $a_i$ to be the primes and $b_S$ the product of the primes indexed by a finite $S$, then apply compactness. Pseudofinite fields have it too. On the other side, every [stable theory](/T7JW5QY6KR/) is NIP, and so is every [o-minimal theory](/DTWHAW1N0V/), every theory of ordered abelian groups, [Presburger arithmetic](/4W0E9G7PPV/), algebraically closed valued fields and the $p$-adic numbers.

Shelah introduced the property in 1971 while counting types. Laskowski observed in 1992 that it is a notion of combinatorics: a formula is NIP exactly when the family of sets it defines, as the parameter varies, has finite [VC dimension](/KCFXR9K3GD/). By the [Sauer–Shelah lemma](/Y4FQT5WPQJ/), such a family cuts an $n$-element set into only polynomially many pieces, and in learning theory the same condition characterizes the classes that can be learned from samples. These facts give NIP theories a measure theory. Keisler measures, finitely additive probability measures on definable sets, behave well in NIP theories, and Hrushovski, Peterzil and Pillay used them in 2008 to prove Pillay's conjecture on definably compact groups in o-minimal expansions of fields. For fields, Shelah conjectured that an infinite NIP field is separably closed, real closed or carries a non-trivial henselian valuation. The conjecture is still open in general.
