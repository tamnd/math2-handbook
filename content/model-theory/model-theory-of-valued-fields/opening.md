A valued field is a field $K$ with a valuation $v \colon K^\times \to \Gamma$ onto an ordered abelian group, such as the $p$-adic valuation on $\Q$, which gives a fraction the exponent of $p$ in its factorization. The elements of non-negative value form a [valuation ring](/QSX12D8RQM/), and with it come two smaller structures: the residue field and the value group $\Gamma$. For the [$p$-adic numbers](/BT6T702C28/) $\Q_p$ these are $\F_p$ and $\Z$, and for the field $\F_p((t))$ of formal Laurent series they are the same. A field is [Henselian](/3ACEE4N6NE/) when [Hensel's lemma](/010QVB71T2/) holds: a simple root of a polynomial modulo the maximal ideal lifts to a true root.

The central result is the Ax–Kochen–Ershov principle, found by Ax and Kochen and, independently, by Ershov. For Henselian valued fields of residue characteristic $0$, the theory of the field is fixed by the theories of its residue field and its value group. Take ultraproducts of the fields $\Q_p$ and of the fields $\F_p((t))$ over the same nonprincipal ultrafilter on the primes. By [Łoś's theorem](/AGYBSTD7BG/) both are Henselian with residue characteristic $0$, and they have the same residue field and value group, so they are elementarily equivalent. So a first-order statement true in $\F_p((t))$ for all but finitely many $p$ is true in $\Q_p$ for all but finitely many $p$. This gives the [Ax–Kochen theorem](/PHT3TJCS90/): for each $d$, a form of degree $d$ in more than $d^2$ variables has a nontrivial zero in $\Q_p$ for all but finitely many primes $p$. Terjanian's form of degree $4$ in $18$ variables with no nontrivial $2$-adic zero shows that the exceptions cannot be removed.

Abraham Robinson proved that the theory of algebraically closed valued fields is model complete, and Angus Macintyre proved quantifier elimination for $\Q_p$ in the language of rings with a predicate for the $n$-th powers for each $n$. Jan Denef used Macintyre's theorem to prove that certain $p$-adic Poincaré series are rational, and this use of definable sets to compute $p$-adic integrals grew into motivic integration.
