In a [strongly minimal](/PNGY60EBDJ/) structure every definable subset of the universe is finite or cofinite. Model-theoretic algebraic closure then has the exchange property, the same law that the [Steinitz exchange lemma](/08ZDRN6H41/) gives for linear span, so every strongly minimal set carries a [dimension](/03GSZ8RTZJ/) and a combinatorial geometry. Three examples show the range. In a pure infinite set the closure of a set is the set itself, and the geometry is trivial. In a vector space over $\Q$, seen as an abelian group, the closure of a set is its linear span, and the geometry is that of linear dependence. In an [algebraically closed field](/0PF6TM8TWT/) the closure is field-theoretic algebraic closure, and the dimension is transcendence degree.

Boris Zilber conjectured that these are the only kinds: the geometry of a strongly minimal set is trivial, or locally modular like that of a vector space, or the set interprets an algebraically closed field. Ehud Hrushovski refuted the conjecture with a new [construction](/B3XW32T6EH/) of strongly minimal sets that are not locally modular and interpret no infinite field. Hrushovski and Zilber then proved the trichotomy for Zariski geometries, structures with a Noetherian topology that behaves like the Zariski topology on a variety. This theorem carries geometric stability into algebraic geometry, and Hrushovski used it in his proof of the Mordell–Lang conjecture for function fields.

Groups appear through the geometry. Hrushovski's group configuration theorem recovers a definable group from a small configuration of points and dependences, and it is the main tool for finding groups and fields inside a structure. Groups of finite [Morley rank](/WN43DRPNBX/) behave much like algebraic groups. The Cherlin–Zilber conjecture says that every infinite simple group of finite Morley rank is an algebraic group over an algebraically closed field; it is open in general, and Altınel, Borovik and Cherlin proved it for groups of even type.
