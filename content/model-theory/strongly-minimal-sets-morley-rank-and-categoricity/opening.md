An infinite [definable set](/CY1TXHF6KQ/) is minimal when each of its definable subsets, with parameters, is finite or cofinite, and strongly minimal when this stays true in every [elementary extension](/CGH8C8EA9N/). A theory is [strongly minimal](/PNGY60EBDJ/) when the whole universe is. Examples are an infinite set with no structure, an infinite vector space over a fixed field, an algebraically closed field, and the integers with the successor function. The order $(\Q, <)$ is not an example, since $x < 0$ is infinite and coinfinite. In a strongly minimal set the algebraic closure satisfies the exchange law, so it is a [matroid](/PX31ZNYS3Z/), and every model has a basis and a [dimension](/03GSZ8RTZJ/). In a vector space this is linear span and linear dimension; in an algebraically closed field it is field-theoretic algebraic closure and transcendence degree. A model of a strongly minimal theory in a countable language is determined by its dimension, so the theory is categorical in every uncountable cardinal.

[Morley rank](/WN43DRPNBX/) extends this dimension to all definable sets. Finite sets have rank $0$, and a set has rank at least $\alpha + 1$ when it contains infinitely many disjoint definable subsets of rank at least $\alpha$. In an algebraically closed field the Morley rank of a constructible set is its dimension as a variety. A theory in which every formula has ordinal Morley rank is totally transcendental, and for a countable language this is the same as being $\omega$-stable. In 1965 Morley proved that a countable theory categorical in one uncountable cardinal is categorical in all of them, and such theories are $\omega$-stable. Baldwin and Lachlan showed in 1971 that every model of such a theory is prime over a strongly minimal set and determined by its dimension, so the theory has either $1$ or $\aleph_0$ countable models. The theory of algebraically closed fields of characteristic $0$ has $\aleph_0$; an infinite vector space over a finite field has $1$.
