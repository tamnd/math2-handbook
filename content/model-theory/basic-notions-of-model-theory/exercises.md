1. ◦ Let $L = \set{+, 0}$ and $\mathcal{Z} = (\Z, +, 0)$. Which of these sets are domains of [substructures](/676H7EGTKJ/) of $\mathcal{Z}$: (a) $\N$; (b) $\set{0}$; (c) $3\Z$; (d) $\set{-1, 0, 1}$; (e) the odd integers? Which answers change in the signature $\set{+, -, 0}$, with $-$ read as negation?

2. ◦ Find the substructure generated by the given set: (a) $\set{12, 18}$ in $(\Z, +, -, 0)$; (b) $\set{\tfrac{1}{2}}$ in the field $(\Q, +, -, \cdot, 0, 1)$; (c) $\emptyset$ in $(\Q, +, -, \cdot, 0, 1)$; (d) $\emptyset$ in $(\Q, +, -, 0)$.

3. ◦ Let $\mathcal{A} = (\set{0, 1, 2}, <)$. Which of these maps are [embeddings](/SCRB74SWQN/) of $\mathcal{A}$ into $(\Z, <)$: (a) $n \mapsto 5n$; (b) $n \mapsto n^2$; (c) $n \mapsto -n$; (d) $n \mapsto 0$? For each map that fails, name the condition it breaks.

4. ◦ Let $\mathcal{R} = (\R, +, \cdot, 0, 1, <)$. Which of these structures are [reducts](/EY20BF96SJ/) of $\mathcal{R}$: (a) $(\R, <)$; (b) $(\R, +, 0)$; (c) $(\Q, +, \cdot, 0, 1, <)$; (d) $(\R, +, \cdot, 0, 1, <, \sqrt{2})$, with a new constant for $\sqrt 2$; (e) $(\R, \cdot, 1)$? For each one that is not a reduct, say what it is instead.

5. ◦ For each pair, give a sentence true in one structure and false in the other, so that the pair is not [elementarily equivalent](/P3B1V182N4/): (a) $(\N, <)$ and $(\Z, <)$; (b) $(\Z, +)$ and $(\Q, +)$; (c) the fields $\Q$ and $\R$; (d) the fields $\R$ and $\C$. Use the signature $\set{+, -, \cdot, 0, 1}$ in (c) and (d).

6. ◦ Which of these theories are [complete](/655K8Y7VS8/)? (a) $\operatorname{Th}(\set{0, 1, 2}, <)$; (b) the theory of linear orders; (c) the theory in the empty signature axiomatized by one sentence saying "there are exactly three elements"; (d) the theory in the empty signature with no axioms.

7. ◦ (a) Show that the only subsets of $\Z$ that are [definable](/CY1TXHF6KQ/) without parameters in $(\Z, <)$ are $\emptyset$ and $\Z$. (b) Write formulas with the parameter $0$ that define $\set{0}$, $\N$ and $\set{1}$.

8. • Let $L = \set{\cdot, {}^{-1}, e}$. (a) Write one sentence whose models are the groups in which $x^6 = e$ for every $x$. (b) Show that the class of groups of finite exponent, the groups $G$ with some $n \ge 1$ such that $g^n = e$ for all $g \in G$, is not an [elementary class](/MEC3ZMC4F1/).

9. • Let $T$ be the theory, in the signature $\set{+, -, 0}$, of nontrivial torsion-free divisible abelian groups. (a) Write a set of axioms for $T$. (b) Show that $T$ is not [finitely axiomatizable](/JR6XPJ7X56/). Use the [criterion for finite axiomatizability](/9XKWFMVKJD/) and, for each $N$, the group of rationals whose denominators have no prime factor larger than $N$.

10. • In the field $(\R, +, -, \cdot, 0, 1)$, write a formula without parameters that defines the graph $\set{(x, y) : y = \abs{x}}$, and one that defines the interval $[0, 1]$. Check each formula on two values.

11. • Show that the subsets of $\Q$ definable without parameters in the group $(\Q, +)$ are exactly $\emptyset$, $\set{0}$, $\Q \setminus \set{0}$ and $\Q$.

12. • Work in $(\N, \cdot)$, with multiplication as the only symbol. (a) Write formulas without parameters that define $0$, $1$, the divisibility relation $x \mid y$ and the set of primes. (b) Show that the order $x < y$ is not definable without parameters. Use a bijection of $\N$ that exchanges the primes $2$ and $3$.

13. • Let $L = \set{E}$ be the signature of graphs. Show that the class of graphs in which every vertex has at most two neighbours is a basic elementary class, and that the class of graphs in which every vertex has finitely many neighbours is not elementary.

14. • Let $T$ be the theory of nontrivial torsion-free divisible abelian groups in $\set{+, -, 0}$; its models are the vector spaces over $\Q$ of positive dimension. Use the [Łoś–Vaught test](/ZR7Y5C235P/) at $\aleph_1$ to show that $T$ is complete. Conclude that $(\Q, +) \equiv (\R, +)$.

15. • (a) Show that for every class $K$ of $L$-structures, $\operatorname{Mod}(\operatorname{Th}(K))$ is the smallest elementary class that contains $K$. (b) Use the [compactness theorem](/1NR3HAWG9D/) to show that $\operatorname{Mod}(\operatorname{Th}(\N, <))$ contains an uncountable structure, so that it is larger than the class of structures isomorphic to $(\N, <)$.

16. ★ Let $L = \set{s}$ with $s$ a unary function symbol, and let $T$ say that $s$ is a bijection and that $s^n(x) \ne x$ for every $x$ and every $n \ge 1$, one axiom for each $n$. (a) Show that the models of $T$ are, up to isomorphism, the disjoint unions of copies of $(\Z, x \mapsto x + 1)$. (b) Show that $T$ is complete, so that $T$ axiomatizes $\operatorname{Th}(\Z, x \mapsto x + 1)$. (c) Show that $T$ is not finitely axiomatizable.

17. ★ Show that the class of bipartite graphs is elementary but not basic elementary. For the second part, compare a hypothetical defining sentence with the odd cycles $C_{2m+1}$.

18. ★ (a) Show that the only automorphism of the field $(\R, +, -, \cdot, 0, 1)$ is the identity. Use the [formula that defines the order](/TCJZNK8PT2/). (b) Conclude that every subset of $\R$ is fixed by every automorphism, and show by counting that some subset of $\R$ is not definable, even with parameters.

19. ★ Let $L$ be a finite signature and let $\mathcal{M}$ be a finite $L$-structure. Show that every model of $\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{M})$ is isomorphic to $\mathcal{M}$. Deduce that a complete theory in a finite signature with one finite model has, up to isomorphism, no other model.
