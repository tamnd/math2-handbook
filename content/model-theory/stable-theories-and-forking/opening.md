Stability measures a complete theory by counting its [types](/9V71Q3SD1G/). A theory $T$ in a countable language is $\lambda$-stable when over every set of at most $\lambda$ parameters there are at most $\lambda$ complete types, and [stable](/T7JW5QY6KR/) when it is $\lambda$-stable for some infinite $\lambda$. Algebraically closed fields are $\omega$-stable: a $1$-type over a field $k$ is fixed by its minimal polynomial over $k$ or is the unique transcendental type, so there are only $\abs{k} + \aleph_0$ of them. Dense linear orders are unstable, because over $\Q$ every Dedekind cut gives a type, and there are $2^{\aleph_0}$ cuts. Shelah showed that instability always has this source. $T$ is unstable exactly when some formula $\varphi(x, y)$ has the order property: there are $a_i$ and $b_j$, for $i, j \in \N$, with $\varphi(a_i, b_j)$ true exactly when $i < j$.

Stable theories have a theory of independence. Every type over a model is definable: for each formula $\varphi(x, y)$, the parameters $b$ with $\varphi(x, b)$ in the type form a definable set. Shelah defined forking: a formula forks over $A$ when it implies a finite disjunction of formulas that divide over $A$, and $\varphi(x, b)$ divides over $A$ when some sequence of copies of $b$, indiscernible over $A$, makes the instances of $\varphi$ inconsistent beyond a fixed finite number at a time. In a stable theory, non-forking gives an independence relation that is symmetric and transitive and has the extension property, and each type over a model has a unique non-forking extension to any larger set. In a vector space, $a$ is independent from $b$ over $C$ when the spans of $C \cup \set{a}$ and $C \cup \set{b}$ meet only in the span of $C$. In an algebraically closed field, it means that adding $b$ does not lower the transcendence degree of $a$ over $C$.
