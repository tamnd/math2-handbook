[Finite model theory](/4XRVJE0KAN/) studies first-order logic and its extensions on finite structures only: finite graphs, finite orders, and the databases and inputs of computer science. Much of the classical theory fails there. The compactness theorem fails, and [Trakhtenbrot's theorem](/XP6FW2WDWT/) in Chapter 2.9 shows that the sentences true in all finite structures do not form a computably enumerable set, so the completeness theorem has no finite analogue. Several preservation theorems fail too, though Rossman proved that the homomorphism preservation theorem survives.

The tools that replace compactness are combinatorial. [Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé games](/2RE7YAZK8R/) remain valid, and they show that [even cardinality](/N3AC6Y96RZ/), connectivity and [Hamiltonicity](/W0JEQM6ZTH/) are not first-order properties of finite structures; on [finite linear orders](/ZZPXVVZARC/), two orders with at least $2^n - 1$ elements satisfy the same sentences of [quantifier rank](/FVEVRE5XNZ/) $n$. Locality theorems of Hanf and Gaifman say that a first-order formula sees only a bounded neighbourhood of its arguments. Zero-one laws describe almost all finite structures. Glebskii, Kogan, Liogon'kii and Talanov, and independently Fagin, proved that for each first-order sentence about graphs, the fraction of graphs on $\set{1, \dots, n}$ that satisfy it tends to $0$ or to $1$; the limit is $1$ exactly when the sentence holds in the countable [random graph](/RMXR0FEF54/).

Descriptive complexity matches logics with complexity classes. [Fagin's theorem](/BG8Q0BYSQ3/) says that a property of finite structures is in $\mathrm{NP}$ exactly when it is definable in existential second-order logic, as [three-colourability](/EHH5XEB6BG/) is. Immerman and Vardi proved that on ordered finite structures, first-order logic with a least fixed-point operator defines exactly the properties in $\mathrm{P}$, and Immerman showed that a transitive closure operator in its place gives $\mathrm{NL}$. So the question of [P versus NP](/MFN5GYZH5Z/) becomes a question about the expressive power of two logics. Whether some logic captures $\mathrm{P}$ on all finite structures, without an order, is open.
