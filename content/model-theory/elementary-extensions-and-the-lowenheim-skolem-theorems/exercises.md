1. ◦ Which of these inclusions are [elementary](/8WZF9SBP39/)? For each one that is not, give a formula and parameters that show it. (a) $(\N, <) \subseteq (\Z, <)$; (b) $(\Z, <) \subseteq (\Q, <)$; (c) $(\Q_{>0}, <) \subseteq (\Q, <)$; (d) $(\Z, +) \subseteq (\Q, +)$; (e) $(2\Z, +) \subseteq (\Z, +)$.

2. ◦ Let $A = (\Q \cap [0, 1], <)$, a substructure of $(\Q, <)$. Give a formula $\varphi(x, y)$, a parameter $a \in A$ and a witness $b \in \Q$ with $\Q \models \varphi(a, b)$ such that no element of $A$ is a witness. Which condition of the [Tarski–Vaught test](/60SA99BER7/) fails?

3. ◦ Say whether a structure with the stated property exists, and why. (a) A countable elementary substructure of $(\R, +, \cdot, 0, 1, <)$ that contains $\pi$ and $e$. (b) An elementary extension of $(\N, +, \cdot)$ of size $\aleph_1$. (c) A finite elementary substructure of an infinite structure. (d) A countable elementary substructure of $(\R, <, (q)_{q \in \Q})$, with a constant symbol for each rational. (e) A countable elementary substructure of $(\R, <, (r)_{r \in \R})$, with a constant symbol for each real.

4. ◦ Let $\mathcal{M} = (\set{0, 1, 2}, <)$ with the usual order. How many sentences does the atomic [diagram](/EP5E317TNW/) $\mathrm{Diag}(\mathcal{M})$ contain, how many of them are negated, and which are they?

5. ◦ Give a [Skolem function](/NKQY9NB955/) in $(\Z, <)$ for each formula: (a) $x < y$; (b) $y < x$; (c) $x_1 < y \wedge y < x_2$; (d) the sentence $\exists y\, \forall z\, \neg(z < y)$, which has no witness.

6. ◦ Let $M_n = \set{k / n! : k \in \Z}$ for $n \ge 1$, with addition. Each $(M_n, +)$ is isomorphic to $(\Z, +)$, the $M_n$ form a chain, and their union is $\Q$. Is the chain elementary? Find $n$, a formula and a parameter that show where it fails, and say what the [elementary chain theorem](/NHCRZ74JHH/) would otherwise give.

7. ◦ Which of these maps are [partial elementary maps](/XTNYY0WRQ5/) from $(\Z, <)$ to $(\Z, <)$? (a) $1 \mapsto 10$, $4 \mapsto 13$; (b) $1 \mapsto 0$, $4 \mapsto 5$; (c) $0 \mapsto 7$, $1 \mapsto 8$, $2 \mapsto 9$; (d) $5 \mapsto -5$; (e) $0 \mapsto 3$, $3 \mapsto 0$.

8. • Use the Tarski–Vaught test and automorphisms of $(\Q, <)$ to show that $(\Q \cap (0, 1), <) \preceq (\Q, <)$. Write the automorphism explicitly for one choice of parameters and witness.

9. • (a) Let $\mathcal{M} \subseteq \mathcal{M}' \subseteq \mathcal{N}$ with $\mathcal{M} \preceq \mathcal{N}$ and $\mathcal{M}' \preceq \mathcal{N}$. Show that $\mathcal{M} \preceq \mathcal{M}'$. (b) Show by the fields $\overline{\Q} \subseteq \overline{\Q}(\pi) \subseteq \C$ that $\mathcal{M} \preceq \mathcal{N}$ and $\mathcal{M} \subseteq \mathcal{M}' \subseteq \mathcal{N}$ do not imply $\mathcal{M}' \preceq \mathcal{N}$.

10. • Prove the [Szpilrajn extension theorem](/T9CJHV6JW3/), that every partial order $(P, \le)$ extends to a linear order on $P$, from its finite case. Use constants for the elements of $P$, the strict relations $a < b$ of the partial order, and the compactness theorem, in the style of the diagram lemmas.

11. • Let $K$ be a countable elementary substructure of the ordered field $(\R, +, \cdot, 0, 1, <)$ with $\pi \in K$. Show that $K$ contains $\sqrt{\pi}$ and every real algebraic number, and that $K \ne \R_{\mathrm{alg}}$.

12. • (a) Show that $(\N, <)$ has an elementary extension of size $\aleph_1$. (b) Show that every proper elementary extension of $(\N, <)$ contains an element that is larger than every natural number.

13. • (a) Show that $(\Z, <)$ has no proper elementary substructure. (b) In the empty signature, find two elementary substructures of the set $\N$ whose intersection is not an elementary substructure.

14. • Let $\mathcal{M} = (\Q, <)$ and $\mathcal{N}_1 = \mathcal{N}_2 = (\R, <)$, with the inclusions as $f_1$ and $f_2$. For which pairs $(a, b)$ of reals is there an amalgam, as in the [elementary amalgamation theorem](/P2VMSM4H4Y/), with $g_1(a) = g_2(b)$?

15. • (a) Show that in the field $(\R, +, -, \cdot, 0, 1)$ the only partial elementary map with domain $\set{\sqrt[3]{2}}$ is the identity. (b) In the field $(\C, +, -, \cdot, 0, 1)$, is $\pi \mapsto e$ a partial elementary map? Is $\pi \mapsto \sqrt 2$? Use [quantifier elimination for algebraically closed fields](/MT8TDVPBXB/) for the first question; you may assume that $e$ is transcendental.

16. • Show that $(\Z, <)$ has a countable elementary extension that is not isomorphic to it. Use both parts of the [Löwenheim–Skolem theorem](/484M0TZ1S7/).

17. ★ Let $\mathcal{M} \preceq \mathcal{N}$, let $\varphi(\bar x, y)$ be a formula and $\bar a$ a tuple of $M$. Show that if $X = \set{b \in N : \mathcal{N} \models \varphi(\bar a, b)}$ is finite, then $X \subseteq M$. Give an example in which $X$ is infinite and not contained in $M$.

18. ★ Show that the smallest elementary substructure of the field $(\C, +, -, \cdot, 0, 1)$ that contains $\pi$ exists, and that it is the set $F$ of complex numbers algebraic over $\Q(\pi)$. Use [this example](/61VW0F3R83/) for one inclusion and the previous exercise, or a direct argument, for the other.

19. ★ Show that every proper elementary extension of the ordered field $(\R, +, \cdot, 0, 1, <)$ is non-Archimedean, and that such extensions exist. Conclude that the Archimedean property is not expressible by a set of first-order sentences in this signature.

20. ★ Find two dense linear orders without endpoints, each of which embeds elementarily into the other, that are not isomorphic. Use the fact that every embedding between dense linear orders without endpoints is elementary.
