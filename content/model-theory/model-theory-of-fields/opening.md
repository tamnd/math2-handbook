Fields were the first large source of examples for model theory, and they remain its main meeting point with algebra. The theory of [algebraically closed fields](/0PF6TM8TWT/) has quantifier elimination, so a definable set is a finite Boolean combination of zero sets of polynomials, a constructible set in the sense of algebraic geometry. The completions of this theory are the theories $\mathrm{ACF}_p$, one for each characteristic $p$, and each is decidable. The [Lefschetz principle](/SKTMN14X5P/) follows: a sentence of the language of rings holds in $\C$ exactly when it holds in algebraically closed fields of every large enough prime characteristic. The [Ax–Grothendieck theorem](/M2XAN2HGZH/), that an injective polynomial map $\C^n \to \C^n$ is surjective, is proved this way from the finite fields.

[Real closed fields](/DDY3Q3PJ6W/) are the ordered counterpart. Tarski proved [quantifier elimination for real closed fields](/FSJ9NGRRPA/), so elementary algebra and geometry over $\R$ is decidable, and the [Tarski–Seidenberg theorem](/3W5JGDZAN0/) says that a projection of a semialgebraic set is semialgebraic. Abraham Robinson used this theory to give a model-theoretic proof of Artin's solution of [Hilbert's seventeenth problem](/J45F4F13VB/) on positive polynomials as sums of squares of rational functions.

Other classes of fields have the same pattern of a good theory and an algebraic payoff. James Ax described the elementary theory of all finite fields and the pseudofinite fields, the infinite fields that satisfy every sentence true in all finite fields; an [ultraproduct of prime fields](/145GABE79C/) is one. [Differentially closed fields](/KPCN3KHG50/) of characteristic $0$ play the part of algebraically closed fields for differential equations, and [separably closed fields](/8B57B7EGK4/) and [PAC fields](/92SQ10PN6J/) link the subject with field arithmetic and Galois theory. The field $\Q$ sits at the other end: Julia Robinson defined $\Z$ in $\Q$, so the theory of $\Q$ is undecidable.
