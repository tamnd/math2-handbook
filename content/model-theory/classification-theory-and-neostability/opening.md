Shelah's classification program asks how many models a complete theory $T$ in a countable language has in each infinite cardinality $\kappa$, and when its models can be described by invariants such as dimensions. [Morley's theorem](/V7N84P60SJ/) answers one case: the count can be $1$ in one uncountable cardinal only when it is $1$ in all of them. Shelah's main gap theorem answers the general question. Either $T$ has $2^{\aleph_\alpha}$ models of size $\aleph_\alpha$ for every ordinal $\alpha > 0$, the most possible, or the number stays below $\beth_{\omega_1}(\abs{\alpha})$ for every $\alpha > 0$. In the second case $T$ is superstable, and each model is built from countable pieces arranged along a well-founded tree, as a vector space is built from a basis. The proof locates a list of dividing lines (stable, superstable, the dimensional order property, the omitting types order property, depth), each a property whose presence forces many models and whose absence yields a structure theorem.

Some of these lines also matter for theories with the most models. Neostability is the study of independence and rank beyond stable theories. Simple theories, introduced by Shelah in 1980, include the [Rado graph](/RMXR0FEF54/), pseudofinite fields and algebraically closed fields with a generic automorphism. In the late 1990s Kim showed that forking in a simple theory is symmetric, and Kim and Pillay characterized simple theories by an independence theorem for types over models. A theory is [stable](/T7JW5QY6KR/) exactly when it is simple and [NIP](/74VCDYQDGP/), so these two classes extend stability in different directions. Further classes, such as $\mathrm{NTP}_2$ and $\mathrm{NSOP}_1$, have been studied closely since about 2010; for $\mathrm{NSOP}_1$, Kaplan and Ramsey built a theory of Kim-independence. In 2016 Malliaris and Shelah used [Keisler's order](/TD5D2G5PYP/) on theories to prove the equality $\mathfrak{p} = \mathfrak{t}$ of two cardinal invariants of the continuum.
