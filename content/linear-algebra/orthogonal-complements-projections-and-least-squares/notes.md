## History

The method of least squares came from astronomy and geodesy, where there were more observations than unknowns and the observations disagreed. Adrien-Marie Legendre published it in 1805, in an appendix to *Nouvelles méthodes pour la détermination des orbites des comètes*, and named it. Carl Friedrich Gauss published his account in 1809 in *Theoria motus corporum coelestium*, said that he had used the method since 1795, and connected it with the normal law of errors. He solved the [normal equations](/4D7MRG4D91/) by the elimination now named after him.

The geometric reading came with inner products on spaces of functions. Jørgen Pedersen Gram studied series expansions by least squares in 1883, and Erhard Schmidt stated the orthogonalization process in its present form in 1907, in his work on integral equations. In Hilbert space theory the projection onto a closed subspace and the decomposition $H = U \oplus U^\perp$ became basic tools, and the [approximation by Legendre polynomials and Fourier sums](/899D63WW04/) became special cases of one projection formula. Camille Jordan had already discussed the angles between subspaces of any dimension in 1875, and Harold Hotelling's canonical correlations of 1936 are the cosines of [principal angles](/YHVD3773AW/).

The shortest solution of a system belongs to the history of the generalized inverse. E. H. Moore described such an inverse in 1920, and Roger Penrose characterized it independently in 1955 in "A generalized inverse for matrices". The computational side changed once computers made rounding errors visible. Alston Householder showed in 1958 how to triangularize a matrix by reflections, and Gene Golub used this in 1965 to solve least-squares problems through a [QR decomposition](/0ERWG4NXQ9/) without forming $A^{\mathsf{T}}A$. Åke Björck and Golub gave a stable method for computing principal angles in 1973.

## Further reading

- Gilbert Strang, *Introduction to Linear Algebra*, sixth edition (2023). The four fundamental subspaces, projections and least squares, with many fitted lines and parabolas.
- Sheldon Axler, *Linear Algebra Done Right*, fourth edition (2024). Orthogonal complements and minimization problems in abstract inner product spaces, including the approximation of functions by polynomials.
- David C. Lay, Steven R. Lay and Judi J. McDonald, *Linear Algebra and Its Applications*, fifth edition (2016). Orthogonality, projections, Gram–Schmidt, QR and least squares at the level of this chapter, with applications to linear models.
- Lloyd N. Trefethen and David Bau III, *Numerical Linear Algebra* (1997). Short lectures on projectors, QR by Gram–Schmidt and by Householder reflections, and the conditioning of least-squares problems.
- Åke Björck, *Numerical Methods for Least Squares Problems* (1996). The standard reference on the algorithms, from the normal equations to QR and the singular value decomposition, with their error analysis.
