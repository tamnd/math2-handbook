Linear algebra starts with arrows. A displacement, a velocity or a force has a size and a direction, and two of them combine by the parallelogram rule. Written with components, an arrow in space is a triple of numbers such as $(1, 1, 1)$, and the geometry of arrows becomes arithmetic with triples. Lengths, angles, areas and volumes are then short formulas.

Two examples show the method. The four hydrogen atoms of a methane molecule sit at alternate corners of a cube with the carbon atom at its centre, so two bonds point along $u = (1, 1, 1)$ and $v = (1, -1, -1)$. The [dot product](/0S52TNNTH9/) gives $u \cdot v = -1$ and $\norm{u} = \norm{v} = \sqrt 3$, so the bond angle $\theta$ has $\cos\theta = -\tfrac13$ and $\theta \approx 109.47^\circ$. No picture of the molecule is needed. In the second example, the plane $x + 2y + 2z = 9$ has the normal vector $n = (1, 2, 2)$ of length $3$, and the distance from the origin to the plane is $9/3 = 3$. Both answers come from one product of two vectors.

The chapter opens with four essays on the subject as a whole. The [overview](/Y7PBW8VD4Q/) shows the three views of linear algebra: the system $x + 2y = 5$, $3x + 4y = 6$ is a pair of equations, a matrix equation $Ax = b$, and a question about a linear map. The rest of the chapter is about vectors in the plane and in space, the setting where the later abstract notions have their pictures.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in five groups, read in order.

1. **The subject and how it is taught.** The [overview](/Y7PBW8VD4Q/) and three essays: [teaching linear algebra](/9AXCDDT1Z1/), [linear algebra without determinants](/4C6EBHFF3R/) and the [common errors](/79YPKR857M/) of learners, each with a counterexample.
2. **Vectors and their length.** The [Euclidean vector](/0QN3D80AWP/) as an arrow and as a list of components, with its sum and scalar multiples, and the [length](/0XKBKGTCPC/) $\norm{v}$, with unit vectors and distances.
3. **The dot product.** The [dot product](/0S52TNNTH9/) in its coordinate and geometric forms, the [angle between two vectors](/0ZEHZY85E6/), the [perpendicularity test](/03NK6RSRQ1/) $u \cdot v = 0$ and the [vector projection](/0JW13B48YP/).
4. **Products in space.** The [cross product](/0N5N15HWZ7/) of two vectors in $\R^3$, the [scalar triple product](/34VJYG6GVD/) as a signed volume and the [vector triple product](/03FTB26QYD/) with its expansion $a \times (b \times c) = (a \cdot c)\, b - (a \cdot b)\, c$.
5. **Lines, planes, distances and proofs.** The [equations of a line](/0399QFDFS8/) and of a [plane](/07SDJ5DNE4/), the [distances](/C6GAS92QHX/) between points, lines and planes, and [vector proofs in plane geometry](/ZVEKWJTSV7/).

## Before you start

The chapter needs the arithmetic of real numbers, square roots and the solution of simple equations, as in [elementary algebra and functions](/DPW5ETDQ27/). From [Euclidean geometry](/D1SR8GDYJX/) it uses triangles, parallels, the Pythagorean theorem and the law of cosines. The cosine of an angle comes from [angles and right-triangle trigonometry](/N4X05D0TPR/), and coordinates in the plane and the equations of lines come from the [analytic geometry of lines and circles](/6NVHQ3XAD6/). No calculus and no matrices are assumed.
