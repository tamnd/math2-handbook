11. The vectors lie in one plane through $0$ exactly when their scalar triple product vanishes. The determinant is $1 + k^3 = (k + 1)(k^2 - k + 1)$, and the second factor has no real root, so $k = -1$.

13. The direction is perpendicular to both normals, so take $(1, 1, 1) \times (1, -1, 2) = (3, -1, -2)$. Setting $z = 0$ gives the point $\left(\tfrac12, \tfrac12, 0\right)$, so the line is $\left(\tfrac12, \tfrac12, 0\right) + t(3, -1, -2)$.

15. The common perpendicular is $d \times e = (-1, -1, 1)$ and $(1, 1, 0) \cdot (d \times e) = -2$, so the distance is $\tfrac{2}{\sqrt 3}$. Asking that the connecting vector be perpendicular to both directions gives $t = \tfrac13$ and $s = -\tfrac13$, so the closest points are $\left(\tfrac13, 0, \tfrac13\right)$ and $\left(1, \tfrac23, -\tfrac13\right)$.

18. By the exchange of dot and cross, $(a \times b) \cdot (c \times d) = a \cdot \big(b \times (c \times d)\big)$. Expand the inner vector triple product as $(b \cdot d)\, c - (b \cdot c)\, d$. For the second claim, put $c = a$ and $d = b$.

19. $a \times x$ is perpendicular to $a$, so $a \cdot b = 0$ is necessary. If $a \cdot b = 0$, the vector triple product gives $a \times (b \times a) = (a \cdot a)\, b$, so $x_0 = \dfrac{b \times a}{a \cdot a}$ is a solution. Two solutions differ by a vector $y$ with $a \times y = 0$, that is, a multiple of $a$. Here $x = \left(-\tfrac12, \tfrac12, 1\right) + t(1, 1, 0)$.

20. For every point $H$ of the plane, $(H - A) \cdot (B - C) + (H - B) \cdot (C - A) + (H - C) \cdot (A - B) = 0$, since all the terms cancel. If the first two terms vanish, so does the third.

21. Cross $a + b + c = 0$ with $a$ and with $b$, and use $a \times a = b \times b = 0$. The angle between the vectors $a$ and $b$ is the exterior angle at their common vertex, which has the same sine as the interior angle $\gamma$ opposite $c$. So $\norm{a}\,\norm{b}\sin\gamma = \norm{b}\,\norm{c}\sin\alpha = \norm{c}\,\norm{a}\sin\beta$. Divide by $\norm{a}\,\norm{b}\,\norm{c}$.
