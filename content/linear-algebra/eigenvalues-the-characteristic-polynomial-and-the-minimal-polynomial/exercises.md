1. ◦ Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & -2 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Find the [characteristic polynomial](/0VSSPAFM2C/), the eigenvalues, and an eigenvector for each eigenvalue.

2. ◦ Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Decide which of the vectors $(1, 2, 1)$, $(1, 1, 1)$ and $(1, 0, -1)$ are [eigenvectors](/0N6960W0BV/) of $A$, and give the eigenvalue when they are. Then use the [trace](/0NMXDCNNKR/) to find the third eigenvalue of $A$.

3. ◦ Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$. Compute $\operatorname{tr} A$, the three $2 \times 2$ principal minors and $\det A$, and write down $p_A(t)$ with the theorem on the [coefficients of the characteristic polynomial](/0FRYPZ6D0X/).

4. ◦ A real $3 \times 3$ matrix $A$ has the eigenvalues $1$, $2$ and $-3$. Find $\operatorname{tr} A$, $\det A$, $\operatorname{tr}(A^2)$ and the eigenvalues of $A^2 + A$. Is $A$ invertible, and if so, what are the eigenvalues of $A^{-1}$?

5. ◦ For $A = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 5 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, find the [algebraic](/0FY42N02DV/) and the [geometric multiplicity](/099TWVJ909/) of each eigenvalue. Does $\R^3$ have a basis of eigenvectors of $A$?

6. ◦ Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Find the [spectrum](/0XPX5NC67E/) of $A$ when its entries are read in $\R$, in $\C$, in $\F_5$ and in $\F_3$.

7. ◦ Find the [minimal polynomials](/03NDVCSHSB/) of $\operatorname{diag}(2, 2, 5)$ and of $\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$, and compare them with the characteristic polynomials.

8. ◦ Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Use the [Cayley-Hamilton theorem](/0F765KFC2J/) to write $A^{-1}$ and $A^3$ as combinations of $A$ and $I$, and compute both matrices.

9. ◦ Let $A = (1 \ \ 2 \ \ 3)$, a $1 \times 3$ matrix, and let $B$ be the $3 \times 1$ matrix with all entries $1$. Compute $AB$, the characteristic polynomial of $BA$, and the eigenvalues of $BA$ with their algebraic multiplicities.

10. • Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$. Find $p_A$, a basis of each [eigenspace](/0G0RFPG8HR/), and the minimal polynomial of $A$. Show that $A$ is not diagonalizable.

11. • Let $A$ be a complex square matrix with $A^3 = A$. Prove that every eigenvalue of $A$ is $-1$, $0$ or $1$, using the [eigenvalues of a polynomial in A](/0E3MV6E6MG/). Give a $3 \times 3$ example in which all three values occur.

12. • Let $P = \begin{pmatrix} 1/2 & 1/2 \\ 1/4 & 3/4 \end{pmatrix}$. Find a [left eigenvector](/32M8VY9A1M/) $\pi$ of $P$ for the eigenvalue $1$ whose entries add up to $1$, and a right eigenvector $v$ for the other eigenvalue. Check that $\pi v = 0$.

13. • A real $3 \times 3$ matrix $A$ has the eigenvalue $1 + 2i$ and $\operatorname{tr} A = 5$. Find the other two eigenvalues of $A$ and $\det A$.

14. • Let $T$ be an endomorphism of a vector space $V$ such that every nonzero vector of $V$ is an eigenvector of $T$. Prove that $T = cI$ for some scalar $c$.

15. • Show that the characteristic polynomial of $C = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & -c_0 \\ 1 & 0 & -c_1 \\ 0 & 1 & -c_2 \end{pmatrix}$ is $t^3 + c_2 t^2 + c_1 t + c_0$. Then give a real $3 \times 3$ matrix with characteristic polynomial $t^3 - 2t + 5$.

16. • Let $A$ be a square matrix with $A^2 - 3A + 2I = 0$, $A \ne I$ and $A \ne 2I$. Find the minimal polynomial of $A$, and list the possible characteristic polynomials of $A$ when $A$ is $3 \times 3$.

17. • A $3 \times 3$ matrix has the characteristic polynomial $(x - 2)^2 (x + 1)$. Use the fact that the [minimal polynomial divides the characteristic polynomial](/093X6MN6Y6/) and has the same roots to list the possible minimal polynomials, and give a matrix for each.

18. • Let $F = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$. Show that $F^2 = F + I$, and deduce that $F^n = f_n F + f_{n-1} I$ for $n \ge 1$, where $f_0 = 0$, $f_1 = 1$ and $f_{k+1} = f_k + f_{k-1}$. Compute $F^6$ in this way.

19. • Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$. Compute $p_A$ and use it to find $A^{-1}$ as a polynomial in $A$, as in the entry on the [inverse and powers by Cayley-Hamilton](/R4CPYXFJEQ/).

20. • Prove that there are no $n \times n$ matrices $A$ and $B$ over a field of characteristic $0$ with $AB - BA = I$. Then find $2 \times 2$ matrices over $\F_2$ with $AB - BA = I$.

21. • Let $A$ be a real $n \times n$ matrix with $n$ odd. Prove that $A$ has a real eigenvalue, using the characteristic polynomial and the intermediate value theorem. Compare with the theorem on [invariant subspaces of real operators](/KAV44BRV77/).

22. ★ Let $A$ and $B$ be complex $n \times n$ matrices. Prove that $p_{AB} = p_{BA}$ without block matrices: first for invertible $A$, by similarity, and then for all $A$, by applying the first case to $A - sI$ and letting $s$ vary.

23. ★ Let $\lambda$ be an eigenvalue of an endomorphism $T$ of a finite-dimensional space. Prove that its geometric multiplicity is at most its algebraic multiplicity. Extend a basis of $E_\lambda$ to a basis of $V$ and use the [characteristic polynomial of a block triangular matrix](/C98TZ6ZAXC/).

24. ★ Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over a field $F$ with $n$ distinct eigenvalues in $F$, and let $B$ be an $n \times n$ matrix with $AB = BA$. Prove that every eigenvector of $A$ is an eigenvector of $B$, and that $B = f(A)$ for some polynomial $f \in F[x]$ of degree less than $n$.

25. ★ Let $A$ be a complex $n \times n$ matrix with $\operatorname{tr}(A^k) = 0$ for $k = 1, 2, \ldots, n$. Prove that $A^n = 0$.

26. ★ Let $T$ be an endomorphism of a finite-dimensional space $V$ and let $v \ne 0$. Let $k$ be the least integer such that $v, Tv, \ldots, T^k v$ are linearly dependent, and write $T^k v = -(a_0 v + a_1 Tv + \cdots + a_{k-1} T^{k-1} v)$. Let $q(x) = x^k + a_{k-1} x^{k-1} + \cdots + a_0$ and $W = \operatorname{span}(v, Tv, \ldots, T^{k-1} v)$. Prove that $W$ is $T$-invariant, that $q$ is the minimal polynomial of $T|_W$, and that $q$ divides $m_T$. Deduce that every root of $q$ in $F$ is an eigenvalue of $T$.
