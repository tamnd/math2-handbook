A car hire firm has two branches, $A$ and $B$, and $600$ cars. Each week every car is hired once. Of the cars that start the week at $A$, $80\%$ are returned to $A$ and $20\%$ to $B$; of those that start at $B$, $40\%$ are returned to $A$ and $60\%$ to $B$. If $x_k$ lists the numbers of cars at the two branches after $k$ weeks, then
$$x_{k+1} = P x_k, \qquad P = \begin{pmatrix} 0.8 & 0.4 \\ 0.2 & 0.6 \end{pmatrix}.$$
Start with all cars at $A$, so $x_0 = (600, 0)$. One step gives $x_1 = (480, 120)$, and the next gives $x_2 = (432, 168)$. The pattern becomes clear from the eigenvectors. The vector $(400, 200)$ satisfies $P(400, 200) = (400, 200)$, and $(1, -1)$ satisfies $P(1, -1) = 0.4\,(1, -1)$. Since $x_0 = (400, 200) + 200\,(1, -1)$,
$$x_k = (400, 200) + 200 \cdot 0.4^k \,(1, -1).$$
So the fleet settles at $400$ cars at $A$ and $200$ at $B$, whatever the starting split, and the distance from that split shrinks by the factor $0.4$ every week. A question about the future of a business has been answered by one eigenvector and one eigenvalue.

The chapter collects problems of this kind. Each entry starts from a situation in science, engineering or computing, turns it into a matrix, and shows which result of the earlier chapters settles it. Row reduction handles networks, chemical equations and interpolation; orthogonal projection handles the fitting of data; diagonalization handles recurrences, populations, Markov chains, web ranking and differential equations; orthonormal changes of basis and the singular value decomposition handle pictures and signals; and the rank over the field $\mathbb F_2$ handles a puzzle. The entries prove little that is new. Their purpose is to show the theory at work and to train the step that textbooks often skip, which is writing down the right matrix.

## What the chapter covers

The four groups are independent of one another, and each can be read on its own once its prerequisites are in place.

1. **Linear systems in models.** The [applications of linear systems](/1QXFM0DNAH/) set up traffic flow, electric circuits, chemical equations and an input-output economy as systems $Ax = b$. [Polynomial interpolation](/NR58EBS932/) is a square Vandermonde system with exactly one solution, and the Lagrange basis writes it down. [Curve fitting by least squares](/C170QFW8QB/) treats data with more equations than unknowns and reads $R^2$ as a ratio of squared lengths.
2. **Evolution in discrete and continuous time.** [Linear recurrences](/K00NJY6C33/) are solved by diagonalizing a companion matrix, which gives Binet's formula for the Fibonacci numbers. [Discrete dynamical systems](/2F1B461YN7/) cover predator and prey, age-structured populations, and the attractors, repellers and saddles of the plane. [Steady states of Markov chains](/Y9FRWY8YX4/) and [PageRank](/9ZPQYZ5NDP/) find an eigenvector for the eigenvalue $1$ of a stochastic matrix, and [systems of differential equations](/DH7CQXQYJX/) replace powers $\lambda^k$ by exponentials $e^{\lambda t}$.
3. **Coordinates for pictures and signals.** [Computer graphics](/REB4ZHN28T/) uses homogeneous coordinates to make translations and perspective into matrices. [Changes of basis in signal and image processing](/6NANBCYMG2/) explain the Fourier and Haar bases, the colour space YCbCr and the steps of JPEG. [Image compression by the SVD](/9QB82PPEMV/) keeps the largest singular values and has an exact error formula.
4. **Puzzles and graphs.** The [Lights Out puzzle](/E8DZ9CPEEE/) is a linear system over $\mathbb F_2$ whose rank decides which patterns can be switched off. The [matrices of a graph](/JFWP2SBCF0/) count walks, describe cycles and solve electrical networks.

## A first reading

A short course takes one or two entries from each group: [curve fitting by least squares](/C170QFW8QB/), [linear recurrences](/K00NJY6C33/), [steady states of Markov chains](/Y9FRWY8YX4/), [systems of differential equations](/DH7CQXQYJX/), [computer graphics](/REB4ZHN28T/), [image compression by the SVD](/9QB82PPEMV/) and the [Lights Out puzzle](/E8DZ9CPEEE/). Add [PageRank](/9ZPQYZ5NDP/) after the Markov chains, since it is the same computation for a very large matrix.

The entry on applications of linear systems is four separate models in one page, and one of them is enough on a first pass. The Fourier part of the signal-processing entry needs complex numbers and the unitary matrices of the chapter on isometries; the Haar part does not. The Lights Out entry needs nothing beyond row reduction and is the clearest place to see that elimination works over any [field](/0ZXZ82FTX2/).

## Before you start

Each group draws on one part of the theory. The first needs [Gaussian elimination](/0JVJ2WCXZH/) from [systems of linear equations and row reduction](/DXSXFVDMXQ/) and the [normal equations](/4D7MRG4D91/) from [orthogonal complements, projections and least squares](/8GQVME4SZZ/). The second rests on [diagonalization, triangularization and invariant subspaces](/Z6S6PWMJPH/), above all on [powers of a diagonalizable matrix](/0TNR9BQ871/). The third uses orthogonal and unitary matrices from [isometries and the orthogonal and unitary groups](/AZS5BPF5H3/) and the [Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem](/JJT6GC055J/) from [positive definite matrices and the singular value decomposition](/SS4EVPKT3A/). The fourth needs the [rank](/0ZC9YTXZRY/), the [null space](/0PHDYNR24D/) and the [rank-nullity theorem](/0W4FSW1TVB/), and at the end the eigenvalues of a symmetric matrix.
