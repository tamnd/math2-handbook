1. ◦ Write the symmetric matrix $A$ of the real quadratic form $Q(x, y, z) = x^2 - 3y^2 + 2z^2 + 4xy - 2xz + 6yz$, and compute $Q(1, 1, 1)$ both from the polynomial and as $v^{\mathsf T} A v$.

2. ◦ (a) For $Q(x, y) = 4x^2 - 6xy + y^2$ over $\R$, write the [polar form](/0HYFWKKY3C/) $b_Q$ and the symmetric bilinear form $B = \tfrac12 b_Q$ as polynomials in $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$, and check that $b_Q(v, v) = 2Q(v)$. (b) Over $\F_2$, compute the polar form of $x^2 + xy + y^2$ and show that $b_Q(v, v) = 0$ for every $v$.

3. ◦ Which of these functions $\R^2 \to \R$ are quadratic forms? (a) $x^2 - 5xy$; (b) $xy + x$; (c) $(x + y)(2x - y)$; (d) $\abs{x} \, \abs{y}$. For each one that is not, name the condition of the definition that fails.

4. ◦ A student writes $Q(x, y, z) = (x - y)^2 + (y - z)^2 - (x - z)^2$ and concludes that the signature of $Q$ is $(2, 1, 0)$. Find the error and give the correct signature.

5. ◦ Sort each real form into one of the five types (positive definite, positive semidefinite but not definite, negative definite, negative semidefinite but not definite, indefinite): (a) $2x^2 - 4xy + 5y^2$; (b) $-x^2 + 2xy - y^2$; (c) $x^2 + 2xy + y^2 - z^2$.

6. ◦ Let $Q(x, y, z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - 2xy + 2xz - 2yz$. Find the rank of $Q$, its normal form over $\C$, and its signature over $\R$.

7. ◦ Compute the [discriminant](/MZFRXECCMC/) in $\Q^\times / \Q^{\times 2}$ of (a) $2x^2 + 2xy + 5y^2$; (b) $3x^2 - 12y^2$; (c) $xy$. Name in each case the simplest representative among $\pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 3, \ldots$.

8. ◦ Find the error: "The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ has both eigenvalues equal to $1$, so the form $x^{\mathsf T} A x = x^2 + 3xy + y^2$ is positive definite." Give a vector at which the form is negative.

9. ◦ Find the largest and the smallest value of $Q(x, y) = 4x^2 + 6xy - 4y^2$ on the unit circle, and the unit vectors at which they are attained.

10. • Use Lagrange's method to [diagonalize](/0Z3FEWZ8HA/) $Q(x, y, z) = x^2 + 2y^2 + 3z^2 - 2xy + 4xz - 2yz$ over $\Q$. Give an invertible $P$ with $P^{\mathsf T} A P$ diagonal, the signature over $\R$, and a nonzero vector $v \in \Q^3$ with $Q(v) < 0$.

11. • The form $Q(x, y, z) = 4xy + 2yz$ has no square terms. Write it as a combination of squares of linearly independent linear forms, find its signature, and find its radical (the vectors $v$ with $B(v, w) = 0$ for all $w$).

12. • Work over $\F_7$. (a) List the nonzero squares. (b) Show that $Q = 3x^2 + 3y^2$ is anisotropic. (c) Find a basis $v_1, v_2$ of $\F_7^2$ with $Q(v_1) = Q(v_2) = 1$ and $B(v_1, v_2) = 0$, and conclude that $Q$ is equivalent to $x^2 + y^2$. (d) Check the answer against the discriminant test of [forms over finite fields](/2HBR7137AD/).

13. • For which real $a$ is the form with matrix
$$A_a = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & a & 0 \\ a & 1 & a \\ 0 & a & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$
positive definite? Answer by leading principal minors, and check by showing that the eigenvalues are $1$ and $1 \pm a\sqrt2$. What is the signature for $a = 1$?

14. • Let $Q(x, y, z) = 2x^2 + y^2 + 4xy + 4yz$. Show that $(2, 2, 1)$, $(2, -1, -2)$ and $(1, -2, 2)$ are eigenvectors of its matrix, find an orthogonal $P$ that turns $Q$ into $\lambda_1 u^2 + \lambda_2 v^2 + \lambda_3 w^2$, and name the surfaces $Q = 4$ and $Q = -4$.

15. • Put the conic $9x^2 - 4xy + 6y^2 - 10x - 20y - 5 = 0$ in normal form: find its centre, the eigenvalues and unit eigenvectors of the quadratic part, the equation in rotated coordinates, and the lengths and directions of the semi-axes.

16. • Find the error: "The matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ has leading principal minors $1, 0, 0$, all nonnegative, so its form is positive semidefinite." Complete the square to find the true type of the form, and give a vector where it is negative.

17. • Find the critical points of $f(x, y) = x^3 + y^3 - 6xy$ and classify each one with the [second derivative test](/0GYDV9FE98/), that is, by the type of the quadratic form of the Hessian.

18. • How many equivalence classes of quadratic forms on $F^4$ are there for $F = \C$, $F = \R$ and $F = \F_5$? How many of them are nondegenerate in each case? Justify each count from the classification theorems.

19. • Over $\F_5$, let $Q = x^2 + 2y^2 + 3z^2$. Find a nonzero isotropic vector, and decide which of $x^2 + y^2 + z^2$ and $x^2 + y^2 + 2z^2$ is equivalent to $Q$.

20. • Show that $2x^2 + 2xy + 5y^2$ and $x^2 + y^2$ are equivalent over $\Q$ by writing both as $2s^2 + 2t^2$ with rational changes of variables.

21. ★ Let $F$ have characteristic not $2$ and let $Q$ be a nondegenerate quadratic form on $V$ of finite dimension. Show that if $Q$ is isotropic, then $Q$ is universal: for every $c \in F$ there is $v$ with $Q(v) = c$. Deduce that every nondegenerate form in at least $3$ variables over $\F_q$, $q$ odd, takes every value.

22. ★ Let $p$ be an odd prime. Show that exactly $p^2$ of the $p^3$ symmetric $2 \times 2$ matrices over $\F_p$ are singular. Then show that for $d \ne 0$ the number of symmetric matrices with determinant $d$ is $p^2 + p$ if $-d$ is a square in $\F_p$ and $p^2 - p$ if it is not. Use this to find the sizes of the two classes of nondegenerate binary forms over $\F_7$.

23. ★ Let $A$ be a real symmetric $n \times n$ matrix with eigenvalues $\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \cdots \ge \lambda_n$ and a unit eigenvector $u_1$ for $\lambda_1$. Prove that the maximum of $x^{\mathsf T} A x$ over unit vectors $x$ with $x \perp u_1$ is $\lambda_2$. For $Q(x, y, z) = 2x^2 + y^2 + 4xy + 4yz$, find the maximum of $Q$ on unit vectors orthogonal to $(2, 2, 1)$ and a vector where it is attained.

24. ★ Let $M$ be a real symmetric positive definite $n \times n$ matrix and $A$ any real symmetric $n \times n$ matrix. Prove that there is an invertible $P$ with $P^{\mathsf T} M P = I$ and $P^{\mathsf T} A P$ diagonal, and that the diagonal entries are the roots of $\det(A - \lambda M) = 0$. Carry this out for $M = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$.

25. ★ For a real quadratic form $Q$ on $V$ of finite dimension, let $p(Q)$ be the number of positive coefficients in a diagonal form. Prove that $p(Q)$ is the largest dimension of a subspace on which $Q$ is positive definite. Deduce that $p(Q_1 + Q_2) \le p(Q_1) + p(Q_2)$ for any two real forms $Q_1, Q_2$ on $V$, and give an example in which the inequality is strict.
