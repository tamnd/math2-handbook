2. The zero vector is $-1$, since $x \oplus (-1) = x$. The negative of $x$ is $-x - 2$, since $x \oplus (-x - 2) = -1$. And $1 \odot x = x + 1 - 1 = x$. The map $x \mapsto x + 1$ carries these operations to the usual ones.

5. (a) Dependent: the third vector is the sum of the first two, so $v_1 + v_2 - v_3 = 0$. (b) Independent: the matrix with these columns has determinant $5$, so it has three pivots.

6. $[(3, 1)]_B = (2, 1)$ and $[(3, 1)]_{B'} = (1, 2)$. The order of the basis vectors is part of an ordered basis.

9. The span is $\set{(0, 0, 0), (1, 1, 0), (0, 1, 1), (1, 0, 1)}$, which does not contain $(1, 1, 1)$. The space $\mathbb{F}_3^2$ has $(9 - 1)(9 - 3) = 48$ ordered bases.

11. If $U \not\subseteq W$ and $W \not\subseteq U$, take $u \in U \setminus W$ and $w \in W \setminus U$; then $u + w$ lies in neither. In $\mathbb{F}_2^2$ the three lines spanned by $(1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$ and $(1, 1)$ cover all four vectors.

15. Put the $v_i$ as columns and row reduce: the pivot columns are the first and the third. So $v_1, v_3$ is a basis and $\dim W = 2$; indeed $v_2 = 2v_1$, $v_4 = v_1 + v_3$, $v_5 = v_1 - v_3$.

20. $(i, -1) = i \cdot (1, i)$. Over $\R$, write the vectors in real coordinates as $(1, 0, 0, 1)$ and $(0, 1, -1, 0)$, which are independent; $\C^2$ has dimension $4$ over $\R$.

22. If $\sum a_p \log p = 0$ with rational $a_p$, clear denominators to make the $a_p$ integers, and exponentiate: $\prod p^{a_p} = 1$. Unique factorization forces all $a_p = 0$. A space with infinitely many independent vectors is not finite-dimensional.

23. Take a union $V = U_1 \cup \cdots \cup U_n$ with $n$ minimal, pick $u \in U_1$ outside the other $U_i$ and $v \notin U_1$. The $\abs{F}$ vectors $v + tu$, $t \in F$, avoid $U_1$, and two of them lie in the same $U_i$ with $i \ge 2$, which puts $u$ in $U_i$.

24. Coordinates in a basis give a bijection $V \to F^n$. A finite field $K$ contains the field $\mathbb{F}_p$ generated by $1$, for some prime $p$, and $K$ is a finite-dimensional vector space over it.

25. Define $(a + bi)v = av + bJv$; this makes $V$ a complex vector space. If $b_1, \dots, b_m$ is a complex basis, then $b_1, Jb_1, \dots, b_m, Jb_m$ is a real basis, so $\dim V = 2m$. Alternatively, $(\det J)^2 = \det(-I) = (-1)^n$ forces $n$ to be even.

26. Write $u_j = \sum_i a_{ij} w_i$ for $j = 1, \dots, n + 1$. The system $\sum_j a_{ij} x_j = 0$, $i = 1, \dots, n$, has $n$ equations and $n + 1$ unknowns, so it has a nonzero solution $x$, and then $\sum_j x_j u_j = 0$.
