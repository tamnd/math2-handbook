## History

Hermann Grassmann worked with linear combinations, linear dependence and the dimension of a space generated by given elements in *Die lineale Ausdehnungslehre*, published in 1844, and again in a reworked *Ausdehnungslehre* of 1862. His books found few readers at the time. Giuseppe Peano read Grassmann and, in his *Calcolo geometrico secondo l'Ausdehnungslehre di H. Grassmann* of 1888, gave an axiomatic definition of a real vector space, which he called a linear system, close to the eight axioms used here. The axioms became standard in the twentieth century, when algebra and functional analysis needed a single language for spaces of numbers, of functions and of sequences.

The exchange lemma carries the name of Ernst Steinitz, who published it in 1913. In the same years the abstract notion of a field, which Steinitz studied in 1910, made it natural to allow any field of scalars, so that the same theorems hold over $\Q$, $\R$, $\C$ and the finite fields.

Georg Hamel, in a paper of 1905 in the *Mathematische Annalen*, used a well-ordering of the real numbers to prove that $\R$ has a basis over $\Q$, and used such a basis to build solutions of the functional equation $f(x + y) = f(x) + f(y)$ that are not continuous. The modern proof that every vector space has a basis uses Zorn's lemma, which Max Zorn published in 1935. Andreas Blass proved in 1984 that the converse holds as well: over the axioms of ZF, the statement that every vector space has a basis implies the axiom of choice.

## Further reading

- Sheldon Axler, *Linear Algebra Done Right*. Chapter 2 treats span, independence, bases and dimension for finite-dimensional spaces with short, careful proofs.
- Paul R. Halmos, *Finite-Dimensional Vector Spaces*. A classic short book that already treats vector spaces in the abstract style of this chapter, over a general field.
- Kenneth Hoffman and Ray Kunze, *Linear Algebra*. Chapter 2 develops vector spaces, bases and coordinates over an arbitrary field, with many worked computations.
- Stephen H. Friedberg, Arnold J. Insel and Lawrence E. Spence, *Linear Algebra*. Chapter 1 has many examples of spaces of functions and matrices and a large stock of exercises.
- Steven Roman, *Advanced Linear Algebra*. For spaces of infinite dimension, Hamel bases and the use of Zorn's lemma.
