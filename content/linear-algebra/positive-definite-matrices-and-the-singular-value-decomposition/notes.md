## History

Positive definite matrices grew out of the theory of quadratic forms in the nineteenth century, where the question was when a form in several variables takes only positive values. The test by leading principal minors carries the name of James Joseph Sylvester, who worked on quadratic forms and their invariants throughout his career. The factorization $A = L L^T$ was found by André-Louis Cholesky, a French military surveyor, for the least-squares problems of geodesy; it was published after his death, in 1924.

The singular value decomposition was found twice in the 1870s, by Eugenio Beltrami in 1873 and by Camille Jordan in 1874, both for real bilinear forms. Sylvester found it again for real square matrices in 1889. Léon Autonne introduced the polar decomposition of matrices in 1902 and used it in 1915 to obtain the SVD. The first proof for rectangular and complex matrices seems to be that of Carl Eckart and Gale Young in 1936.

In between, the decomposition appeared in analysis. Erhard Schmidt introduced singular values in 1907 for integral operators, calling them eigenvalues, and solved the problem of best approximation by operators of finite rank. The name "singular value" is first quoted in 1937, from Smithies. Eckart and Young rediscovered the approximation theorem for matrices in the same 1936 paper, and Leon Mirsky extended it to all unitarily invariant norms.

The generalized inverse has a similar history. Erik Ivar Fredholm used a pseudoinverse of integral operators in 1903. E. H. Moore described the matrix version in 1920, Arne Bjerhammar again in 1951, and Roger Penrose in 1955 characterized it by the four conditions that now carry his name. The SVD became a practical tool only after Gene Golub and William Kahan gave a stable way to compute it in 1965; since then it has been a standard step in statistics, signal processing and data analysis.

## Further reading

- Sheldon Axler, *Linear Algebra Done Right*, fourth edition (2024). Chapter 7 treats positive operators, square roots, the polar decomposition and the SVD from the side of operators on inner product spaces.
- Roger A. Horn and Charles R. Johnson, *Matrix Analysis*, second edition (2013). The standard reference for positive definite matrices (chapter 7), the SVD and its many inequalities.
- David C. Lay, Steven R. Lay and Judi J. McDonald, *Linear Algebra and Its Applications*, fifth edition (2016). A gentle account of quadratic forms, the SVD and its use in statistics, with many computed examples.
- Gilbert Strang, *Introduction to Linear Algebra*, sixth edition (2023). Places positive definite matrices and the SVD at the centre of applied linear algebra, with emphasis on the four fundamental subspaces.
- Lloyd N. Trefethen and David Bau III, *Numerical Linear Algebra* (1997). The SVD, Cholesky and least squares from the point of view of computation and stability.
