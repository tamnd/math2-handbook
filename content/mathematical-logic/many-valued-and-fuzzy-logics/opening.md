Give the sentence "there will be a sea battle tomorrow" the value $\tfrac12$, as Łukasiewicz did in 1920, and keep $\neg x = 1 - x$. Then $p \lor \neg p$ has the value $\max(\tfrac12, \tfrac12) = \tfrac12$, so excluded middle is no longer a law. Whether $p \to p$ survives depends on one table entry. Łukasiewicz set $\tfrac12 \to \tfrac12 = 1$, and $p \to p$ stays a tautology. Kleene, who used $\tfrac12$ for a computation that has not halted, set $\tfrac12 \to \tfrac12 = \tfrac12$, and his logic has no tautologies at all. Now take a whole interval of values. A man of height 1.78 m is tall to degree $0.6$, say, and a man of 1.77 m to degree $0.59$. Each step down the scale loses only a little truth, and the sorites paradox loses its force.

These are many-valued logics: the classical tables extended to more than two values, with a set of designated values that count as true. The chapter treats the general frame, the classical three- and four-valued systems, the logic of the unit interval and its algebras, fuzzy logics built from t-norms, and three neighbours where values are bookkeeping, probabilities or vectors.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in seven groups, read in order.

1. **Truth values and matrices.** A [many-valued logic](/DWYCKAKXZD/) is given by a [logical matrix](/P7E926JK13/): an algebra of truth tables with a set of designated values. Classical logic has a two-element matrix, and Suszko's reduction shows that "many-valued" is a property of the semantics, not of the logic alone.
2. **Three and four values.** [Łukasiewicz three-valued logic](/1CE2TGM6MQ/), [strong Kleene logic](/SZHA10A9X7/) with its dual LP, [weak Kleene logic](/BCK5QXQXA6/) with Bochvar's and Halldén's versions, and Belnap and Dunn's [first-degree entailment](/KXTP4P3GHT/) with the values "both" and "neither".
3. **Functional completeness.** [Post's many-valued logics](/YHYSGRE7JV/) define every function on $m$ values, and a single [Sheffer function](/1K829SJG3K/), such as Webb's, can do the same.
4. **Infinite-valued Łukasiewicz logic.** The logic [on the unit interval](/NFCEM44XYH/), its algebraic completeness by [Chang's theorem](/QM0F33QE9P/), and [McNaughton's theorem](/E11A9H8Z7Q/) on the functions its formulas define.
5. **Fuzzy logics from t-norms.** [Fuzzy logic](/8DH4QM6AX8/) in the narrow and the broad sense, [triangular norms](/VR6D8JXX1W/) and their residua, and the logics [BL](/3AQ5GHTXA4/), [MTL](/PT5PHKJDYM/) and [product logic](/YTHRDT9PDR/).
6. **Quantifiers and degrees.** [First-order Gödel logics](/H8C323CV3T/), graded consequence in [Pavelka logic](/YG62F8YC0P/), and the [arithmetical complexity of first-order Łukasiewicz logic](/VAHH1505Z0/).
7. **Games, probabilities and vectors.** The [Rényi–Ulam game with one lie](/402GXME26B/), [probability logic](/Z2WRGJDRQT/) and [vector logic](/DJ41X602BJ/).

## A first reading

Read [many-valued logic](/DWYCKAKXZD/), [logical matrix](/P7E926JK13/), [Łukasiewicz three-valued logic](/1CE2TGM6MQ/), [strong Kleene logic](/SZHA10A9X7/), [first-degree entailment](/KXTP4P3GHT/), [Łukasiewicz infinite-valued logic](/NFCEM44XYH/), [fuzzy logic](/8DH4QM6AX8/), [triangular norm](/VR6D8JXX1W/), [Hájek's basic logic BL](/3AQ5GHTXA4/) and [probability logic](/Z2WRGJDRQT/), in that order. Work the attached examples of the three-valued logics by hand: most later errors come from mixing up their tables or their designated sets.

On a first pass, skip Sheffer functions, McNaughton's theorem, MTL, first-order Gödel logics, Pavelka logic and the complexity theorem. They need more algebra or computability, and the rest of the chapter does not depend on them. The Rényi–Ulam game and vector logic can be read at any time after the three-valued logics.

## Before you start

You need [the propositional calculus](/JDK63589SA/): formulas, valuations, [tautologies](/05006AJ7DZ/) and Hilbert calculi with [modus ponens](/0Y0MQYST0M/). The [truth tables](/05RSK4Q657/) of Chapter 2.2 are the two-valued case of everything here, and [functional completeness](/TDZVCWYJ9F/) for two values is the model for group 3. For the algebraic side, know [Boolean algebras](/0XADJKM699/) and [lattices](/WH4SFAACAS/); Chapter 2.27 on [algebraic logic](/QYA4H5Y7VR/) treats MV-algebras and their relatives. [Heyting algebras](/NG943XNQWH/) and [Gödel–Dummett logic](/474SGS2N0X/) from Chapter 2.23 help with Gödel logic. Group 6 uses [first-order structures](/983GZBQYT4/) and the [arithmetical hierarchy](/7CVJCQKY41/).
