A function $f$ is continuous when, for every point $a$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$, there is a $\delta > 0$ such that for every $x$, if $|x - a| < \delta$ then $|f(x) - f(a)| < \varepsilon$. The sentence has four quantifiers, an implication and two inequalities, and the order of the parts matters. Move "for every point $a$" to just after "there is a $\delta$", so that one $\delta$ must serve all points, and the definition becomes a different one, uniform continuity. To prove that $f$ is not continuous at a point $a$, we negate the inner sentence, and the negation has the form "there is an $\varepsilon > 0$ such that for every $\delta > 0$ there is an $x$ with $|x - a| < \delta$ and $|f(x) - f(a)| \ge \varepsilon$". Every step of that negation follows a law of this chapter.

The chapter gives the small logical vocabulary that all of mathematics is written in: the connectives $\neg$, $\land$, $\lor$, $\to$, $\leftrightarrow$, the quantifiers $\forall$ and $\exists$, truth tables, the standard laws, and the rules of inference that proofs use. It treats them as working tools and checks each claim by a truth table or a short argument. The formal treatment, with axioms and derivations, comes in the next chapters.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in eight groups, read in order.

1. **Propositions and truth values.** A [proposition](/0ZGC7ZESYN/) has exactly one truth value, and [bivalence](/5DYNDW9526/) says there are only two. An open sentence such as $x > 3$ is not a proposition.
2. **The connectives.** [Negation](/029JBBF693/), [conjunction](/025YZN3VJW/), [disjunction](/0F72FK8EKB/), [exclusive or](/PTMR3P9J6C/), [material implication](/0NMG7W7Q11/) and the [biconditional](/0S6GMNJ62Y/), then the [connectives](/QS0XVAN9BG/) in general and the [Sheffer stroke](/1NNYWRN6B8/), which alone can express all of them.
3. **Truth tables, tautologies and equivalence.** The [truth table](/05RSK4Q657/) method, [tautologies](/05006AJ7DZ/), and [logical equivalence](/0JMR2HM2N2/) with the rule of replacement.
4. **The laws of propositional logic.** The [laws](/A9CSGZNBV5/) used to rewrite formulas, with separate entries on [excluded middle](/0RYYK8PG4S/), [noncontradiction](/MP4R7T546Z/), [double negation](/0NFAGWH84K/) and [De Morgan's laws](/09PKJKA6J0/).
5. **An implication and its relatives.** The [converse and inverse](/09A3N7ZN5G/), the [contrapositive](/04T1CT7212/), and [necessary and sufficient conditions](/09BJQQB3VS/).
6. **Rules of inference and fallacies.** [Modus ponens](/0Y0MQYST0M/), [modus tollens](/32AQPJQQAM/), [hypothetical syllogism](/7MT8CPCEM4/) and [disjunctive syllogism](/6EFJF9NAYX/), the [formal fallacies](/T5BG7Y4J0T/) that imitate them, and [vacuous truth](/0V4QXPGJQK/).
7. **Predicates and quantifiers.** [Predicates](/0QHEWQD30B/), the [universal](/0GHS47PDAQ/), [existential](/0MSRQHCNN9/) and [uniqueness](/0QAJHFGZZJ/) quantifiers, the negation of [universal](/0P3A06QNDP/) and [existential](/05SZQKH75K/) statements, and the [order of quantifiers](/0FYQMKFE77/).
8. **Putting statements into symbols.** [Translating into logical form](/WGZX7GV3PP/), and the [island of knights and knaves](/MVADXMFV59/), whose puzzles are an exercise in the biconditional.

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these twelve entries in order: [proposition](/0ZGC7ZESYN/), [negation](/029JBBF693/), [material implication](/0NMG7W7Q11/), [truth table](/05RSK4Q657/), [logical equivalence](/0JMR2HM2N2/), [De Morgan's laws](/09PKJKA6J0/), [contrapositive](/04T1CT7212/), [modus ponens](/0Y0MQYST0M/), [universal quantifier](/0GHS47PDAQ/), [existential quantifier](/0MSRQHCNN9/), [order of quantifiers](/0FYQMKFE77/) and [translating into logical form](/WGZX7GV3PP/). Conjunction and disjunction are short and can be read as they come. The remarks on intuitionistic logic in the entries on the laws, and the entries on bivalence, the Sheffer stroke and the uniqueness quantifier, can wait for a second reading.

## Before you start

Nothing from earlier chapters of logic is needed, and the first entries use only school mathematics. The examples use school algebra, even, odd and prime integers, and a few facts about [sets](/0BSJ7RQ9BW/); a few examples use limits or continuity and say so. [The language of mathematical statements](/WMY13MXY3W/) is a gentle companion that shows the same ideas in mathematical English, and [basic proof methods](/958EF46EYJ/) puts the rules of inference to work. The next topic is [the propositional calculus](/JDK63589SA/), which treats these connectives as a formal system.
