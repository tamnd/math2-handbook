Gödel showed in 1931, and Rosser sharpened in 1936, that every consistent theory with a computable set of axioms, strong enough to do some arithmetic, leaves a sentence of arithmetic neither proved nor refuted. The sentence he built says, through a coding of formulas by numbers, "I am not provable". He also showed that such a theory cannot prove its own consistency. The first result ends the hope of a complete axiom system for arithmetic. The second ends the hope, central to Hilbert's program, of proving the consistency of mathematics by means that the mathematics itself contains.

The proofs are less mysterious than the results. They rest on three ideas: syntax can be coded in arithmetic, the coded notion "provable in $T$" is a $\Sigma_1$ formula, and the diagonal lemma gives a sentence that says anything we like about its own code. The chapter builds these ideas, proves the two theorems and their relatives, and ends with what the theorems do and do not show.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in seven groups, read in order.

1. **Coding syntax in arithmetic.** [Gödel numbering](/MFJXPAF0R0/), the [arithmetization of syntax](/6M12VR1GT6/), the [provability predicate](/NB2BCZR40F/), the [derivability conditions](/F6H4STF9RG/) that it satisfies, and the [diagonal lemma](/3PRW76471J/).
2. **The first incompleteness theorem.** [$\omega$-consistency](/4S29X1GTV4/), [Gödel's first incompleteness theorem](/X9WYNYE29C/) with three proofs, [Rosser's theorem](/DH9RQ61GVD/), and [negation completeness](/319P85G2C1/).
3. **The second incompleteness theorem.** [Gödel's second incompleteness theorem](/4PZM5GJTR5/) and the [consistency statement](/4NBAX3VRQ7/) $\mathrm{Con}(T)$, whose exact form matters.
4. **Truth.** [Tarski's undefinability theorem](/YRV6SYK2BY/) and the [partial truth predicates](/Y2MF1EZAM7/) for $\Sigma_n$ sentences.
5. **Löb's theorem and self-reference.** [Löb's theorem](/3HD30YG3DG/) and the [Henkin sentence](/SBMQ5QHG9W/).
6. **Beyond consistency.** [Reflection principles](/NB485KFXKV/) and the [progressions of Turing and Feferman](/F7Z4BV2KHD/).
7. **Length of proofs, weak theories and meaning.** [Gödel's speed-up theorem](/37X5XBDBNX/), Willard's [self-verifying theories](/D8SGFVA3RT/), and the [significance of the incompleteness theorems](/PT61MFHC13/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these ten entries in order: [Gödel numbering](/MFJXPAF0R0/), [arithmetization of syntax](/6M12VR1GT6/), [the provability predicate](/NB2BCZR40F/), [the diagonal lemma](/3PRW76471J/), [the first incompleteness theorem](/X9WYNYE29C/), [Rosser's theorem](/DH9RQ61GVD/), [the derivability conditions](/F6H4STF9RG/), [the second incompleteness theorem](/4PZM5GJTR5/), [Tarski's undefinability theorem](/YRV6SYK2BY/) and [Löb's theorem](/3HD30YG3DG/). The derivability conditions come late in this list on purpose: they are needed only for the second theorem and Löb's theorem.

Read the [significance](/PT61MFHC13/) entry early if the theorems are new to you, since it corrects common misreadings. Reflection principles, the progressions, the speed-up theorem and self-verifying theories can wait.
