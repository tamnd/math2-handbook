[Modal logic](/C3RZY20ETK/) extends propositional logic with an operator $\square$, read "necessarily", and its dual $\lozenge = \neg\square\neg$, read "possibly". Other readings give other subjects: "it is known that", "it will always be the case that", "it is provable that", "after every run of the program". Each reading accepts some laws and rejects others. Knowledge is true, so $\square p \to p$ holds when $\square$ means "it is known that"; obligations can be broken, so it fails when $\square$ means "it is obligatory that". The chapter gives one mathematical frame for all these readings and the exact methods to compare, axiomatize and decide the logics they lead to.

The frame is [Kripke semantics](/NGHWZ9Y7ZV/). A frame is a set of worlds with an [accessibility relation](/XBCYFDPS2F/) $R$, and $\square\varphi$ is true at a world $w$ when $\varphi$ is true at every world $v$ with $w R v$. Laws then become properties of $R$. On the frame $(\N, <)$, where $\square$ means "at every later time", the axiom $\square p \to \square\square p$ is valid because $<$ is transitive, and $\square p \to p$ fails because $<$ is not reflexive. The completeness theorems go the other way: every formula valid on all transitive frames is derivable from the axiom $\square p \to \square\square p$. Filtration then shows that a non-theorem always fails in a finite model whose size is bounded by the formula, and so the standard logics are decidable.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in six groups, read in order.

1. **The modal language and Kripke semantics.** The language, frames and models, truth at a world, and the difference between validity in a model, on a frame and on a class of frames.
2. **Normal modal logics and their axioms.** A [normal modal logic](/D1TJYZZDCY/) contains $\square(p \to q) \to (\square p \to \square q)$ and is closed under the [necessitation rule](/G989VP2NGP/). The [common modal axiom schemata](/FDFQFWPM4X/) $\mathrm{T}$, $\mathrm{D}$, $\mathrm{B}$, $\mathrm{4}$ and $\mathrm{5}$ match reflexive, serial, symmetric, transitive and Euclidean relations.
3. **The standard systems.** [$\mathsf{T}$, $\mathsf{D}$ and $\mathsf{K4}$](/XVYMEZKH7R/), [$\mathsf{S4}$](/N60RVXSA5W/), [$\mathsf{S5}$](/VC3A2VWXND/) and the [Brouwerian logic](/2QPT4KCM0Q/) $\mathsf{B}$, how they are ordered, and how their strings of boxes and diamonds reduce. [Strict implication](/5JW54NP52K/) gives the history.
4. **Completeness, filtration and decidability.** The [canonical model](/TVE4F04KME/) proves completeness, [filtration](/6ZZSHAKG5F/) gives small countermodels, and the [finite model property](/3NT8RCA6HE/) gives decision procedures.
5. **Modal logic and first-order logic.** The [standard translation](/YW6YGBMVQY/) makes modal logic a fragment of first-order logic, and the [complexity of modal satisfiability](/1P2B0C7CQ4/) says how hard the logics are to decide.
6. **Neighbourhoods and several boxes.** [Neighbourhood semantics](/CN7HK9K1B6/) covers logics without axiom $\mathrm{K}$, and [multimodal logic](/ERG3KAQWYF/) has a box for each agent, direction of time or program.

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these entries in order: [modal logic](/C3RZY20ETK/), [Kripke semantics](/NGHWZ9Y7ZV/), [accessibility relation](/XBCYFDPS2F/), [normal modal logic](/D1TJYZZDCY/), [necessitation rule](/G989VP2NGP/), [common modal axiom schemata](/FDFQFWPM4X/), [modal logic S4](/N60RVXSA5W/), [modal logic S5](/VC3A2VWXND/), [canonical model](/TVE4F04KME/), [filtration](/6ZZSHAKG5F/) and [finite model property](/3NT8RCA6HE/). Work through the small countermodels in the attached examples with pencil and paper: most questions about modal logic are settled by drawing a frame of two or three worlds. The canonical model and filtration are the two methods of the chapter; the rest of the theory, here and in the next chapter, is built on them.

Strict implication, the complexity theorem, neighbourhood semantics and multimodal logic can wait for a second pass. The standard translation is worth reading before Chapter 2.21, which starts from it.

## Before you start

You need [the propositional calculus](/JDK63589SA/): [tautologies](/05006AJ7DZ/), [modus ponens](/0Y0MQYST0M/), the [Hilbert system](/1MGMQX43B8/) and the [completeness theorem for propositional logic](/81ZNY1XKYJ/). The canonical model follows the method of [Lindenbaum's lemma](/SC0BSJ3GT6/) and the [Henkin construction](/RY8KRKR097/) of Chapter 2.7. For the standard translation you need [first-order logic](/086QKPF0A6/) and [structures, satisfaction and truth](/NJDME6F845/), and a little [second-order logic](/G6X7SZSNC5/). The complexity theorem uses the classes NP and PSPACE. Chapter 2.16 reads the box as provability, and Chapter 2.23 links $\mathsf{S4}$ to [intuitionistic logic](/Q11N8X99FK/).
