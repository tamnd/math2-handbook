Usually one starts from axioms and proves theorems. [Reverse mathematics](/7SQS69T17R/) goes the other way: it starts from a theorem of ordinary mathematics and asks which axioms are needed to prove it. The setting is [second-order arithmetic](/DC60FYCSPM/), a theory with variables for natural numbers and for sets of natural numbers, in which real numbers, continuous functions and countable algebraic structures can all be coded. The program, begun by Harvey Friedman in the 1970s and developed by Stephen Simpson and many others, fixes a weak base theory and proves that a theorem is *equivalent* to a set-existence axiom over that base. Then the theorem needs exactly that axiom.

The base theory is $\mathrm{RCA}_0$, with comprehension only for computable sets and induction only for $\Sigma^0_1$ formulas; it corresponds to computable mathematics. A striking finding is that most theorems studied so far are equivalent to one of five systems, the *big five*: $\mathrm{RCA}_0$, $\mathrm{WKL}_0$, $\mathrm{ACA}_0$, $\mathrm{ATR}_0$ and $\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathrm{CA}_0$. For example, the [Heine–Borel theorem](/07PFYV65MB/) for $[0, 1]$, in its form for countable covers, is equivalent to $\mathrm{WKL}_0$, which adds weak König's lemma: every infinite binary tree has an infinite path. The [Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem](/0KDJD249DT/) is equivalent to $\mathrm{ACA}_0$, which adds comprehension for arithmetical formulas. The comparability of countable well-orderings is equivalent to $\mathrm{ATR}_0$.

Not everything fits. Ramsey's theorem for pairs and two colours lies outside the big five: Seetapun showed that it does not imply $\mathrm{ACA}_0$, and Jiayi Liu that it does not imply $\mathrm{WKL}_0$. Many such combinatorial principles sit between the main systems, and their mutual relations are a large part of current work.
