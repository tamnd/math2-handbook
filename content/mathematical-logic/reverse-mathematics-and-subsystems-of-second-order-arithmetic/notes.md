## History

Weyl's *Das Kontinuum* (1918) developed a large part of analysis with arithmetical definitions only, and is the first systematic piece of [predicative mathematics](/43BSXNWQBG/). Hilbert and Bernays showed in the second volume of *Grundlagen der Mathematik* (1939) that analysis can be formalized in [second-order arithmetic](/DC60FYCSPM/). Kreisel, Feferman and others studied subsystems of analysis in the 1950s and 1960s, and in 1964 and 1965 Feferman and Schütte independently identified $\Gamma_0$ as the limit of predicative provability.

Harvey Friedman began [reverse mathematics](/7SQS69T17R/) in the early 1970s and presented it at the International Congress of Mathematicians in Vancouver in 1974, in "Some systems of second order arithmetic and their use". He introduced the systems that became the big five and the idea of proving axioms from theorems. Stephen Simpson and his students then carried out the program for large parts of analysis, algebra and combinatorics. Simpson's paper "Partial realizations of Hilbert's program" (1988) drew the [foundational consequences](/Z26S516BKH/) of Friedman's result that $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ is $\Pi^0_2$-conservative over primitive recursive arithmetic, and Harrington proved its $\Pi^1_1$ conservation over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$.

The computability theory behind the models came largely from Jockusch. Jockusch and Soare proved the low basis theorem and its relatives for $\Pi^0_1$ classes in 1972, which give the $\omega$-models of [$\mathsf{WKL}_0$](/7W9EG02T79/). In "Ramsey's theorem and recursion theory" (1972), Jockusch showed that every computable coloring of pairs has an infinite $\Pi^0_2$ homogeneous set and that some has no $\Sigma^0_2$ one, and that some computable coloring of triples has only homogeneous sets that compute the halting set.

Seetapun and Slaman proved in "On the strength of Ramsey's theorem" (1995) that [$\mathrm{RT}^2_2$](/ZT5KV6KJ3E/) does not imply $\mathsf{ACA}_0$. Cholak, Jockusch and Slaman analyzed it further in "On the strength of Ramsey's theorem for pairs" (2001), splitting it into stable Ramsey's theorem and cohesiveness and proving that $\mathsf{RCA}_0 + \mathrm{I}\Sigma^0_2 + \mathrm{RT}^2_2$ is $\Pi^1_1$-conservative over $\mathsf{RCA}_0 + \mathrm{I}\Sigma^0_2$. Liu proved in 2012 that $\mathrm{RT}^2_2$ does not imply weak König's lemma. The study of these principles grew into the [zoo](/E3X5B175ZR/).

Kohlenbach proposed [higher-order reverse mathematics](/NPDRXTBW5W/) in a paper of 2005, with base theories in all finite types and functionals such as Kleene's quantifier as objects of study.

## Further reading

- Stephen G. Simpson, *Subsystems of Second Order Arithmetic* (second edition, Cambridge University Press, 2009). The standard reference, with the big five, their equivalents and their models in full detail.
- Denis R. Hirschfeldt, *Slicing the Truth: On the Computable and Reverse Mathematics of Combinatorial Principles* (World Scientific, 2014). An introduction to Ramsey's theorem for pairs and the principles of the zoo, with the computability theory they need.
- John Stillwell, *Reverse Mathematics: Proofs from the Inside Out* (Princeton University Press, 2018). A short introduction for readers with a background in analysis rather than logic.
- Damir D. Dzhafarov and Carl Mummert, *Reverse Mathematics: Problems, Reductions, and Proofs* (Springer, 2022). A modern textbook that combines reverse mathematics with Weihrauch reducibility and computable reductions.
