Propositional proof complexity asks how long proofs of propositional tautologies must be. Every tautology has a proof in any complete [propositional calculus](/09K9MWD2QQ/), and a [truth table](/05RSK4Q657/) check always works, but a truth table has $2^n$ rows for $n$ letters. The question is whether some proof system always has short proofs, of length polynomial in the size of the tautology.

Take the pigeonhole principle: $n + 1$ pigeons do not fit into $n$ holes, one pigeon to a hole. Written as a CNF in the variables $x_{ij}$, "pigeon $i$ sits in hole $j$", its negation has $(n+1)n$ variables and $(n+1) + n\binom{n+1}{2}$ clauses, so its size is polynomial in $n$. Armin Haken proved in 1985 that every [resolution](/ZDW6N1PGJ7/) refutation of it has exponentially many clauses, so every run of a DPLL or CDCL solver on it takes exponential time. Samuel Buss proved in 1987 that the same formulas have Frege proofs of polynomial size, because a Frege system can count. The two results put resolution and Frege systems on different levels, and much of the subject is a map of such levels: which systems simulate which, and which formulas separate them.

Stephen Cook and Robert Reckhow made the question precise in 1979. A proof system is a polynomial-time function whose values are exactly the tautologies, and it is polynomially bounded if every tautology has a proof of polynomial length. A polynomially bounded system exists exactly when NP equals [coNP](/NBSD5FG7NY/). So lower bounds for stronger and stronger systems are a route toward separating complexity classes, and in the end toward the [P versus NP](/MFN5GYZH5Z/) problem. No superpolynomial lower bound is known for Frege systems, and this is the central open problem of the field.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in five groups, read in order.

1. **Proof systems and the main question.** [Proof complexity](/1Q046J0AW2/) names the measures of proofs: size, number of lines, width and degree. The [definition of a propositional proof system](/3V24CYWDT6/) and of p-simulation follows Cook and Reckhow, and the [Cook-Reckhow theorem](/YS1T29B00B/) links the existence of short proofs with NP and coNP.
2. **Frege and extended Frege.** [Frege systems](/J2BAR09TPF/) are the Hilbert calculi, all p-equivalent by Reckhow's theorem. The [extended Frege system](/5YSE04SHFH/) may name formulas by new variables. The page on [lower bounds for Frege systems](/ANSV13M4FS/) describes the open problem and why the known methods fail.
3. **Resolution and its lower bounds.** [Resolution as a proof system](/6JSH907W5R/) in its tree-like, regular and general forms; [Haken's theorem](/4PCEYEW3JG/); the [size-width relation](/GWDGRPAQSM/), which turns lower bounds on width into lower bounds on size; and the two families of hard examples, the [Tseitin formulas](/JXTCC6KXNG/) and the [pigeonhole formulas](/RJ571F7FF0/).
4. **Between resolution and Frege.** [Bounded-depth Frege](/41QJ1WEMJ3/) and Ajtai's lower bound, [cutting planes](/KKCX2HQP2K/) with linear inequalities and rounding, and the [algebraic proof systems](/ZERBQSTZB5/), Nullstellensatz and polynomial calculus, measured by degree.
5. **Interpolation and proof search.** [Feasible interpolation](/59GJCM8C66/) reads a small circuit off a short refutation and so turns circuit lower bounds into proof lower bounds. [Automatizability](/WBECDG163W/) asks whether short proofs can be found in time polynomial in their size; for resolution the answer is no unless P = NP.

## A first reading

Take these entries in order: [proof complexity](/1Q046J0AW2/), [propositional proof system](/3V24CYWDT6/), [Cook-Reckhow theorem](/YS1T29B00B/), [Frege system](/J2BAR09TPF/), [resolution as a proof system](/6JSH907W5R/), [pigeonhole formulas](/RJ571F7FF0/), [Haken's theorem](/4PCEYEW3JG/), [size-width relation](/GWDGRPAQSM/), [Tseitin formulas](/JXTCC6KXNG/) and [cutting planes](/KKCX2HQP2K/). The attached examples are small and worth working by hand, in particular the tree-like refutation from a DPLL run and the cutting-planes refutation of the four clauses on two variables.

Extended Frege, bounded-depth Frege, the algebraic systems, feasible interpolation and automatizability can wait for a second pass. They lean on circuit complexity and on [bounded arithmetic](/RA380RWEC0/) more than the rest, and they read best once resolution and its lower bounds are familiar.

## Before you start

You need [the propositional calculus](/JDK63589SA/): formulas, [tautologies](/05006AJ7DZ/), the [Hilbert system](/1MGMQX43B8/), and [resolution](/ZDW6N1PGJ7/) with its [soundness and completeness](/DH1P95R390/). From the [theory of computation](/52TNMMSXD3/) you need polynomial time, the classes [NP](/SBDQH0TJ5X/) and coNP, and Boolean circuits. [Craig interpolation for propositional logic](/TRS2HY57EG/) is the starting point of feasible interpolation, and [satisfiability](/QN7M1JCGT9/) and [SAT solving](/3BW08M1EX7/) give the algorithmic side. The remarks on logic refer to [bounded arithmetic](/RA380RWEC0/), the theories of arithmetic that correspond to these proof systems.
