## History

Brouwer rejected the unrestricted law of excluded middle in his writings from 1907 and 1908, on the ground that a mathematical statement is true only when it has been proved by a mental construction. He did not formalize the logic his mathematics used. Glivenko proved in 1929 that a propositional formula is a classical tautology exactly when its double negation is intuitionistically valid, and Heyting published formal rules for [intuitionistic logic](/Q11N8X99FK/) in 1930. Kolmogorov's 1932 reading of formulas as problems, and Heyting's reading of them as proofs, became the BHK interpretation. Johansson's minimal calculus of 1937 dropped the principle that a contradiction implies everything.

Gödel showed in his 1932 note that no finite matrix characterizes intuitionistic propositional logic, and that infinitely many logics lie between it and classical logic. In 1933 he embedded it into a modal logic now called $\mathsf{S4}$, and McKinsey and Tarski proved in 1948 that the embedding is faithful. Gentzen's calculi $\mathrm{NJ}$ and $\mathrm{LJ}$ of 1934 and 1935 gave the disjunction property a proof through cut elimination. Tarski's 1938 paper "Der Aussagenkalkül und die Topologie" interpreted formulas as open sets, and the algebraic semantics in Heyting algebras grew from it and from the work of Stone. Beth's trees and Kripke's 1965 semantics of partially ordered worlds made countermodels easy to draw.

The study of intermediate logics as a family began in the 1950s. Dummett studied the logic $\mathsf{LC}$ in 1959, following Gödel's infinite matrix, and Kreisel and Putnam isolated their axiom as a principle that keeps the disjunction property. Rieger and Nishimura described all formulas in one variable. Kleene introduced the slash in 1962 and 1963, and Medvedev his logic of finite problems in papers from 1962 on. Jankov introduced characteristic formulas, studied $\mathsf{KC}$, and proved in 1968 that there are continuum many intermediate logics. Maksimova proved in 1977 that exactly seven consistent intermediate logics have the Craig interpolation property.

Admissible rules became a subject of their own after Harrop's observation of 1960. Rybakov's 1997 monograph proved that admissibility in $\mathsf{IPC}$ is decidable and has no finite basis, and Iemhoff proved in 2001, building on Ghilardi's 1999 work on unification, that the Visser rules form a basis. On another line, Lorenzen proposed dialogue games in the late 1950s, and Felscher's 1985 paper "Dialogues as a foundation for intuitionistic logic" gave the first careful proof that they capture intuitionistic validity. Intuitionistic modal logics were studied by Fischer Servi from the late 1970s, by Plotkin and Stirling in 1986, and in Simpson's 1994 thesis.

## Further reading

- D. van Dalen, *Logic and Structure*, 5th ed., Springer, 2013. Its chapters on intuitionistic logic give natural deduction, Kripke models and completeness at the level of this chapter.
- A. S. Troelstra and D. van Dalen, *Constructivism in Mathematics: An Introduction*, two volumes, North-Holland, 1988. The standard reference for the logic, its semantics and its metatheorems, with the predicate case in full.
- M. Dummett, *Elements of Intuitionism*, 2nd ed., Oxford University Press, 2000. A philosophical and technical account of intuitionistic logic with Beth and Kripke semantics.
- A. Chagrov and M. Zakharyaschev, *Modal Logic*, Oxford University Press, 1997. Covers intermediate logics together with modal logics, including Jankov formulas, canonical formulas and the Gödel–McKinsey–Tarski translation.
- V. V. Rybakov, *Admissibility of Logical Inference Rules*, Elsevier, 1997. The monograph on admissible rules in intuitionistic and modal logics.
