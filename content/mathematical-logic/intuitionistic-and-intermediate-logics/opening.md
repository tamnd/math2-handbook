A classical proof that there are irrational numbers $a$ and $b$ with $a^b$ rational runs as follows. If $\sqrt2^{\sqrt2}$ is rational, take $a = b = \sqrt2$; otherwise take $a = \sqrt2^{\sqrt2}$ and $b = \sqrt2$, so that $a^b = 2$. The proof uses the [law of excluded middle](/0RYYK8PG4S/) and does not say which pair works. [Intuitionistic logic](/Q11N8X99FK/) is the logic of proofs that do say. It grew from L. E. J. Brouwer's view that a statement is true only when there is a construction that proves it, and Arend Heyting gave it axioms in 1930. On the [Brouwer–Heyting–Kolmogorov reading](/7B53ZGXXEQ/), a proof of $\varphi \vee \psi$ is a proof of one of the disjuncts together with the information which one, and a proof of $\varphi \to \psi$ is a method that turns proofs of $\varphi$ into proofs of $\psi$.

Intuitionistic logic is weaker than classical logic, but it is not a fragment that loses information. By [Glivenko's theorem](/B9M3P61ZVA/), a propositional formula is a classical tautology exactly when its double negation is intuitionistically provable, so $\neg\neg(p \vee \neg p)$ is a theorem although $p \vee \neg p$ is not. In exchange the logic has the [disjunction property](/YTRVX6F3FX/): if $\varphi \vee \psi$ is provable, one of the disjuncts is. Through the [Gödel–McKinsey–Tarski translation](/BXVNEN55MR/) it is a part of the modal logic $\mathrm{S4}$, and through the [Curry–Howard correspondence](/A77FER1Q5H/) its proofs are typed programs.

Between intuitionistic and classical propositional logic lie the intermediate logics. The two-world model $u \le v$ with $p$ true at $v$ only refutes $p \vee \neg p$ at $u$, The axiom $(p \to q) \vee (q \to p)$ is valid exactly on the frames in which the worlds above each world form a chain, and adding it gives the Gödel–Dummett logic $\mathrm{LC}$. There are continuum many intermediate logics, and only seven of them have Craig interpolation.

## What the chapter covers

1. **The logic.** The Hilbert system of [intuitionistic logic](/Q11N8X99FK/) and its basic laws, and [minimal logic](/V7MTF4P01E/), which has no rule for $\bot$ at all.
2. **Four semantics.** [Kripke semantics](/5GTQ69W47T/) on partial orders, [Beth semantics](/GPC8Y4ZCSA/) on trees with bars, the [topological semantics](/RKFTMQNWRC/) of open sets, with Tarski's completeness for the real line, and the [Heyting algebra semantics](/JM1XJXWZYH/).
3. **Metatheorems.** The [disjunction and existence properties](/YTRVX6F3FX/), [Glivenko's theorem](/B9M3P61ZVA/), [Gödel's theorem on finite matrices](/CFCPJA1QF5/), [intuitionistic predicate logic](/MW0RVDRFTA/) with growing domains, the [Kleene slash](/YRRKEEHNCD/) and [admissible rules](/C5K35H0R4Y/).
4. **Intermediate logics.** The [lattice of intermediate logics](/H5NSKEP3P2/), [Gödel–Dummett logic](/FA88H7FDPY/), the [weak excluded middle](/CX032ACTMZ/) and $\mathrm{KC}$, [Kreisel–Putnam logic](/X0C1NC7FXX/) and [Medvedev's logic](/R2FD01J3PC/), whose decidability is an open problem.
5. **Tools for the lattice.** [Jankov formulas](/CVGBEFQG0Q/), the [Rieger–Nishimura lattice](/KAB6KM98ZZ/) of one-variable formulas and [Maksimova's interpolation theorem](/M3RTNGG3J5/).
6. **Modalities and dialogues.** [Intuitionistic modal logic](/J57YRDWX3G/) and [dialogical logic](/ZCEBYYP67J/).

## A first reading

Take these entries in order: [intuitionistic logic](/Q11N8X99FK/), [Kripke semantics](/5GTQ69W47T/), [Heyting algebra semantics](/JM1XJXWZYH/), the [disjunction and existence properties](/YTRVX6F3FX/), [Glivenko's theorem](/B9M3P61ZVA/), [intuitionistic predicate logic](/MW0RVDRFTA/), [intermediate logic](/H5NSKEP3P2/), [Gödel–Dummett logic](/FA88H7FDPY/) and the [weak excluded middle](/CX032ACTMZ/). Kripke models are the working tool of the whole chapter: draw the two-world chain and the fork with three worlds, and check every law of the first entry on them.

Beth and topological semantics, Gödel's theorem on finite matrices, the slash and admissible rules can wait for a second pass. Medvedev's logic, Jankov formulas, the Rieger–Nishimura lattice and Maksimova's theorem are for readers who want to study the lattice of logics itself. The last two entries are independent of the rest after the first group.

## Before you start

You need [classical propositional logic](/JDK63589SA/), the [law of excluded middle](/0RYYK8PG4S/), the [double negation law](/0NFAGWH84K/) and [Peirce's law](/GMSZ75D5WE/), which intuitionistic logic rejects. Natural deduction and the sequent calculus from [proof systems for first-order logic](/TTCRX3NFNY/) help with derivations, and [cut elimination](/AYRZ98Y0NW/) gives the first proof of the disjunction property. From modal logic you need [Kripke semantics](/NGHWZ9Y7ZV/) and [$\mathrm{S4}$](/N60RVXSA5W/), and the [canonical model](/TVE4F04KME/) and [filtration](/6ZZSHAKG5F/) methods return here. For arithmetic, the [double-negation translation](/HGJVM49PA4/) extends Glivenko's theorem to first-order theories.
