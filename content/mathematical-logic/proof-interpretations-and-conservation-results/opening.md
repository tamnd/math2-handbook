Euclid's proof that there are infinitely many primes is a proof by contradiction: if $p_1, \dots, p_k$ were all the primes, a prime factor of $p_1 \cdots p_k + 1$ would be a new one. In [Peano arithmetic](/0QFT7Z76N5/) the theorem reads $\forall n\, \exists p\, (p > n \wedge p \text{ is prime})$. Read with care, the proof gives more than existence: some prime lies in the interval $n < p \le n! + 1$, so a search below $n! + 1$ finds one. This is a small case of a general fact. A proof of a $\forall \exists$ sentence in a classical theory of arithmetic contains a bound or an algorithm for the witness, and the theorems of this chapter show how to read it off.

A proof interpretation translates the formulas of one theory into formulas of another, and its proofs into proofs, so that what one theory proves the other proves in translation. The Gödel–Gentzen [double-negation translation](/HGJVM49PA4/) puts $\neg\neg$ in front of chosen subformulas, so that a formula is classically provable exactly when its translation is intuitionistically provable. It follows that Peano arithmetic is consistent if [Heyting arithmetic](/CK8ZQRN183/) is, since the translation of $\bot$ is $\bot$. Gödel's [Dialectica interpretation](/HZ17ES9PDY/) of 1958 sends each formula $A$ of Heyting arithmetic to a formula $\exists x\, \forall y\, A_D(x, y)$, and each proof of $A$ to a term of his [system T](/A4WR995XZP/) that computes the witness $x$. For $\forall n\, \exists m\, (n < m)$ the witness is the successor function. For a formula with more quantifier alternations, the witness is a functional of higher type that answers every challenge $y$.

Some classical theorems have no computable witness at all. Every bounded monotone sequence of rationals converges, yet a computable sequence of this kind can fail to have a computable rate of convergence. The [no-counterexample interpretation](/4BCD1EM542/) still finds finite content in such a theorem: for each $\varepsilon > 0$ and each function $g$, an $n$ below an explicit bound with $\abs{a_i - a_j} \le \varepsilon$ for all $i, j \in [n, n + g(n)]$. [Proof mining](/XR1JXQG1CD/) uses this form, called metastability, to extract explicit bounds from proofs in analysis.

The second theme is conservation. A [conservative extension](/N238J43XNE/) proves no new sentences of a given class. Peano arithmetic proves the same $\Pi^0_2$ sentences as Heyting arithmetic, by [Friedman's A-translation](/6CCK0TT69P/). The fragment $\mathrm{I}\Sigma_1$ proves the same $\Pi^0_2$ sentences as [primitive recursive arithmetic](/WCXH72T8D8/), by [Parsons's theorem](/2VYJXZZDGB/), so a proof in $\mathrm{I}\Sigma_1$ that a function is total gives a primitive recursive witness. The system $\mathsf{WKL}_0$ of second-order arithmetic is conservative over $\mathsf{PRA}$ for $\Pi^0_2$ sentences, which shows that a good part of analysis is finitistically justified in the sense of [Hilbert's program](/P1RW6JWX73/).

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in four groups, read in order.

1. **Negative translations.** The [double-negation translation](/HGJVM49PA4/) embeds classical logic into intuitionistic logic, and [Friedman's A-translation](/6CCK0TT69P/) removes the double negation in front of a $\Pi^0_2$ conclusion.
2. **Functional interpretations and proof mining.** [Gödel's system T](/A4WR995XZP/) of primitive recursive functionals, the [Dialectica interpretation](/HZ17ES9PDY/) of Heyting arithmetic in T, Kreisel's [no-counterexample interpretation](/4BCD1EM542/) of Peano arithmetic, Spector's [bar recursion](/8CCG6F2FAT/) for analysis, and the extraction of bounds in [proof mining](/XR1JXQG1CD/).
3. **Conservation theorems.** The [general picture](/TNZ995J8AP/), with model-theoretic, proof-theoretic and interpretation methods, and [Parsons's theorem](/2VYJXZZDGB/) that $\mathrm{I}\Sigma_1$ is $\Pi^0_2$-conservative over $\mathsf{PRA}$.
4. **Interpretations between theories and Herbrand analysis.** [Interpretability between theories](/8H03GRMDYG/), its link to consistency and to $\Pi_1$ conservativity, and [Herbrand analysis](/MESZ87G9ET/), which reads primitive recursive witnesses off proofs in $\mathrm{I}\Sigma_1$.

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these entries in order: [double-negation translation](/HGJVM49PA4/), [Friedman's A-translation](/6CCK0TT69P/), [Gödel's system T](/A4WR995XZP/), [Dialectica interpretation](/HZ17ES9PDY/), [no-counterexample interpretation](/4BCD1EM542/), [conservation theorems](/TNZ995J8AP/), [Parsons's theorem](/2VYJXZZDGB/) and [interpretability between theories](/8H03GRMDYG/). The A-translation is short, and it is the quickest way to see how a classical proof yields a witness. Work through its attached example on Markov's rule before the Dialectica interpretation.

[Bar recursion](/8CCG6F2FAT/) can wait for a second pass; it needs the Dialectica interpretation and some comfort with functionals of type $(\N \to \N) \to \N$. [Proof mining](/XR1JXQG1CD/) and [Herbrand analysis](/MESZ87G9ET/) are easier once the interpretations are familiar, and Herbrand analysis also needs partial cut elimination.

## Before you start

You need [intuitionistic logic](/Q11N8X99FK/) and natural deduction, the [fragments of Peano arithmetic](/T70PYM1F36/) and [primitive recursive functions](/5NVA2JRHPV/), all from the chapter on [formal arithmetic and its fragments](/QHCRN4JVG0/). The chapter on [structural proof theory](/R10RMQCJQX/) supplies the [sequent calculus](/W72CA3R9MP/) and the [cut-elimination theorem](/AYRZ98Y0NW/), and [Herbrand's theorem](/VZ21F10WRS/) is the oldest result that extracts witnesses from a proof. For system T you need the [simply typed lambda calculus](/2DP940895E/), and the chapter on [type theory and constructive mathematics](/K6P3NSQEZ3/) gives [realizability](/4H35VQ16DC/), a close relative of the Dialectica interpretation. The [relative consistency proof](/X3G0RG2GPZ/) of the background chapter already uses an interpretation.
