Gödel's [first incompleteness theorem](/X9WYNYE29C/) gives a true sentence that [Peano arithmetic](/HACXBCM7SS/) does not prove, but the sentence is built for the purpose: it speaks about its own provability. For decades it was possible to hope that every question an ordinary mathematician asks about the natural numbers is settled by $\mathsf{PA}$. In 1977 Jeff Paris and Leo Harrington ended that hope. The [Paris–Harrington theorem](/509ZWA0R26/) is a variant of the finite Ramsey theorem in which the homogeneous set must be *large*, with at least as many elements as its least element. It is true, and $\mathsf{PA}$ does not prove it.

Other examples followed. [Goodstein's theorem](/XQMNJF241J/) says that every Goodstein sequence reaches $0$, and Laurie Kirby and Paris showed in 1982 that $\mathsf{PA}$ cannot prove it. Harvey Friedman showed that a finite form of [Kruskal's tree theorem](/XVHKCX4F5Y/) is not provable even in the much stronger system $\mathrm{ATR}_0$. The [graph minor theorem](/WP3GDNWJJW/) of Robertson and Seymour reaches higher still.

What these results share is growth. Each statement implies that some function is total, and that function grows faster than every function the theory can prove total. Hierarchies of fast-growing functions, indexed by ordinals, make this exact. The first step beyond the primitive recursive functions is the [Ackermann function](/N470JF7ZJX/), which [is not primitive recursive](/97QF3KHSBY/). For $\mathsf{PA}$ the measure is the ordinal [$\varepsilon_0$](/KT50SMH3EC/): every function that $\mathsf{PA}$ proves total is eventually dominated by a level $F_\alpha$ of the fast-growing hierarchy with $\alpha < \varepsilon_0$, and each of these levels is provably total in $\mathsf{PA}$.

## What to read meanwhile

Read [Peano arithmetic](/HACXBCM7SS/) and the [fragments of Peano arithmetic](/T70PYM1F36/) in Chapter 2.10, and Gödel's [first](/X9WYNYE29C/) and [second](/4PZM5GJTR5/) incompleteness theorems in Chapter 2.11: the proofs that $\mathsf{PA}$ cannot prove the Paris–Harrington and Goodstein theorems show that each of them, added to $\mathsf{PA}$, proves the consistency of $\mathsf{PA}$. The ordinal side is in [ordinal numbers](/0YSCSX5YZC/), [transfinite induction](/0ZHQMQ2C1N/) and [Gentzen's consistency proof](/QPV0HNP22S/). The combinatorics starts from [Ramsey's theorem](/00TQV3F5S4/) and the [strengthened finite Ramsey theorem](/4P2BAT6YC3/).
