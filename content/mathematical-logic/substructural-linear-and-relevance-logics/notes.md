## History

Gerhard Gentzen set out the sequent calculi LK and LJ in 1934–35, in "Untersuchungen über das logische Schließen", and he separated the structural rules from the rules for the connectives. Doubts about weakening are older. Ivan Orlov gave in 1928 an axiom system that is now read as the implication–negation fragment of $\mathbf{R}$. Moh Shaw-Kwei in 1950 and Alonzo Church in 1951 studied implication without weakening, and Wilhelm Ackermann proposed his "strenge Implikation" in 1956. Alan Ross Anderson and Nuel Belnap built the systems $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{R}$ from this work and presented them in *Entailment: The Logic of Relevance and Necessity*, whose first volume appeared in 1975 and whose second, with J. Michael Dunn among the authors, in 1992.

A semantics came in the early 1970s. Alasdair Urquhart gave a semilattice semantics in 1972, and Richard and Val Routley introduced the star operation for negation in the same year. Richard Routley and Robert K. Meyer then gave the ternary semantics, published in 1973 as "The semantics of entailment". Urquhart proved in 1984, in "The undecidability of entailment and relevant implication", that $\mathbf{R}$, $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{T}$ are undecidable, with a coding based on von Neumann's coordinatization of modular lattices.

Joachim Lambek published "The mathematics of sentence structure" in the *American Mathematical Monthly* in 1958. It turned the categorial grammar of Kazimierz Ajdukiewicz and Yehoshua Bar-Hillel into a sequent calculus with no structural rule and proved cut elimination for it. Mati Pentus proved in 1993 that Lambek grammars generate only context-free languages, and in 2006 that derivability is NP-complete. On the side of set theory, V. N. Grišin observed in the 1970s that naive comprehension does not lead to Russell's paradox over a logic without contraction. Kosta Došen proposed the name "substructural logics" at a conference in Tübingen in October 1990, and the proceedings appeared as *Substructural Logics* in 1993, edited by Peter Schroeder-Heister and Došen.

Jean-Yves Girard introduced linear logic in "Linear logic", *Theoretical Computer Science* 50 (1987). That paper already contains phase semantics, coherence spaces and proof nets, which he had found by studying the semantics of System F. Vincent Danos and Laurent Regnier gave their correctness criterion in 1989, in "The structure of multiplicatives". Patrick Lincoln, John Mitchell, Andre Scedrov and Natarajan Shankar proved in 1992 that full propositional linear logic is undecidable and MALL is PSPACE-complete, and Max Kanovich proved in the same year that MLL is NP-complete. A. P. Kopylov showed in 1995 that affine linear logic is decidable. Girard's "Light linear logic" appeared in 1998, Yves Lafont's "Soft linear logic and polynomial time" in 2004, and Girard's "Locus Solum", the paper on ludics, in 2001.

Robert K. Meyer and John Slaney introduced abelian logic in 1989. Peter O'Hearn and David Pym introduced the logic of bunched implications in "The logic of bunched implications" in 1999, and it became the assertion language of separation logic.

## Further reading

- Greg Restall, *An Introduction to Substructural Logics* (2000). A textbook account of the whole family, through sequent calculi, Hilbert systems, algebras and frame semantics.
- Nikolaos Galatos, Peter Jipsen, Tomasz Kowalski and Hiroakira Ono, *Residuated Lattices: An Algebraic Glimpse at Substructural Logics* (2007). The reference for $\mathsf{FL}$, its extensions and their varieties of residuated lattices.
- Jean-Yves Girard, Yves Lafont and Paul Taylor, *Proofs and Types* (1989). A short book on System F, coherence spaces and the first ideas of linear logic.
- A. S. Troelstra, *Lectures on Linear Logic* (1992). A careful account of the sequent calculus, proof nets and the semantics of linear logic.
- Alan Ross Anderson and Nuel D. Belnap, *Entailment: The Logic of Relevance and Necessity*, volume 1 (1975). The founding text of relevance logic, with the systems $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{R}$ and their natural deduction with indices.
- Edwin D. Mares, *Relevant Logic: A Philosophical Interpretation* (2004). The motives and the Routley–Meyer semantics of relevance logic, read as a theory of information.
