## What to remember

- **The three structural rules.** [Weakening](/VHRXX0T5XT/) adds a hypothesis, [contraction](/V0MZVC2XA9/) merges two copies of one, and [exchange](/YAFD18WYJ8/) swaps two. In Hilbert style they are $A \to (B \to A)$, $(A \to (A \to B)) \to (A \to B)$ and $(A \to (B \to C)) \to (B \to (A \to C))$. On a residuated lattice they are $x \le 1$, $x \le x \cdot x$ and $x \cdot y = y \cdot x$.
- **The base.** The [full Lambek calculus](/V5XVVN16GS/) $\mathsf{FL}$ has none of the three rules, and its algebras are residuated lattices with a constant $0$. Without exchange, implication splits into $\backslash$ and $/$; with all three rules, $\mathsf{FL}$ becomes intuitionistic logic.
- **The Lambek calculus** $\mathsf{L}$ has nonempty antecedents and reads formulas as types of words. The atom count is a necessary test for derivability, not a sufficient one. Pentus proved that every language generated by a Lambek grammar is context-free, and that derivability is NP-complete.
- **Linear logic** splits each connective in two: the multiplicatives $\otimes$ and $\mathbin{\wp}$ split the context, and the additives $\mathbin{\&}$ and $\oplus$ copy it or choose. Weakening and contraction hold only for formulas $?A$. The Girard translation $(A \to B)^\circ = {!A^\circ} \multimap B^\circ$ embeds intuitionistic implication.
- **Three semantics.** A [proof net](/HN6WN1VE39/) is a proof structure whose correction graphs are all trees, by the Danos–Regnier criterion. In [phase semantics](/64MHHQQ6G6/) formulas are facts $X = X^{\perp\perp}$ of a commutative monoid, and $\vdash A^\perp, B$ is valid when $A^* \subseteq B^*$. In [coherence spaces](/WSGZ5HGK1X/) proofs are cliques and maps are stable functions, linear when every set in the trace has exactly one token.
- **Complexity.** By the [decidability theorem](/BNRWJB2R8C/), MLL is NP-complete, MALL is PSPACE-complete and full linear logic is undecidable. [Light linear logics](/E7JVJSSWBT/) restrict the exponentials: ELL captures the elementary functions, LLL and SLL the polynomial-time ones.
- **Relevance.** A [relevance logic](/KXTCZ2FY5M/) proves $A \rightarrow B$ only if $A$ and $B$ share a variable. In a [Routley–Meyer model](/H4Z8J0KMAY/), $A \rightarrow B$ is true at $a$ when $Rabc$ and $A$ true at $b$ give $B$ true at $c$, and $\neg A$ is true at $a$ when $A$ fails at $a^*$. By [Urquhart's theorem](/X7F69S7QPX/), $\mathbf{R}$, $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{T}$ are undecidable.
- **Other members.** [Affine logic](/K67C2N2QAH/) is linear logic with weakening, and it is decidable. [Bunched logic](/5JKHNK7AR5/) has an intuitionistic pair $\wedge, \to$ and a multiplicative pair $\ast, \mathrel{-\!\!*}$. [Abelian logic](/W3CPQAFQJ3/) reads $A \to B$ as $v(B) - v(A)$ and proves $((A \to B) \to B) \to A$, which classical logic does not prove.

## Structural rules and decision problems

| Logic | Exchange | Weakening | Contraction | Provability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Lambek calculus](/5FAC9KX4H0/) $\mathsf{L}$ | no | no | no | NP-complete |
| MLL, multiplicative [linear logic](/2P2184B6HE/) | yes | no | no | NP-complete |
| MALL, with the additives | yes | no | no | PSPACE-complete |
| Full linear logic | yes | for $?A$ only | for $?A$ only | undecidable |
| [Affine linear logic](/YREXK6X3RP/) | yes | yes | for $?A$ only | decidable |

| [Intuitionistic logic](/Q11N8X99FK/) | yes | yes | yes | decidable |

## Common errors

- **Reading $\otimes$ and $\mathbin{\&}$ as the same "and".** $\vdash A^\perp, A \mathbin{\&} A$ is derivable, but $\vdash A^\perp, A \otimes A$ is not. $A \otimes B$ gives both resources, and $A \mathbin{\&} B$ gives one of them, of your choice.
- **Treating the count check as a proof.** In the Lambek calculus, $n, np/n \Rightarrow np$ passes the atom count and is not derivable. Only a derivation or a cut-free search settles the question.
- **Taking variable sharing as a definition of relevance logic.** It is a necessary test. The formula $p \rightarrow (q \rightarrow p)$ passes it, and $\mathbf{R}$ still rejects it; a Routley–Meyer model is needed to show this.
- **Validity at every point.** A Routley–Meyer model validates a formula when it is true at the base point $0$; even $q \rightarrow q$ can fail at other points.
- **Calling affine logic a fragment of linear logic.** It adds a rule, so it proves more: $A \otimes B \multimap A$ is derivable in affine logic and not in linear logic.
- **Forgetting the exponentials.** $!A \multimap A \otimes A$ is derivable in linear logic; only $A \multimap A \otimes A$ fails.
- **Confusing the two conjunctions of bunched logic.** A resource forces $p \wedge q$ when it forces both; it forces $p \ast q$ when it splits into a part for $p$ and a separate part for $q$.
