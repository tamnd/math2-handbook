## History

George Boole's *The Mathematical Analysis of Logic* (1847) and *An Investigation of the Laws of Thought* (1854) treated logic as a calculus of equations. Augustus De Morgan, Charles S. Peirce and Ernst Schröder extended the calculus to relations in the second half of the nineteenth century, and Schröder collected the work in his *Vorlesungen über die Algebra der Logik*. In the 1920s Adolf Lindenbaum used the algebra of formulas modulo provable equivalence in the logic seminar of Jan Łukasiewicz in Warsaw, and Alfred Tarski developed the method. Marshall Stone's paper "The theory of representations for Boolean algebras" (1936) showed that every Boolean algebra is isomorphic to an algebra of sets.

The 1940s brought the first algebras for non-classical logics and for relations. Tarski axiomatized relation algebras in "On the calculus of relations" (1941). J. C. C. McKinsey and Tarski studied closure algebras in "The algebra of topology" (1944), and their later work connected these algebras with the modal logic $\mathrm{S4}$. Bjarni Jónsson and Tarski published "Boolean algebras with operators" in two parts (1951 and 1952), with the representation of each such algebra in the complex algebra of a relational structure; its link with Kripke frames was noticed only later. Roger Lyndon found the first non-representable relation algebras in 1950.

C. C. Chang introduced MV-algebras in "Algebraic analysis of many valued logics" (1958) and used them for an algebraic proof that Łukasiewicz logic is complete for the unit interval. Daniele Mundici proved the equivalence of MV-algebras with lattice-ordered abelian groups with a strong unit in 1986. Petr Hájek introduced basic logic and BL-algebras in *Metamathematics of Fuzzy Logic* (1998). Gr. C. Moisil studied his algebras from about 1940, David Nelson proposed constructive logic with strong negation in "Constructible falsity" (1949), and Helena Rasiowa developed the algebras of non-classical logics in Warsaw.

For first-order logic, Tarski introduced cylindric algebras around 1950, and Paul Halmos introduced polyadic algebras in the 1950s; Halmos collected his papers in *Algebraic Logic* (1962). Leon Henkin, J. Donald Monk and Tarski wrote *Cylindric Algebras*, Part I (1971) and Part II (1985). Monk proved that the representable relation algebras have no finite axiomatization (1964), and he proved the same for the representable cylindric algebras of each finite dimension at least $3$ (1969). Tarski and Steven Givant showed in *A Formalization of Set Theory without Variables* (1987) that set theory can be expressed in an equational calculus of relations.

Abstract algebraic logic took its present form in the 1980s. Roman Suszko argued in the 1970s that every logic is two-valued in the sense of his reduction. Janusz Czelakowski studied equivalential logics, and W. J. Blok and Don Pigozzi defined protoalgebraic and algebraizable logics; their memoir *Algebraizable Logics* (1989) proved the main theorems. Larisa Maksimova's theorem of 1977 on interpolation and amalgamation became the model for the bridge theorems, and Josep Maria Font and Ramon Jansana extended the theory beyond the protoalgebraic logics in 1996.

## Further reading

- Paul Halmos and Steven Givant, *Logic as Algebra* (1998). A short introduction to propositional logic through Boolean algebras and to the algebraic view of quantifiers.
- Helena Rasiowa and Roman Sikorski, *The Mathematics of Metamathematics* (1963). The classical account of algebraic semantics for classical, intuitionistic and modal logics, with Lindenbaum–Tarski algebras throughout.
- Roberto Cignoli, Itala D'Ottaviano and Daniele Mundici, *Algebraic Foundations of Many-valued Reasoning* (2000). MV-algebras, Chang's completeness theorem and Mundici's equivalence in full.
- Nikolaos Galatos, Peter Jipsen, Tomasz Kowalski and Hiroakira Ono, *Residuated Lattices: An Algebraic Glimpse at Substructural Logics* (2007). Residuated lattices and the substructural logics they model.
- Robin Hirsch and Ian Hodkinson, *Relation Algebras by Games* (2002). Relation and cylindric algebras, representation, and the games behind the non-finite axiomatizability and undecidability results.
- Josep Maria Font, *Abstract Algebraic Logic: An Introductory Textbook* (2016). Algebraizable logics, the Leibniz hierarchy and the bridge theorems, from the beginning.
