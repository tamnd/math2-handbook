[Algebraic logic](/Z3DE4R4RET/) studies logics through classes of algebras. The idea goes back to George Boole, who in 1847 treated the laws of thought as equations in an algebra of classes. In modern form it starts from the [Lindenbaum–Tarski algebra](/QC9M3Q8B8Q/) of a logic: the formulas, with two formulas identified when they are [logically equivalent](/0JMR2HM2N2/), and with the connectives as operations. For classical propositional logic this algebra is a [Boolean algebra](/0XADJKM699/), and a formula is a tautology exactly when it equals the top element in every Boolean algebra under every assignment. The [laws of propositional logic](/A9CSGZNBV5/) are then identities of Boolean algebras.

The same pattern works for many logics, each matched with its own class of algebras. Intuitionistic logic corresponds to [Heyting algebras](/NG943XNQWH/), the modal logic $\mathrm{S4}$ to [interior algebras](/BDEX7HET71/), and normal modal logics in general to Boolean algebras with operators, studied by Jónsson and Tarski in 1951. Łukasiewicz logic corresponds to [MV-algebras](/8T4M7RN9AC/) and substructural logics to [residuated lattices](/E337A6WVH3/). Under the match, logical properties become algebraic ones: completeness for a given semantics says that the algebras arising from that semantics generate the whole class, and interpolation becomes a form of amalgamation. Dualities add geometry. The [Stone representation theorem](/S4QJ395PA5/) of 1936 represents every Boolean algebra as an algebra of sets, and [Stone duality](/B9T33D5MJK/) matches Boolean algebras with compact totally disconnected spaces; [Esakia duality](/CD9Y7YP4EJ/) does the same for Heyting algebras, and links algebraic semantics with Kripke frames.

First-order logic needs algebras that also handle quantifiers. Tarski, with Henkin and Monk, developed [cylindric algebras](/1MDT9BF81H/), in which existential quantification over each variable is an operation; Paul Halmos developed polyadic algebras. [Relation algebras](/GQK4DSA11K/), axiomatized by Tarski in 1941, are an algebraic form of the logic of binary relations. Abstract algebraic logic, founded by Blok and Pigozzi in 1989, asks in general which logics have an algebraic semantics and classifies them by how closely they are tied to their algebras.
