Take all formulas of classical propositional logic in the two variables $p$ and $q$, and call two of them equal when the logic proves them equivalent. There are infinitely many formulas but only $16$ classes, one for each truth table in two variables. The connectives act on the classes, and the result is a [Boolean algebra](/0XADJKM699/), the [Lindenbaum–Tarski algebra](/QC9M3Q8B8Q/) of the logic. A formula is a theorem exactly when its class is the top element, so the question "is $\varphi$ provable?" becomes the question "does the equation $\varphi \approx 1$ hold in every Boolean algebra?". [Algebraic logic](/Z3DE4R4RET/) is the study of logics through such classes of algebras.

The method works far beyond classical logic. Intuitionistic logic gives [Heyting algebras](/NG943XNQWH/), the modal logic $\mathrm{S4}$ gives interior algebras, and infinite-valued Łukasiewicz logic gives MV-algebras. Once a logic has its algebras, logical questions become algebraic ones. A completeness theorem says that some small family of algebras, such as the unit interval for Łukasiewicz logic, generates the whole class. The extensions of the logic correspond to subclasses of algebras. Interpolation turns into amalgamation, and the deduction theorem into a property of congruences.

First-order logic needs more operations, because quantifiers bind variables. Cylindric algebras add one operation for each variable, polyadic algebras add substitutions, and relation algebras treat binary relations with composition and converse. Here a new problem appears that propositional logic does not have: the abstract algebras need not be algebras of sets or relations, and the representable ones can fail to have a finite list of axioms. The last part of the chapter steps back and asks which logics have an algebraic semantics at all, and how close the match between a logic and its algebras can be.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in eight groups, read in order.

1. **Formulas as elements of an algebra.** The program of [algebraic logic](/Z3DE4R4RET/), the [Lindenbaum–Tarski algebra](/QC9M3Q8B8Q/), and [Boolean algebras in logic](/KG7730QZMQ/) as the model case.
2. **Algebras of modal logic.** [Interior algebras](/BDEX7HET71/) for $\mathrm{S4}$, [modal algebras](/3H85NSFWHK/) for normal modal logics, and the [Jónsson–Tarski representation theorem](/HC5V2QFFWB/), which embeds each modal algebra in the complex algebra of a frame.
3. **Algebras of fuzzy logics.** [MV-algebras](/8T4M7RN9AC/), [Mundici's equivalence](/64NDFWQEZ2/) with lattice-ordered abelian groups with a strong unit, and [BL-algebras](/ERNYMVEGKC/).
4. **Residuation.** [Residuated lattices](/E337A6WVH3/), the algebras of substructural logics, and [residuated Boolean algebras](/KCG48CWY69/).
5. **Post, Moisil and Nelson algebras.** [Post algebras](/RD2A5Z7E2B/) with a cyclic negation, [Łukasiewicz–Moisil algebras](/51A2GMPDYK/) and [Nelson algebras](/XRGDT262HQ/) for constructive logic with strong negation.
6. **Algebras of first-order logic.** [Cylindric algebras](/1MDT9BF81H/), the [representation theorems for cylindric algebras](/WZ1BZYVD5G/), and [polyadic algebras](/CQ9A86S3J4/).
7. **Relation algebras.** [Relation algebras](/GQK4DSA11K/), the [representation theorems for relation algebras](/7R95RBEPP8/), and the link between [relation algebras and three-variable logic](/AHWXJ3GT02/).
8. **Abstract algebraic logic.** [Algebraizable logics](/CESWST3NWA/), the [Leibniz hierarchy](/6JT3CMJT8F/), [Suszko's reduction](/2FQB3NQF8A/) and the [bridge theorems](/C3DC1C23SQ/).

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these entries in order: [algebraic logic](/Z3DE4R4RET/), [Lindenbaum–Tarski algebra](/QC9M3Q8B8Q/), [Boolean algebras in logic](/KG7730QZMQ/), [modal algebra](/3H85NSFWHK/), [Jónsson–Tarski representation theorem](/HC5V2QFFWB/), [MV-algebra](/8T4M7RN9AC/), [residuated lattice](/E337A6WVH3/), [cylindric algebra](/1MDT9BF81H/), [relation algebra](/GQK4DSA11K/) and [algebraizable logic](/CESWST3NWA/). Together they show the method once for each kind of logic: propositional, modal, many-valued, substructural and first-order, and then in general. The attached examples of the Lindenbaum–Tarski algebra and of algebraizable logic are the ones most worth working through.

Post, Łukasiewicz–Moisil and Nelson algebras, residuated Boolean algebras, polyadic algebras and Suszko's reduction can wait for a second pass. The two representation theorems for cylindric and relation algebras and the bridge theorems are research-level results; on a first pass read their statements and the remarks on why each hypothesis is needed.

## Before you start

You need [the propositional calculus](/JDK63589SA/), with its Boolean semantics and its notion of consequence, and the general facts on lattices, congruences, varieties and quasivarieties from [order, lattices and universal algebra](/V1VVQR12AQ/). For the intuitionistic side read [intuitionistic and intermediate logics](/38EQ20GR7M/) and [pseudocomplements, Heyting algebras, frames and locales](/CEFSPCBHAF/). The modal groups use the frames and normal modal logics of [syntax, Kripke semantics and completeness of modal logic](/SKY7AQFG1V/). The residuation group uses [ordered semigroups, residuation and quantales](/M3SXTMQ3TM/), and the logics behind its algebras are in [substructural, linear and relevance logics](/98QF5J4W16/) and [many-valued and fuzzy logics](/WZP584JQ06/). For the first-order groups you need [first-order logic](/086QKPF0A6/) and [Gödel's completeness theorem](/WQ1ZE255SF/).
