The box of modal logic can be read in many ways, and each reading gives a logic of its own. Read $\square\varphi$ as "$\varphi$ will always hold", and the axiom $\square p \to \square\square p$ says that time is transitive. Read it as "agent $a$ knows $\varphi$", and the axiom $\square p \to p$ says that only truths are known. Read it as "$\varphi$ is obligatory", and $\square p \to p$ must fail, since obligations are broken, while $\square p \to \lozenge p$ must hold, since what is obligatory is permitted. Each reading uses the [Kripke semantics](/NGHWZ9Y7ZV/) of Chapter 2.20 with worlds and relations chosen to fit, and the frame conditions of the [common modal axioms](/FDFQFWPM4X/) tell you which frames a reading needs.

The readings matter outside philosophy. A specification such as "every request is eventually granted" is the formula $\mathbf{G}(\mathit{req} \to \mathbf{F}\,\mathit{grant})$ of [linear temporal logic](/RMJ56ZPA63/), and [model checking](/VNY0VXVZ53/) decides whether a hardware or software design satisfies it. A distributed protocol over an unreliable channel can never make a fact [common knowledge](/7BCMKF2P7X/) among its processes, and this explains why some coordination tasks are impossible. The [description logic](/TXQ80B8ZSA/) $\mathcal{ALC}$, which is multimodal $\mathrm{K}$ in another notation, underlies the web ontology language OWL and large medical terminologies.

The chapter also treats logics that change the language rather than the reading. A counterfactual "if $\varphi$ were the case, $\psi$ would be" is a binary connective, true when $\psi$ holds at the closest worlds where $\varphi$ holds. Justification logic replaces the box by explicit evidence $t{:}\varphi$. Inquisitive logic evaluates formulas at sets of worlds, so that a formula can ask a question.

## What the chapter covers

1. **Logics of time.** Prior's [temporal logic](/8XB117FKKV/) with future and past operators and its frame classes; [linear temporal logic](/RMJ56ZPA63/) over the natural numbers; [computation tree logic](/F6JA5C5BJX/) over branching time, with its linear-time model checking; the full logic [CTL*](/Y4XZ94GRXV/); [Kamp's theorem](/CQ0QCQK5JT/) on the expressive completeness of since and until; and [interval temporal logics](/991B5RR6X4/).
2. **Knowledge and belief.** [Epistemic logic](/EA34W4ERRE/) with the introspection axioms, [common knowledge](/7BCMKF2P7X/) as a fixed point, the [muddy children puzzle](/BG9TYCJ7HP/), [doxastic logic](/RTP4HTZYK3/) with Moore's paradox, and [dynamic epistemic logic](/9465T2PWZT/), where public announcements delete worlds.
3. **Obligation, conditionals and agency.** [Deontic logic](/7B74J6J5JV/) as $\mathrm{KD}$ and its paradoxes, [conditional logic](/ZGYG7WN7EC/) in Lewis's sphere semantics, and [STIT logic](/WYY2JXWMYY/) of agents who see to it that something holds.
4. **Evidence, belief change and knowability.** [Justification logic](/5HKWCXEFG1/) and the realization of $\mathrm{S4}$, the postulates of [AGM belief revision](/Y05YQN7HGQ/), [Gärdenfors's triviality theorem](/WMZ1QP7KQY/) and [Fitch's paradox of knowability](/HVCW7QYXSW/).
5. **Questions, concepts and values.** [Inquisitive logic](/EX0ND8DCTN/), [description logic](/TXQ80B8ZSA/) and [axiological logic](/4BRXSBXXWG/).

## A first reading

Take these entries in order: [temporal logic](/8XB117FKKV/), [linear temporal logic](/RMJ56ZPA63/), [computation tree logic](/F6JA5C5BJX/), [epistemic logic](/EA34W4ERRE/), [common knowledge](/7BCMKF2P7X/), the [muddy children puzzle](/BG9TYCJ7HP/), [dynamic epistemic logic](/9465T2PWZT/), [deontic logic](/7B74J6J5JV/), [conditional logic](/ZGYG7WN7EC/) and [AGM belief revision](/Y05YQN7HGQ/). They show the main pattern of the chapter: fix a reading of the box, choose the frames that fit it, and test the axioms on small models. The attached examples of linear temporal logic and computation tree logic are worth working through, because the difference between a property of one run and a property of a tree of runs is the source of most errors.

[CTL*](/Y4XZ94GRXV/), [Kamp's theorem](/CQ0QCQK5JT/) and [interval temporal logics](/991B5RR6X4/) can wait until linear and branching time are familiar. [STIT logic](/WYY2JXWMYY/), [justification logic](/5HKWCXEFG1/), the triviality theorem, Fitch's paradox and the last group are independent of each other and can be read in any order.

## Before you start

You need the language of [modal logic](/C3RZY20ETK/), [Kripke semantics](/NGHWZ9Y7ZV/), the [normal modal logic](/D1TJYZZDCY/) $\mathrm{K}$, and its extensions [T, D and K4](/XVYMEZKH7R/), [S4](/N60RVXSA5W/) and [S5](/VC3A2VWXND/), with the [necessitation rule](/G989VP2NGP/). [Bisimulation](/X27BR4YP2Y/) explains which properties a modal language cannot express, and [propositional dynamic logic](/VJ78A0Q22R/) and the [modal mu-calculus](/MZ0M2KR9S0/) are close relatives of the temporal logics. For the decision procedures of linear time, [Büchi's theorem on S1S](/YPDXVTH5JR/) is the background. The [material conditional](/0NMG7W7Q11/) and the [strict conditional](/5JW54NP52K/) are the older accounts of "if" that conditional logic improves on, and [provability logic](/WJK0YA4GP2/) is the source of justification logic.
