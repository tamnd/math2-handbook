## History

Smullyan introduced the rudimentary relations in *Theory of Formal Systems* (1961), and Bennett showed in his 1962 dissertation that they are exactly the relations definable by bounded formulas. Wrathall proved in 1978 that they form the linear-time hierarchy. Cobham characterized the polynomial-time functions in "The intrinsic computational difficulty of functions" (1965), by limited recursion on notation, the scheme behind [Cook's PV](/FMM44KFZQT/).

Parikh studied $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ in "Existence and feasibility in arithmetic" (1971) and proved the theorem that now carries his name. Cook introduced $\mathsf{PV}$ in "Feasibly constructive proofs and the propositional calculus" (1975), together with the translation of its equations into polynomial-size propositional proofs. Nelson's *Predicative Arithmetic* was the source of the smash function, and Paris and Wilkie studied $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0 + \Omega_1$ in a series of papers, among them "Counting problems in bounded arithmetic" (1985), where the [Paris–Wilkie translation](/ZVD982MVST/) appears.

Buss defined the theories $S^i_2$ and $T^i_2$ and proved the [witnessing theorem](/RH4R7GTTAX/) in his 1985 Princeton dissertation, published as *Bounded Arithmetic* (1986). Paris, Wilkie and Woods showed in 1988 that $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0 + \Omega_1$ proves the weak pigeonhole principle for $\Delta_0$ relations and the existence of infinitely many primes. In the same year Ajtai proved that constant-depth proofs of the pigeonhole formulas need superpolynomial size, so that $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0(\alpha)$ does not prove the [pigeonhole principle](/NJ18EZ5JBC/) for a new relation $\alpha$. Krajíček, Pudlák and Woods, and independently Pitassi, Beame and Impagliazzo, improved the bound to an exponential one.

Krajíček, Pudlák and Takeuti proved in "Bounded arithmetic and the polynomial hierarchy" (1991) that a collapse of the hierarchy of theories gives a [collapse of the polynomial hierarchy](/YP7KE8E6DC/). Buss, and Zambella in "Notes on polynomially bounded arithmetic" (1996), showed that $S_2$ is finitely axiomatizable exactly when it proves that the polynomial hierarchy collapses. Krajíček's book *Bounded Arithmetic, Propositional Logic, and Complexity Theory* (1995) brought the subject together.

Jeřábek developed [approximate counting](/65K2S22DGZ/) in a series of papers from 2004 to 2009, with the dual weak pigeonhole principle for polynomial-time functions as the basic axiom. Whether $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$ alone proves that there are infinitely many primes, and whether the theories $S^i_2$ and $T^i_2$ [can be separated](/PEJQDHHX8E/), remain open.

## Further reading

- Samuel R. Buss, *Bounded Arithmetic* (Bibliopolis, 1986). The original account of $S^i_2$ and $T^i_2$ and of the witnessing theorems.
- Jan Krajíček, *Bounded Arithmetic, Propositional Logic, and Complexity Theory* (Cambridge University Press, 1995). The standard monograph, covering the theories, the witnessing theorems, the KPT theorem and the propositional translations.
- Petr Hájek and Pavel Pudlák, *Metamathematics of First-Order Arithmetic* (Springer, 1993). Its chapter on bounded arithmetic treats $\mathsf{I}\Delta_0$, $\Omega_1$ and Buss's theories next to the stronger fragments of PA.
- Stephen Cook and Phuong Nguyen, *Logical Foundations of Proof Complexity* (Cambridge University Press, 2010). Develops two-sorted theories for complexity classes from $\mathrm{AC}^0$ to P and their propositional translations.
- Jan Krajíček, *Proof Complexity* (Cambridge University Press, 2019). A broad treatment of propositional proof systems, with bounded arithmetic as one of its main tools.
