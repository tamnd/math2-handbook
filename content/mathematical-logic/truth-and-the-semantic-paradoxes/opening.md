The [liar sentence](/DYAB23WA1X/), "this sentence is false", is true if it is false and false if it is true. Paradoxes of this kind show that a language cannot contain its own truth predicate under naive rules, and the theory of truth studies what can be done instead. The subject touches logic, philosophy and the foundations of mathematics, and its tools are those of Chapter 2.11.

Alfred Tarski's paper of 1933 set the terms. An adequate definition of truth for a language must yield every instance of Convention T, the schema "$\varphi$" is true if and only if $\varphi$. Tarski showed how to define truth for a formal language in a richer [metalanguage](/D9G8M42GKC/), by the recursive clauses of [satisfaction](/2RBQPV02AS/), and he proved that truth for the language of arithmetic cannot be defined in that language: this is [Tarski's undefinability theorem](/YRV6SYK2BY/), a consequence of the [diagonal lemma](/3PRW76471J/). His solution was a [hierarchy of languages](/Z6MCGB0ZFX/), each with a truth predicate for the ones below it. Restricted truth is still definable, as the [partial truth predicates](/Y2MF1EZAM7/) for $\Sigma_n$ sentences show.

Saul Kripke's paper of 1975 allowed a single language to contain its own truth predicate, at the price of [truth-value gaps](/4CBCWZQZ12/). Using the [strong Kleene connectives](/B7KATP4SEA/), he built the extension of the predicate in stages and took the least fixed point. The sentences that receive a value there are the grounded ones; the liar and the [truth-teller](/EWR59SWNB3/) get no value. The revision theory of Gupta, Herzberger and Belnap treats truth as a rule that is applied over and over. Axiomatic theories of truth add a truth predicate to Peano arithmetic and study its strength. The disquotational theory, with the Tarski biconditionals alone, is conservative over $\mathsf{PA}$, while the compositional theory with full induction proves the consistency of $\mathsf{PA}$; the Kripke–Feferman and Friedman–Sheard theories axiomatize the two main approaches. Other paradoxes test every proposal, among them Curry's paradox, which needs no negation, and Yablo's paradox of 1993, which avoids direct self-reference.
