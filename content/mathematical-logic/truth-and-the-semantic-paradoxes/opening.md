Take the sentence "this sentence is not true". If it is true, then what it says holds, so it is not true. If it is not true, then that is just what it says, so it is true. In arithmetic such a sentence exists. The [diagonal lemma](/3PRW76471J/) gives a sentence $\lambda$ with $\lambda \leftrightarrow \neg T(\ulcorner \lambda \urcorner)$, and the naive truth schema $T(\ulcorner \varphi \urcorner) \leftrightarrow \varphi$ for $\varphi := \lambda$ turns this into a contradiction. The derivation uses no excluded middle, so it is valid in intuitionistic logic as well. A theory of truth must say which premise goes: the schema, classical logic, or the idea that one language can speak of its own truth.

A second example shows that the trouble is not only self-reference. Let $S_0, S_1, S_2, \ldots$ be sentences, where $S_n$ says that every later $S_k$ is untrue. No sentence speaks about itself, yet no assignment of true and false fits all of them. Over arithmetic the T-sentences for this sequence are consistent, but no model with only the standard numbers satisfies them.

The chapter studies four answers. Tarski keeps classical logic and the T-sentences, and he defines truth for a language in a richer metalanguage. Kripke keeps one language with its own truth predicate and lets some sentences have no value. The revision theory keeps classical logic at every stage and asks which sentences settle down when hypotheses about truth are revised. The axiomatic theories take truth as a primitive over Peano arithmetic and measure what each choice of axioms proves. The chapter ends with the sorites paradox, which uses no self-reference but is answered with similar tools.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in six groups, read in order.

1. **The liar and Tarski's theory.** The [liar paradox](/KBYY281V8T/) makes the naive truth schema inconsistent over Robinson arithmetic. The [semantic conception of truth](/8FBF0EFFC9/) states Tarski's conditions of formal correctness and material adequacy, and the [Tarskian hierarchy](/Z6MCGB0ZFX/) gives a truth predicate at each level for the levels below it.
2. **Other paradoxes of self-reference.** The [truth-teller sentence](/XADBJH4872/) is consistent with either value. The [Berry paradox](/MYP4A5VRBA/) and the [Grelling-Nelson paradox](/E1XCM5TEFD/) refute naive schemas for definability and satisfaction. The [Yablo paradox](/6FJXAN1ND9/) uses an infinite sequence of sentences, and the [knower paradox](/N3BWQRFQCC/) shows that knowledge cannot be both factive and closed under proof.
3. **Fixed points and revision.** [Kripke's theory of truth](/ES71QTSTNV/) builds a partial truth predicate as a fixed point of the strong Kleene jump, and defines the grounded sentences. The [revision theory of truth](/ZQ4TWM7K6E/) treats truth as a circular definition and studies revision sequences.
4. **Gaps and gluts.** A [truth-value gap](/4CBCWZQZ12/) is neither true nor false; a [truth-value glut](/WH94DG6D3Q/) is both, as the liar is in the logic of paradox.
5. **Axiomatic theories of truth.** The [axiomatic theories of truth](/KE8ZX8KVA3/) TB, UTB, $\mathsf{CT}^-$, CT, FS and KF are compared by strength. [Compositional truth over PA proves the consistency of PA](/W4MSCKSG3D/), the [Kripke-Feferman theory](/2F15E76R0Z/) describes Kripke's fixed points in classical logic, and [McGee's theorem](/GQW8RQVZJ4/) shows that natural type-free axioms give an $\omega$-inconsistent theory.
6. **Vagueness.** The [sorites paradox](/2BV7KAZG1H/) and the epistemic, supervaluational and degree-theoretic answers to it.

## A first reading

For a first reading, take these entries in order: [liar paradox](/KBYY281V8T/), [semantic conception of truth](/8FBF0EFFC9/), [Tarskian hierarchy](/Z6MCGB0ZFX/), [truth-teller sentence](/XADBJH4872/), [Kripke's theory of truth](/ES71QTSTNV/), [revision theory of truth](/ZQ4TWM7K6E/), [truth-value gap](/4CBCWZQZ12/), [axiomatic theories of truth](/KE8ZX8KVA3/), [compositional truth over PA](/W4MSCKSG3D/) and the [Kripke-Feferman theory](/2F15E76R0Z/). Kripke's theory is the centre of the chapter: work through its attached examples, which follow the liar and the iterated truth of $0 = 0$ through the stages and show that each fixed point is a model of KF.

The Berry, Grelling-Nelson, Yablo and knower paradoxes can wait for a second pass; each one repeats the pattern of the liar for a new notion. So can the truth-value glut, McGee's theorem and the sorites paradox. McGee's theorem is easier once FS, from the axiomatic theories, is familiar, and the glut page is easier after the [logic of paradox](/BXJZG67PX3/).
