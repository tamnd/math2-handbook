1. ◦ For each sentence, say whether it is a statement, an open sentence or neither. For a statement, give its truth value or say that it is not known; for an open sentence, give one value of the variable that makes it true and one that makes it false.
(a) $2^{10} > 1000$.
(b) $3x - 1 > 5$, where $x$ is a real number.
(c) Is $2^{11} - 1$ prime?
(d) There are infinitely many primes $p$ such that $p + 2$ is also prime.
(e) Let $m = 2^{11} - 1$.

2. ◦ In which of these sentences does "and" join two statements? For each, give the truth value.
(a) $4$ and $6$ are even.
(b) $4$ and $6$ have a common factor greater than $1$.
(c) $12$ and $18$ are multiples of $6$.
(d) The lines $y = 2x$ and $y = 2x + 1$ are parallel.

3. ◦ Let $n$ range over $\set{1, 2, \dots, 10}$. List the $n$ for which each sentence is true.
(a) $n$ is prime or $n$ is odd.
(b) $n$ is neither prime nor odd.
(c) $n$ is prime but not odd.
(d) $n$ is odd and $n$ is not prime.
Which two of the four sentences are negations of each other?

4. ◦ Rewrite each sentence in the form "if …, then …", with any hidden "for every" written out, and say whether it is true.
(a) A triangle is right-angled only if the square of its longest side equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides.
(b) For a real number $x$, $x^2 = 9$ is sufficient for $x = 3$.
(c) For an integer $n$, $9 \mid n$ is sufficient for $3 \mid n$.
(d) For a sequence $(a_n)$ of real numbers, the series $\sum a_n$ diverges unless $a_n \to 0$.
(e) A function has an inverse whenever it is bijective.

5. ◦ Consider the statement "for every integer $n$, if $10 \mid n$, then $5 \mid n$". Write its [converse](/09A3N7ZN5G/), its [inverse](/VB7B8766D2/), its [contrapositive](/04T1CT7212/) and its negation, and give the truth value of each of the five statements.

6. ◦ Give the truth value of each statement.
(a) If $\sqrt 2$ is rational, then $\sqrt 2 = 7/5$.
(b) If $9$ is prime, then $9$ is even.
(c) If $5$ is odd, then $5 > 8$.
(d) Every integer $n$ with $n^2 = 3$ is even.
(e) $2$ is even only if $3$ is even.

7. ◦ Each sentence hides at least one quantifier. Write it with all its quantifiers, and their domains, made explicit.
(a) $\sin 2x = 2 \sin x \cos x$.
(b) The square of an integer leaves remainder $0$ or $1$ on division by $4$.
(c) The equation $x^2 + x = 6$ has an integer solution.
(d) Between two different rational numbers there is a rational number.

8. ◦ Negate each statement, pushing "not" inward until it stands next to simple statements. Then say which of the statement and its negation is true; for (c), take $x$ and $y$ to be fixed real numbers and say only what the negation is.
(a) Some prime is a perfect square.
(b) For every real $x$, $x^2 \ge x$.
(c) $x > 0$ or $y \le 3$.
(d) For every integer $n$, if $n$ is odd, then $n^2 + 1$ is even.

9. ◦ Each of four cards has a shape on one side and a whole number on the other. The visible sides show a square, a circle, $9$ and $5$. Which cards must you turn over to check the rule "if a card has a square on one side, then it has a prime number on the other side"? Why need the card showing $5$ not be turned?

10. ◦ Spoken aloud, "one over $x$ plus one" can mean $\frac{1}{x+1}$ or $\frac{1}{x} + 1$. Evaluate both at $x = 3$. Do the same for "two to the three squared", read as $(2^3)^2$ or as $2^{(3^2)}$, and say which of the two the written $2^{3^2}$ means.

11. • Let $D = \set{1, 2, 3, 4}$, with every variable ranging over $D$. Give the truth value of each statement, with a reason.
(a) $\forall x \, \exists y \, (x + y \text{ is even})$ and $\exists y \, \forall x \, (x + y \text{ is even})$.
(b) $\forall x \, \exists y \, (x < y)$ and $\exists x \, \forall y \, (x \le y)$.
(c) $\forall x \, \exists y \, (5 \mid xy - 1)$ and $\exists y \, \forall x \, (5 \mid xy - 1)$.

12. • For an integer $n$, decide whether each condition is necessary for $4 \mid n$, sufficient for it, both or neither. Give a counterexample for each "not".
(a) $8 \mid n$. (b) $2 \mid n$. (c) $4 \mid n^2$. (d) $8 \mid n^2$. (e) $2 \mid n$ and $6 \mid n$.

13. • Let $n$ range over $\set{1, 2, \dots, 12}$. Rewrite each sentence as a conditional with its quantifier written out, and decide whether it is true. For each false one, list every counterexample in the range.
(a) $n$ is prime only if $n$ is odd.
(b) $n$ is prime unless $n$ is even.
(c) $n^2 > 20$ is sufficient for $n > 4$.
(d) $n \le 3$ is necessary for $n^2 < 10$.

14. • A map $f : \Z \to \Z$ is injective when for all integers $m$ and $n$, $f(m) = f(n)$ implies $m = n$, and surjective when for every integer $k$ there is an integer $n$ with $f(n) = k$. Write the negation of each definition with "not" pushed inward. Then decide whether $g(n) = n^2 - n$ and $h(n) = \floor{n/2}$ are injective and whether they are surjective, giving a witness for each negation that holds.

15. • A function $f : \R \to \R$ is periodic when there is a $p > 0$ such that $f(x + p) = f(x)$ for every real $x$. Write the statement "$f$ is not periodic" with "not" pushed inward. Use it to prove that $f(x) = x^2$ is not periodic, and show that a constant function is periodic.

16. • A sequence $(a_n)$ tends to $\infty$ when for every real $M$ there is an $N$ such that $a_n > M$ for every $n \ge N$. Write the negation with "not" pushed inward. Let $a_n = n$ for even $n$ and $a_n = 0$ for odd $n$. Prove that $(a_n)$ does not tend to $\infty$, and show that it is still not bounded.

17. • Find the error. A student is asked to negate "for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a positive integer $N$ with $1/N < \varepsilon$" and writes "for every $\varepsilon \le 0$ there is a positive integer $N$ with $1/N \ge \varepsilon$". Name the two mistakes, write the correct negation, and explain why the student's sentence cannot be the negation by finding the truth values of both.

18. • Find the error. Asked to prove "for every integer $n$, $3 \mid n^2$ only if $3 \mid n$", a student writes: "Suppose $3 \mid n$, so $n = 3k$. Then $n^2 = 9k^2 = 3(3k^2)$, so $3 \mid n^2$." What did the student prove? Give a correct proof of the statement asked for, using [proof by contrapositive](/0BB5TPGQ1N/).

19. • Prove that there is exactly one real number $x$ with $x^3 + 2x = 3$. Label the existence part and the uniqueness part.

20. • Let every variable range over $\set{0, 1, 2}$. For each condition $P(x, y)$, decide whether $\exists! x \, \exists! y \, P(x, y)$ is true, and count the pairs $(x, y)$ that satisfy $P$.
(a) $x \le y$. (b) $x + y = 2$. (c) $xy = 0$.

21. • Decide whether each phrase names exactly one object. If it does, give the object; if not, say whether existence or uniqueness fails.
(a) The real number $x$ with $x^3 = 5x$.
(b) The positive real number $x$ with $x^2 = 7$.
(c) The integer $n$ with $2n + 1 = 8$.
(d) The prime $p$ with $p \mid 91$ and $p < 10$.
(e) The least element of $\set{x \in \R : x > 0}$.

22. • Each sentence has two readings. Write both in unambiguous words and give the truth value of each.
(a) All solutions of $x^2 - 3x + 2 = 0$ are not even.
(b) For every function $f : [0, 1] \to \R$, if $f(x) = 0$ for any $x \in [0, 1]$, then $f(x) = 0$ for every $x \in [0, 1]$.

23. • Find the error. "Claim: the equation $x^3 = 9x$ has exactly two real solutions, $3$ and $-3$. Proof: both satisfy it. Dividing $x^3 = 9x$ by $x$ gives $x^2 = 9$, so every solution is $3$ or $-3$." Locate the error, say which half of the claim it breaks, and give the correct set of solutions.

24. ★ A function $f : \R \to \R$ is uniformly continuous when for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a $\delta > 0$ such that for all real $x$ and $y$, $\abs{x - y} < \delta$ implies $\abs{f(x) - f(y)} < \varepsilon$.
(a) Write the negation with "not" pushed inward.
(b) Prove that $f(x) = x^2$ is not uniformly continuous.
(c) Write "$f$ is [continuous](/0KRMJRDRZT/) at every real $x$" with all its quantifiers, and say which quantifier has moved compared with uniform continuity.

25. ★ Prove that there is exactly one prime $p$ such that $p + 2$ and $p + 4$ are also prime.

26. ★ Let $f : \R \to \R$. Write "$f$ has exactly two zeros" using only $\forall$, $\exists$, $=$, $\ne$, the connectives and the expressions $f(x)$. Write its negation with "not" pushed inward and say it in words. Check your formula on $x^2 - 1$, $x^3 - x$ and $x^2 + 1$.

27. ★ A sequence $(a_n)$ converges to $L$ when for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is an $N$ such that $\abs{a_n - L} < \varepsilon$ for every $n \ge N$. Prove that a sequence converges to at most one $L$. Say which half of "exactly one" this is, and why it is the half that allows the phrase "the limit of $(a_n)$" once the sequence converges.

28. ★ Prove that for every real number $a$ there is exactly one real number $x$ with $x^3 = a$. You may use the intermediate value theorem for continuous functions on a closed interval.

29. ★ Let $P(x, y)$ be a condition on a finite set $D$.
(a) Prove that if exactly one pair $(x, y) \in D \times D$ satisfies $P$, then $\exists! x \, \exists! y \, P(x, y)$ is true.
(b) Show by an example that the converse of (a) is false.
(c) Let $D = \set{0, 1, 2}$ and let $P(x, y)$ hold exactly for the pairs $(1, 2)$, $(2, 0)$ and $(2, 1)$. Show that $\exists! x \, \exists! y \, P(x, y)$ is true and $\exists! y \, \exists! x \, P(x, y)$ is false. Compare this with two quantifiers $\exists$, which may always change places.
