A class is asked to show that $n^3 - n$ is divisible by $6$ for every positive integer $n$. Four students hand in four answers. Ana computes $0, 6, 24, 60, 120, 210, 336, 504, 720, 990$ for $n = 1$ to $10$, sees that every value is a multiple of $6$, and writes "true". Ben writes "true, we proved this in class last week". Cara writes $n^3 - n = (n - 1)\, n\, (n + 1)$, notes that among three consecutive integers one is even and one is a multiple of $3$, and concludes that $6$ divides the product. Dev writes $n^3 - n = n(n^2 - 1)$, "and $n^2 - 1$ is divisible by $6$, so $n^3 - n$ is too".

Only Cara has written a proof. Dev's lines look like algebra, but the claim in the middle is false: at $n = 2$ the number $n^2 - 1 = 3$ is not a multiple of $6$. Each of the other three answers fails for a different reason, and so each needs a different response from the teacher. Ana is convinced by examples, and a pattern that holds for forty cases and then fails, such as [$n^2 + n + 41$](/H75AB4EJT5/), speaks to her doubt directly. Ben is convinced by the authority of the teacher, so another example would not help him; he needs to see that the class itself can settle the question. Dev treats symbols as things to move around, and he needs to be asked what each symbol says about numbers.

Sorting answers in this way is the work of research on the teaching of proof. It asks what convinces a learner, which difficulties block a proof, how students read a proof written by someone else, and which ways of teaching move a class from examples to deductive arguments. The entries of this chapter collect the main ideas of that research, with the evidence behind them and the questions it leaves open. They are written mainly for teachers, at school and at university.

## What the chapter covers

1. **The field and the views behind it.** [Teaching foundations of mathematics](/H5BM68GXS9/) describes the field and its central design question, and [philosophy and mathematics in education](/174ES70CS8/) shows how a teacher's view of mathematics shapes what a class learns about proof.
2. **Logic, language and argument in the classroom.** [Teaching logic](/K1Y2MAQQ9E/), [mathematical language in education](/S19WMS43TW/), [reasoning and proof in the classroom](/E77B813MT4/) and [teaching sets and relations](/12ZNG0NRY7/) treat the conditional, the clash of everyday and technical words, a definition of proof for school classes, and the history of sets in school.
3. **What convinces a learner.** [Proof schemes](/YVNRVXJR68/) classify what convinces a person, [transition-to-proof courses](/HQDCSJG379/) describe the university course built to change it, and the [Toulmin model](/RVZCSFDB2Y/) lays out the parts of an argument.
4. **Difficulties with proving and with reading proofs.** [Student difficulties with proof](/5K2969GSWE/) sorts errors by cause, and [proof comprehension and validation](/52Y2MW27J0/) treats reading and checking proofs.
5. **Ways of teaching proof.** The [Moore method and inquiry-based learning](/WKM6535VHG/), [proof in school curricula](/7ZSHDS63QY/) and [generic and pre-formal proofs](/SG9XA69J64/) describe three answers to these difficulties.
6. **Concept images and machine-checked proof.** [Concept image and concept definition](/C5S2X0WDJ8/) explains why students reason from pictures instead of definitions, and [proof assistants in teaching](/KGPHY3F0GC/) describes what software that checks every step can and cannot do.

## A first reading

Read these in order:

- [proof schemes](/YVNRVXJR68/), which gives the vocabulary for the opening example;
- [reasoning and proof in the classroom](/E77B813MT4/), for Stylianides's definition of proof at school;
- [teaching logic](/K1Y2MAQQ9E/), on how students read a conditional;
- [student difficulties with proof](/5K2969GSWE/);
- [generic and pre-formal proofs](/SG9XA69J64/), the most useful tool for a school class;
- [concept image and concept definition](/C5S2X0WDJ8/);
- [proof comprehension and validation](/52Y2MW27J0/).

A teacher at university should add [transition-to-proof courses](/HQDCSJG379/) and the [Moore method](/WKM6535VHG/). The two entries of the first group, the entry on sets, the [Toulmin model](/RVZCSFDB2Y/) and the entry on [proof assistants](/KGPHY3F0GC/) can wait until a particular question calls for them.

## Before you start

You should know what a [mathematical proof](/0B94TKFQJX/) is, how to read the [language of mathematical statements](/WMY13MXY3W/), and the [basic proof methods](/958EF46EYJ/), since the chapter studies how students learn these. The entries on reading and checking proofs build on [writing and reading proofs](/STSM6PR7TX/). The wider field of [mathematics education](/9622VKAWMZ/), with its theories of learning and its studies of curricula, is the setting of the chapter, but the entries explain each research term when it first appears.

The mathematics in the examples is school algebra, parity and divisibility, a little geometry, and in a few places limits, continuity and the derivative from a first calculus course.
