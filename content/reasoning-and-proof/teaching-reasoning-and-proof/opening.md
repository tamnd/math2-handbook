Ask a class of new university students to prove that the sum of two odd numbers is even. Some write $3 + 5 = 8$, $7 + 9 = 16$ and $11 + 13 = 24$ and stop. Some write $2n + 1 + 2n + 1 = 4n + 2$, using the same $n$ for both numbers. Some say the teacher proved it last week. A few write $(2a + 1) + (2b + 1) = 2(a + b + 1)$. Each of these students is following a standard of what convinces, and research on teaching proof begins by describing those standards.

The research asks why proof is hard to learn, how a class comes to agree that an argument is valid, how students read conditional statements and definitions, and what curricula, courses and tools help. It draws on psychology, on the philosophy of mathematics, and on careful study of classrooms and of students reading proofs. This chapter collects the main results and the main teaching approaches, from primary school to the university course on proof.

## What the chapter covers

The chapter is in five groups, read in order.

1. **The field and the views behind it.** [Teaching foundations of mathematics](/H5BM68GXS9/) surveys the place of logic, sets and proof in teaching, and [philosophy and mathematics in education](/174ES70CS8/) the views of mathematics that shape what a class accepts.
2. **Logic, language, argument and sets.** [Teaching logic](/K1Y2MAQQ9E/), [mathematical language in education](/S19WMS43TW/), [reasoning and proof in the classroom](/E77B813MT4/) and [teaching sets and relations](/12ZNG0NRY7/), including the New Math.
3. **Learning to prove.** [Proof schemes](/YVNRVXJR68/), [transition-to-proof courses](/HQDCSJG379/), [the Toulmin model](/RVZCSFDB2Y/), [student difficulties with proof](/5K2969GSWE/) and [proof comprehension and validation](/52Y2MW27J0/).
4. **Approaches to teaching proof.** [The Moore method and inquiry-based learning](/WKM6535VHG/), [proof in school curricula](/7ZSHDS63QY/) and [generic and pre-formal proofs](/SG9XA69J64/).
5. **Concept images and new tools.** [Concept image and concept definition](/C5S2X0WDJ8/), and [proof assistants in teaching](/KGPHY3F0GC/).

## A first reading

Read [proof schemes](/YVNRVXJR68/), [student difficulties with proof](/5K2969GSWE/), [concept image and concept definition](/C5S2X0WDJ8/), [teaching logic](/K1Y2MAQQ9E/), [generic and pre-formal proofs](/SG9XA69J64/), [the Toulmin model](/RVZCSFDB2Y/) and [transition-to-proof courses](/HQDCSJG379/). These seven give the central ideas of the field and the examples most often cited. A teacher of a university course on proof should add [proof comprehension and validation](/52Y2MW27J0/) and [the Moore method](/WKM6535VHG/); a school teacher should add [proof in school curricula](/7ZSHDS63QY/) and [reasoning and proof in the classroom](/E77B813MT4/).

## Before you start

You need to know what a [mathematical proof](/0B94TKFQJX/) is, [the language of mathematical statements](/WMY13MXY3W/) with its connectives and quantifiers, the [basic proof methods](/958EF46EYJ/), and the practice of [writing and reading proofs](/STSM6PR7TX/). The general theories of learning and the methods of research are in [mathematics education](/9622VKAWMZ/). No mathematics beyond a first course on proof is used, but the exercises ask you to look at arguments as a teacher would.
