1. ◦ Four cards show $E$, $K$, $4$ and $7$. Each card has a letter on one side and a number on the other. Which cards must be turned over to test the rule "if a card has a vowel on one side, then it has an even number on the other"? Most people choose $E$ and $4$. Say what this suggests for [teaching logic](/K1Y2MAQQ9E/).

2. ◦ Five students explain why $n^3 - n$ is divisible by $6$ for every integer $n$. Name the [proof scheme](/YVNRVXJR68/) of each. (a) "I checked $n = 1$ to $20$." (b) "It is a theorem in the textbook." (c) "$n^3 - n = (n - 1)n(n + 1)$, and among three consecutive integers one is even and one is a multiple of $3$." (d) "Base case $n = 1$; assume it for $n$; then it holds for $n + 1$ because the pattern continues." (e) "The bar chart of $n^3 - n$ for $n = 1, \ldots, 6$ goes up in steps of $6$."

3. ◦ Lay out the argument "$91 = 7 \cdot 13$, so $91$ is certainly not prime" in [Toulmin's model](/RVZCSFDB2Y/): name the data, the claim, the warrant, the backing and the qualifier, and say whether a rebuttal is needed.

4. ◦ A student writes: "$0.9, 0.99, 0.999, \ldots$ never reaches $1$, so $0.999\ldots < 1$." Describe the student's [concept image](/C5S2X0WDJ8/) of an infinite decimal, state the concept definition, and use it to show that $0.999\ldots = 1$.

5. ◦ Give the everyday meaning and the mathematical meaning of each word: "or", "any", "odd", "product". For each, write a sentence a student might misread, in the sense of [mathematical language in education](/S19WMS43TW/).

6. ◦ Is "if $n$ is divisible by $4$, then $n$ is even" true at $n = 6$? At $n = 3$? Explain, and describe the "defective" reading of the conditional, under which such cases are neither true nor false.

7. ◦ Asked to prove "if $n^2$ is even, then $n$ is even", a student writes: "Let $n = 2k$. Then $n^2 = 4k^2 = 2(2k^2)$, which is even." What has the student proved? Write a correct proof.

8. ◦ A comprehension test on a proof asks these questions. Say whether each tests local or holistic understanding, in the sense of [proof comprehension and validation](/52Y2MW27J0/). (a) What does "coprime" mean in line 3? (b) Summarize the proof in two sentences. (c) Why does line 5 follow from lines 2 and 4? (d) Use the method of the proof to prove a similar theorem. (e) Is line 4 assumed or deduced? (f) Into what main parts does the proof fall?

9. • Write the negations of "$f$ is continuous at $a$" and "$f$ is bounded on $S$" with all quantifiers explicit, as in [negating statements](/K930V33BSC/). Which student errors does writing the quantifiers out prevent?

10. • Write "every even number greater than $2$ is a sum of two primes" in symbols, with [quantifiers](/EGTZPA6QH3/). Then write the statement obtained by moving the existential quantifiers to the front, and say why it is false.

11. • Give a [generic proof](/SG9XA69J64/) that the product of two consecutive integers is even, using $7 \cdot 8$ as the example, and then a general proof. What must the generic proof say to count as a proof?

12. • Show that $8T_n + 1 = (2n + 1)^2$, where $T_n = 1 + 2 + \cdots + n$, by a picture of eight triangles around a square, and then by algebra. Which version would you give first to a school class, and why?

13. • Fermat conjectured that every $F_n = 2^{2^n} + 1$ is prime. Lay out his argument in Toulmin's model, with the values $F_0, \ldots, F_4$ as data. What is the warrant, what qualifier fits it, and what is the rebuttal?

14. • Show $A \setminus (B \cup C) = (A \setminus B) \cap (A \setminus C)$ by a [Venn diagram](/0KCT6DPXJK/) and by an argument with elements. Which is a proof, and what would a student need to turn the diagram into one?

15. • In a [Moore-style course](/WKM6535VHG/) the students are given the statement "every continuous function on $(0, 1)$ is bounded" and asked to prove it or find a counterexample. Find a counterexample, and change the hypothesis so that the statement becomes a theorem.

16. • Write the truth tables of $P \to Q$, its contrapositive $\neg Q \to \neg P$ and its converse $Q \to P$. Which two agree? Give a statement about integers that is true while its converse is false.

17. • A student says that $f(x) = 1/x$ is not continuous because its graph cannot be drawn without lifting the pencil. Prove from the definition that $f$ is continuous at every point of its domain, and explain the conflict with the student's concept image.

18. • Show that the sum of $k$ consecutive integers is always divisible by $k$ if $k$ is odd, and not always if $k$ is even. Give a generic proof for $k = 5$ and then the general proof.

19. • In a sequence of exercises on divisibility, students are asked to prove "if $a \mid b$ and $b \mid a$, then $a = b$" for integers $a$ and $b$. Find a counterexample, correct the statement, and prove the corrected statement.

20. ★ Define addition on $\N$ by $a + 0 = a$ and $a + S(b) = S(a + b)$, where $S$ is the successor. Prove that $a + b = b + a$ for all $a, b$, proving the lemmas you need by induction. Which steps would a student in a [transition course](/HQDCSJG379/) find hardest?

21. ★ Write the definition of "$f$ is uniformly continuous on $\R$" and its negation with all quantifiers. Prove that $f(x) = x^2$ is continuous at every point but not uniformly continuous on $\R$. Explain why the order of the quantifiers is the whole difference.

22. ★ Prove that $n$ circles in the plane divide it into at most $n^2 - n + 2$ regions. Deduce that no four circles can form a Venn diagram for four sets.

23. ★ Show that for every $N$ there is a polynomial $p$ with integer coefficients such that $p(n) = n^2 + n + 41$ for $n = 0, 1, \ldots, N - 1$ but $p(N)$ is composite. What does this say about an inductive warrant in Toulmin's model?
