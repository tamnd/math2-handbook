1. ◦ Say whether each of these is a heuristic, an algorithm or a proof, in the sense of [heuristics in mathematics](/6KCEWEP4WA/): (a) "compute the first five cases and look for a pattern"; (b) long division of one polynomial by another; (c) "draw a figure"; (d) the remark that $\frac{1}{k(k+1)} = \frac{1}{k} - \frac{1}{k+1}$, so that $\sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{1}{k(k+1)}$ telescopes to $1 - \frac{1}{n+1}$.

2. ◦ Problem: find the radius of the circle through the three vertices of a right triangle with legs $6$ and $8$. Following the first phase of [Pólya's four steps](/DYD57G2Y5Z/), name the unknown, the data and the condition. Then solve the problem.

3. ◦ Claim: $n^2 - n + 11$ is prime for every positive integer $n$. Use [specialization](/FTBJX4G51S/) to find the least $n$ for which the claim fails.

4. ◦ A number is doubled, then $7$ is subtracted, then the result is divided by $3$, and the answer is $9$. Find the number by [working backwards](/3QTB9S1THS/), and say why each backward step is reversible here.

5. ◦ Find all real solutions of $x^6 - 9x^3 + 8 = 0$ through an [auxiliary problem](/DYSJB93V11/) in a new unknown. Is the reduction bilateral or unilateral?

6. ◦ A proof that the angles of an $n$-gon add up to $(n - 2) \cdot 180^\circ$ uses this lemma: from a vertex $V$, the diagonals to the other vertices lie inside the polygon and cut it into $n - 2$ triangles. In the language of [proofs and refutations](/79P9CTRN0V/), is each of these a local counterexample, a global counterexample, or both? (a) A non-convex quadrilateral (a "dart"), with $V$ the vertex opposite the reflex angle. (b) The five-pointed star whose vertices are the five points of a pentagram, taken as a polygon with $5$ vertices.

7. ◦ A student works for two evenings on an inequality and fails. On Saturday she plays football and does not think about it. On the bus home she sees that the inequality between the arithmetic and the geometric mean applies. On Sunday she writes the proof, finds that one case is missing, and fixes it. Match each part of the story with one of Wallas's four stages in the [psychology of mathematical invention](/XMGGTNSTYC/).

8. ◦ A cyclist rides $30$ km at $60$ km/h and returns the same way at $40$ km/h. Intuition says the average speed is $50$ km/h. Compute the true average speed, and say which hidden assumption made the intuition wrong.

9. ◦ Apply the "what if not" question of [problem posing](/Y896GWYF8A/) to the attribute "the angle at $C$ is $90^\circ$" of the Pythagorean theorem $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$. Which theorem answers the new question? What does it give when the angle at $C$ is $60^\circ$?

10. • Compute $1 \cdot 1! + 2 \cdot 2! + \cdots + n \cdot n!$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, 4$, guess a closed form, and prove it. Which heuristic suggested the step that makes the proof short?

11. • A student draws an isosceles triangle with $AB = AC$ and sees that the bisector of the angle at $A$ meets $BC$ at its midpoint. Show that this happens if and only if $AB = AC$, and give side lengths of a triangle in which the bisector divides $BC$ in the ratio $1 : 2$. Compare with [a figure that is too special](/SXA7G1N6HZ/).

12. • Show that a direct induction on "$\sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{1}{k^2} < 2$ for all $n$" gets stuck at the induction step. Then prove the stronger statement $\sum_{k=1}^{n} \frac{1}{k^2} \le 2 - \frac{1}{n}$ by induction. Explain the result in terms of the [inventor's paradox](/FVRKDXBJF6/).

13. • Prove that $\frac{a + b}{2} \ge \sqrt{ab}$ for all real $a, b \ge 0$, first by working backwards from the inequality to a statement you know, then by writing the synthesis forwards. When does equality hold?

14. • Two players take turns. Each in turn adds a whole number from $1$ to $10$ to a running total that starts at $0$, and the player who makes the total exactly $100$ wins. Work backwards to find which player can force a win, and how.

15. • Prove that an angle inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle. Use one [auxiliary element](/8TKKRAXKFN/): the segment from the centre to the vertex of the angle.

16. • Find the last two digits of $2^{100}$ by [decomposing and recombining](/KEZPDNM2NH/): work modulo $4$ and modulo $25$ and combine.

17. • Let $A = (-1, 0)$ and $B = (1, 0)$. Find all points $P$ such that the angle $APB$ is a right angle and $P$ is at distance $\frac{1}{2}$ from the line $AB$. Use [variation of the problem](/KT5SASGTQ5/): first drop the second condition and find the locus of $P$.

18. • Find two numbers whose sum is $8$ and the sum of whose squares is $34$. [Introduce notation](/6CVYB413ER/) that is symmetric in the two numbers, so that the problem becomes an equation in one unknown with no linear term.

19. • Prove by coordinates that the midpoint of the hypotenuse of a right triangle is at the same distance from the three vertices. Choose the coordinates so that the computation is as short as possible, and say why your choice loses no generality.

20. • Conjecture: if $n \mid ab$, then $n \mid a$ or $n \mid b$. A "proof" runs: if $n \nmid a$, then $\gcd(n, a) = 1$, so $n \mid b$ by Bézout's identity. Find a global counterexample, find the lemma it refutes, and state the corrected theorem by incorporating that lemma, as in [proofs and refutations](/79P9CTRN0V/).

21. • An $8 \times 8$ chessboard contains $204$ squares made of whole cells. Pose the variant for a board of $n \times m$ cells with $n \le m$, give a formula for the number of squares, and compute it for a $3 \times 5$ board.

22. ★ In the plane, $n$ lines in general position cut the plane into $1 + n + \binom{n}{2}$ regions. By [analogy](/7KDT0R1HF2/), guess how many regions $n$ planes in general position cut space into. Check the guess for $n \le 3$, and prove it.

23. ★ Let $P_n = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{3}{4} \cdots \frac{2n - 1}{2n}$. Show that a direct induction cannot prove $P_n \le \frac{1}{\sqrt{3n}}$, then prove the stronger $P_n \le \frac{1}{\sqrt{3n + 1}}$ by induction.

24. ★ Compute $\displaystyle\int_0^1 \frac{x^5 - 1}{\ln x}\, dx$ by [generalization](/8NJ0VN9Z97/): replace the exponent $5$ by a parameter $t$ and differentiate with respect to $t$.

25. ★ Euclid proved that there are infinitely many primes by multiplying finitely many of them and adding $1$. By analogy, prove that there are infinitely many irreducible polynomials over the field $\F_2$ of two elements.

26. ★ Prove the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality $\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i b_i\right)^2 \le \left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i^2\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} b_i^2\right)$ for real numbers with an auxiliary function: the quadratic $q(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (a_i t + b_i)^2$. Find when equality holds.
