1. ◦ For each procedure, say whether it is a [heuristic](/6KCEWEP4WA/) or an algorithm, and why: (a) "try the cases $n = 1, 2, 3, 4$ and look for a pattern"; (b) long division of $7$ by $13$ to twenty decimal places; (c) "draw a figure and mark the data on it"; (d) the sieve of Eratosthenes up to $100$; (e) "solve a simpler problem of the same kind first".

2. ◦ A car drives for one hour at $40$ km/h and then for one hour at $60$ km/h. A cyclist rides $12$ km uphill at $12$ km/h and then rides the same $12$ km back down at $24$ km/h. Find the average speed of each trip. Which of the two is the mean of its two speeds, and what condition on the trip makes the mean correct?

3. ◦ Compute $2^p - 1$ for the primes $p = 2, 3, 5, 7$ and check that each value is prime. State the conjecture that this [specialization](/FTBJX4G51S/) to four cases suggests, and find the least prime $p$ for which it fails, with a factorization.

4. ◦ Two circles have the same centre. A chord of the larger circle touches the smaller circle and has length $10$. (a) Guess the area of the ring between the circles by taking the extreme case in which the smaller circle shrinks to its centre. (b) Prove that the guess is right for every pair of circles that fits the data.

5. ◦ You have a $3$-litre jug, a $7$-litre jug and a tap, and you may fill a jug, empty a jug, or pour one jug into the other until one is empty or the other is full. [Work backwards](/3QTB9S1THS/) from the goal to get exactly $5$ litres in the $7$-litre jug, and list your moves.

6. ◦ Solve $(x^2 - 3x)^2 - 2(x^2 - 3x) - 8 = 0$ over $\R$ by introducing an auxiliary unknown, one of the [auxiliary elements](/8TKKRAXKFN/).

7. ◦ Let $A = (0, 3)$ and $B = (4, 5)$. Find the point $P$ on the $x$-axis for which $AP + PB$ is least, and the least value.

8. ◦ A [variation of the problem](/KT5SASGTQ5/) that exchanges hypothesis and conclusion gives the converse. Write the converse of each statement and say whether it is true, with a counterexample if it is false: (a) in a triangle with sides $a \le b \le c$, if the angle opposite $c$ is a right angle then $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$; (b) if a quadrilateral is a rectangle then its diagonals have equal length; (c) for a positive integer $n$, if $6 \mid n$ then the sum of the digits of $n$ is divisible by $3$.

9. ◦ A cube of side $1$ stands in the centre of the top face of a cube of side $3$. The solid has $16$ vertices and $24$ edges; count its faces, taking the top face of the large cube, which has a square hole, as one face. Compute $V - E + F$, and say which assumption of the proof of [Euler's formula](/09HT3P35YP/) $V - E + F = 2$ this solid breaks, in the sense of [proofs and refutations](/79P9CTRN0V/).

10. • Compute $\sum_{k=1}^{n} k \cdot k!$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, 4$, guess a closed form, and prove it without induction by writing each term as a difference.

11. • Starting from $\int_0^\infty e^{-tx}\,dx = 1/t$ for $t > 0$, and assuming that [differentiation under the integral sign](/0AR06W1E1B/) is allowed here, find $\int_0^\infty x^n e^{-x}\,dx$ for every integer $n \ge 0$. Which fixed number in the problem did you replace by a parameter, as in [generalization](/8NJ0VN9Z97/)?

12. • A tetrahedron has three edges at one vertex that are pairwise perpendicular. Let $A$, $B$, $C$ be the areas of the three faces at that vertex and $D$ the area of the fourth face. Guess the relation between $A, B, C, D$ by [analogy](/7KDT0R1HF2/) with Pythagoras' theorem and prove it, putting the vertices at $(0,0,0)$, $(a,0,0)$, $(0,b,0)$, $(0,0,c)$. Check it when $a = b = c = 1$.

13. • Solve $x^4 + (x - 2)^4 = 82$ over $\R$. Choose the auxiliary unknown so that the equation becomes symmetric.

14. • For real $a, b > 0$, prove that $\dfrac{2ab}{a + b} \le \dfrac{a + b}{2}$, with equality exactly when $a = b$. First work backwards from the inequality to one you know, then write the proof forwards and check that every step reverses.

15. • Find the last three digits of $2^{2026}$ by [decomposing](/KEZPDNM2NH/) the problem into one modulo $8$ and one modulo $125$, and recombine the answers.

16. • Find the least positive integer $n$ that leaves remainder $2$ when divided by $3$, remainder $3$ when divided by $4$, remainder $4$ when divided by $5$, and is divisible by $11$. (Look at $n + 1$.)

17. • How many of the integers $1, 2, \dots, 1000$ have no common factor with $30$ other than $1$? Solve the problem for one block of $30$ consecutive integers first.

18. • In the [birthday problem](/0NMWG2PHJ0/), among $n$ people whose birthdays fall independently and uniformly on $365$ days, find the probability that two share a birthday for $n = 22$ and $n = 23$, to three decimal places. Which complementary problem is easier to count?

19. • Let $P_n = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{3}{4} \cdots \dfrac{2n - 1}{2n}$. Show that induction on the claim $P_n \le 1/\sqrt{3n}$ does not go through, and prove the stronger claim $P_n \le 1/\sqrt{3n + 1}$ for all $n \ge 1$ by induction. Explain why this is an instance of the [inventor's paradox](/FVRKDXBJF6/).

20. • Two players take turns adding an integer from $1$ to $10$ to a running total that starts at $0$; the player who makes the total exactly $100$ wins. Work backwards to find which totals a player wants to leave, and give a winning strategy for the first player.

21. ★ Call $n$ planes in space in general position if no two are parallel, every three meet in exactly one point, and no four share a point. Find the number $R(n)$ of regions into which $n$ planes in general position cut space: compute $R(0), \dots, R(3)$, find how many new regions the $n$th plane adds by looking at the lines the earlier planes cut on it, and prove a closed form. Give $R(4)$.

22. ★ A plane graph is a finite graph drawn in the plane without crossings; $F$ counts the regions of the complement, the unbounded one included. Two disjoint triangles give $V - E + F = 6 - 6 + 3 = 3$, a counterexample to "$V - E + F = 2$ for every plane graph". Find the hypothesis that the proof by removing edges uses, and prove the corrected statement $V - E + F = 1 + c$ for a plane graph with $c$ connected components.

23. ★ Prove the [Cauchy–Schwarz inequality](/0RFWW56Y22/) $\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i b_i\right)^2 \le \left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i^2\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n} b_i^2\right)$ for real numbers by introducing the auxiliary quadratic $q(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (a_i t + b_i)^2$. Say when equality holds.

24. ★ In the box $[-2, 2]^n$ place the $2^n$ balls of radius $1$ with centres $(\pm 1, \dots, \pm 1)$, and the largest ball with centre $0$ that does not overlap them. Find its radius $r_n$, check it for $n = 2$ with a figure, and find the least $n$ for which the central ball is not contained in the box.

25. ★ Let $n \ge 1$ be an integer. Find $I = \displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{\sin^n x}{\sin^n x + \cos^n x}\,dx$ by introducing the auxiliary integral $J$ with $\cos^n x$ in the numerator, computing $I + J$, and comparing $I$ with $J$ by the substitution $x \mapsto \pi/2 - x$.
