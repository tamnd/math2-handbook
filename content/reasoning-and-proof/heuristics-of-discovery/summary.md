## What to remember

- A **heuristic** is a rule of thumb for finding a solution or a proof. It differs from an algorithm, which always ends with a correct answer, and from a proof, which shows that a statement is true. What a heuristic produces is a guess or a plan, and it stays a [conjecture](/WMEMMHZ3BH/) until it is proved or refuted.
- **Pólya's four steps**: understand the problem (the unknown, the data and the condition; or the hypothesis and the conclusion), devise a plan, carry out the plan and check each step, and look back.
- **Specialization** passes to small, extreme or special cases. One special case can refute a universal statement; no number of special cases proves one.
- **Generalization** replaces the problem by a more general one, often by turning a number into a parameter. By the **inventor's paradox**, the stronger problem can be the easier one, because it gives more to work with; this is why a stronger induction hypothesis can make an induction succeed.
- **Working backwards** starts from the goal and asks what would come just before it. For a problem to prove, the chain must then be run forwards, and each step must be checked, since a backward step need not be reversible.
- An **auxiliary problem** is solved because it helps with the given one. A bilateral reduction passes between equivalent problems; a unilateral reduction passes to a problem that is more ambitious or less ambitious than the original.
- In Lakatos's **proofs and refutations**, a *global* counterexample refutes the conjecture and a *local* counterexample refutes a lemma of the proof. Proof analysis turns the refuted lemma into a hypothesis, and new concepts often come from such hypotheses.
- **Invention**, in the reports of Poincaré and Hadamard, often follows Wallas's four stages: preparation, incubation, illumination, verification. The evidence is mostly the self-reports of successful mathematicians.

## Using heuristics well

- **Separate finding from proving.** Write the guess as a guess. Then prove it, or test it on a case unlike the ones that suggested it.
- **When stuck, change the problem.** Specialize to see what is going on, generalize when a parameter or a stronger hypothesis would help, or drop a condition and see what remains.
- **Check every backward step.** Working backwards finds a route; only the forward argument, step by step, is a proof.
- **Distrust a special figure.** Draw the general case, with no equal sides or right angles that the problem does not give.
- **Know when to stop.** A heuristic that has failed several times should be put aside. The four steps, and the reports on invention, both say: look back, take a break, and try another plan.
