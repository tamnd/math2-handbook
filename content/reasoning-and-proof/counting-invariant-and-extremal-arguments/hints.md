1. $16$. Sort by remainder on division by $7$: remainder $0$ has $4$ numbers, remainders $1$ and $2$ have $5$ each, and remainders $3, 4, 5, 6$ have $4$ each. A sum is divisible by $7$ exactly when the remainders are $\set{0, 0}$, $\set{1, 6}$, $\set{2, 5}$ or $\set{3, 4}$. A set with no such pair takes at most one number with remainder $0$ and, from each pair of remainders, numbers of one remainder only, so it has at most $1 + 5 + 5 + 4 = 15$ elements. The set $\set{7, 1, 8, 15, 22, 29, 2, 9, 16, 23, 30, 3, 10, 17, 24}$ reaches $15$.

16. Use the sum of the squares of the four amounts. A move from $a$ coins to a neighbour with $b \le a - 2$ coins changes it by $(a - 1)^2 + (b + 1)^2 - a^2 - b^2 = -2(a - b - 1) \le -2$. It starts at $64$, and since the amounts add up to $8$ it is always at least $8^2/4 = 16$. So there are at most $(64 - 16)/2 = 24$ moves. A search over all runs shows that the longest run has $9$ moves and that every run ends at $3, 2, 2, 1$.

21. For the difference $d$ the first term $a$ satisfies $1 \le a \le 100 - 9d$, so $d \le 11$ and there are $\sum_{d=1}^{11} (100 - 9d) = 1100 - 594 = 506$ progressions. A fixed progression has one colour in $2 \cdot 2^{90}$ of the $2^{100}$ colourings, so at most $506 \cdot 2^{91}$ colourings are bad, and this is less than $2^{100}$ because $506 < 2^9 = 512$.

23. A path that goes above $y = x$ meets the line $y = x + 1$. Reflect the part after its first meeting point in that line, which swaps right and up steps. The result is a path from $(0, 0)$ to $(n - 1, n + 1)$, every such path meets the line, and reflecting back inverts the map. So the bad paths number $\binom{2n}{n+1}$. For $n = 4$ the count is $70 - 56 = 14$.

24. Colour the cells like a chessboard. A move swaps the blank with one tile, so it changes the parity of the arrangement, read as a permutation of the six symbols, and it moves the blank to a cell of the other colour. So the sum of the permutation parity and the colour of the blank is invariant, and at most $720/2 = 360$ arrangements can be reached. Swapping two tiles changes the parity and keeps the blank where it was. A computer search confirms that exactly $360$ arrangements are reachable.

25. There are $2^{10} - 1 = 1023$ nonempty subsets, and each has a sum at most $90 + 91 + \cdots + 99 = 945$. So two different subsets have the same sum. Neither contains the other, since the extra elements would add a positive amount, so removing the common elements leaves two disjoint nonempty sets with equal sums.

26. Let $A, B$ be at the least distance. No point of $S$ lies on the segment $AB$ apart from $A$ and $B$, and some points lie off the line $AB$; among them choose $C$ with the angle $ACB$ largest. This angle is less than $90^\circ$, since otherwise $AB$ would be the longest side of the triangle and $AC < AB$. A point $D$ inside the circle on the same side of $AB$ as $C$ gives a larger angle $ADB$. A point inside on the other side gives $\angle ADB > 180^\circ - \angle ACB > 90^\circ$, so $AD < AB$. Points of the line $AB$ outside the segment are outside the circle.

27. A fixed pair $u, v$ is bad when each of the other $n - 2$ players lost to $u$ or to $v$; this holds in a fraction $(3/4)^{n-2}$ of all tournaments, since different games are independent choices. So the bad tournaments are at most a fraction $\binom{n}{2} (3/4)^{n-2}$ of all. For $n = 21$ this is $210 \cdot (3/4)^{19} \approx 0.89 < 1$. For $n = 20$ it is $190 \cdot (3/4)^{18} \approx 1.07$, and the count proves nothing, although a tournament with the property exists already for $7$ players: let $i$ beat $j$ when $j - i$ leaves remainder $1$, $2$ or $4$ on division by $7$.
