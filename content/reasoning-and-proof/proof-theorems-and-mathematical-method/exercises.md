1. ◦ The values of $n^2 - n + 41$ are prime for many small $n \geq 0$. Find the least $n \geq 0$ for which the value is not prime, and factor it.

2. ◦ Say whether each argument is deductive, inductive or abductive (see [the three kinds of reasoning](/5V35SGEN1T/)). (a) Every multiple of $6$ is a multiple of $3$, and $42$ is a multiple of $6$, so $42$ is a multiple of $3$. (b) $1 + 2 = 2^2 - 1$, $1 + 2 + 4 = 2^3 - 1$ and $1 + 2 + 4 + 8 = 2^4 - 1$, so $1 + 2 + \cdots + 2^k = 2^{k+1} - 1$ for every $k$. (c) A computed area came out negative, so the limits of the integral were probably written in the wrong order.

3. ◦ A student writes: "If $x > 3$ then $x^2 > 9$. Here $x^2 > 9$, so $x > 3$." Name the error and give a real number $x$ that shows the conclusion can fail.

4. ◦ For each statement, say whether it is a theorem, a refuted conjecture or an open conjecture today: [Goldbach's conjecture](/063JNBSMJ0/), [Fermat's Last Theorem](/0Y5X3SDBCZ/), [Euler's sum of powers conjecture](/E486QA4JS4/), the [Kepler conjecture](/AFMBQT06ZV/), the [Mertens conjecture](/4Y4HWKA2MP/), the [Poincaré conjecture](/3R064KFF68/), the [twin prime conjecture](/0FER36N8SB/).

5. ◦ Take the statement: if $f \colon [a, b] \to \R$ is continuous and $f(a) < 0 < f(b)$, then $f(c) = 0$ for some $c \in (a, b)$. List its hypotheses and its conclusion. Then give a function on $[-1, 1]$ that satisfies every hypothesis except continuity and has no zero.

6. ◦ The entry on [hypotheses and conclusion](/W48XN2MEM9/) proves $2^n > n^2$ for all $n \geq 5$. For which positive integers $n$ does the weaker inequality $2^n \geq n^2$ hold?

7. ◦ Use [Fermat's little theorem](/0M25PRN3WQ/) to find the remainder of $3^{100}$ on division by $7$.

8. ◦ Compute $V - E + F$ for the octahedron, the triangular prism and the square pyramid. What do you notice?

9. • Prove that the product of two odd integers is odd. Name the justification of each step: a definition, a rule of algebra, or a closure property of the integers.

10. • Prove that $6$ divides $n^3 - n$ for every integer $n$, without induction.

11. • The numbers $2^p - 1$ for the primes $p = 2, 3, 5, 7$ are $3, 7, 31, 127$, all prime. Find the least prime $p$ for which $2^p - 1$ is not prime, and factor it. Then prove the true statement in the other direction: if $2^n - 1$ is prime, then $n$ is prime.

12. • The mean value theorem says: if $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$ and differentiable on $(a, b)$, then $f'(c) = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}$ for some $c \in (a, b)$. For each of the two hypotheses, give a function that satisfies the other hypothesis but not the conclusion.

13. • The cyclic group $\Z/6$ and the symmetric group $S_3$ both have six elements. Find two [invariants](/9HQ3TADPA8/) whose values show that they are not isomorphic.

14. • Remove two opposite corner squares from an $8 \times 8$ chessboard. Can the remaining $62$ squares be covered by $31$ dominoes, each covering two adjacent squares? Prove your answer with an invariant.

15. • From the [group](/G5S1M9/) axioms, prove that $(ab)^{-1} = b^{-1}a^{-1}$ in every group, and say which axiom each step uses. Then give two elements $a, b$ of $S_3$ with $(ab)^{-1} \neq a^{-1}b^{-1}$.

16. • [Lagrange's theorem](/3C53TD/), applied to the group of units modulo $15$, gives $a^8 \equiv 1 \pmod{15}$ for every $a$ coprime to $15$. Show that in fact $a^4 \equiv 1 \pmod{15}$ for every such $a$.

17. • Give two proofs that $1 + 2 + 4 + \cdots + 2^{n-1} = 2^n - 1$ for every $n \geq 1$: one by [induction](/0ASJVEFA9J/), and one that computes $2S - S$ for the sum $S$. Which of the two would let you find the formula if you did not know it?

18. • A student generalizes [Euclid's lemma](/051VYKA1EX/) to: "if $n \mid ab$ and $n \nmid a$, then $n \mid b$", for every integer $n \geq 2$. Give a counterexample, and state a hypothesis on $n$ and $a$ that makes the statement true for every $n \geq 2$.

19. ★ Prove that for a positive integer $n$, the number $\sqrt n$ is rational only if $n$ is a perfect square. Use the [fundamental theorem of arithmetic](/084HH1NDCB/).

20. ★ Find all positive integers $n$ with $3^n \leq n^3$, and prove that $3^n > n^3$ for every other positive integer $n$.

21. ★ Prove that a $10 \times 10$ board cannot be covered by $25$ tiles of size $1 \times 4$, each placed along a row or a column.

22. ★ Prove that no polynomial $f$ with integer coefficients, other than a constant, takes a prime value at every integer $n \geq 0$. This shows at once that $n^2 + n + 41$ had to fail somewhere.

23. ★ A finite geometry has $v \geq 3$ points and satisfies two axioms: every two distinct points lie on exactly one line, and every line has exactly three points. Find the number of lines in terms of $v$, and prove that $v \equiv 1$ or $3 \pmod 6$.
