20. Call a number losing if it leaves remainder $0$ or $2$ on division by $7$. From remainder $0$ the three moves give remainders $6, 4, 3$, and from remainder $2$ they give $1, 6, 5$, so a losing position leads only to winning ones. From remainders $1, 3, 4, 5, 6$ the moves $1, 1, 4, 3, 4$ lead to a losing position. The induction must be strong, because a move can go back $3$ or $4$ steps. 22. Prove by induction on $n$ the stronger claim $Q(n)$: $f(m) \ge n$ for every $m \ge n$. If $Q(n)$ holds and $m \ge n + 1$, then $f(m - 1) \ge n$, so $Q(n)$ applies to $f(m - 1)$ and gives $f(f(m - 1)) \ge n$; hence $f(m) > n$. So $f(n) \ge n$ for every $n$, and then $f(n + 1) > f(f(n)) \ge f(n)$, so $f$ is strictly increasing. If $f(n) \ge n + 1$ for some $n$, then $f(f(n)) \ge f(n + 1)$, which contradicts the hypothesis.

23. Use strong induction. If $n = 2m$, double every term of a sum for $m$. If $n$ is odd, let $3^b$ be the largest power of $3$ with $3^b \le n$. Then $n - 3^b$ is even and less than $2 \cdot 3^b$; double a sum for $(n - 3^b)/2$ and add the term $3^b$. The doubled terms are even, so none of them divides $3^b$, and each is less than $2 \cdot 3^b$, so $3^b$ divides none of them. For $100$ the method gives $100 = 36 + 64$.

26. The root $a$ of $x^2 - kbx + b^2 - k = 0$ has the partner $a' = kb - a = (b^2 - k)/a$, an integer. If $a' < 0$, then $a'^2 - kba' + b^2 - k \ge a'^2 + b^2 > 0$, which is impossible for a root.
