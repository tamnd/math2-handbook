Two textbooks state the same sentence: "every increasing function $f \colon \R \to \R$ is injective". The first proves it in two lines. The second sets it as an exercise to be refuted and gives the answer $f(x) = \floor{x}$, which takes the value $0$ at both $0$ and $\tfrac12$. Neither book contains an error. In the first, "increasing" means $x < y \Rightarrow f(x) < f(y)$; in the second it means $x < y \Rightarrow f(x) \le f(y)$. The disagreement is entirely in the notation and the vocabulary, and a reader who does not check the definition can believe a false theorem or reject a true one.

The same thing happens with phrases that sound informal. Read "almost all natural numbers are composite". If "almost all" means "all but finitely many", the sentence is false, because there are infinitely many primes. If it means "all except a set of density zero", the sentence is true, because the proportion of primes up to $n$ tends to $0$. The words are the same and the two readings give opposite truth values. A third example: the statement "$2^n > n^3$ for sufficiently large $n$" hides the two quantifiers $\exists N \; \forall n \ge N$, and the claim that the threshold $N = 10$ is sharp is a further statement, that $2^9 = 512 < 729 = 9^3$.

This chapter teaches you to read such sentences exactly and to write them so that they have only one reading. It treats signs first, then the words that appear inside proofs, then the phrases that carry a precise hidden definition, and finally the words that judge results and proofs.

## What the chapter covers

1. **Signs, layout and the conventions behind them.** How [mathematical notation](/QKEX4PD029/) is read, with bound and free letters and precedence; the [conflicting conventions](/CSFEP56A7D/) for $\N$, $\subset$, $f^2$ and $\log$; [abuse of notation](/JV68YRB5YZ/) and when it is safe; and the different [equality, definition and equivalence signs](/BCGR3NPPXC/), with a [glossary of symbols](/2PTCAGCWZE/).
2. **The words of proofs.** The [abbreviations](/4ZNYMYRRY3/) iff, i.e., resp., wlog and TFAE; the [jargon](/4M1FGQ0HNZ/) of talks and papers; and what [trivial, obvious and clearly](/Y0HXCJRB9B/) do and do not license.
3. **Phrases with a hidden definition.** [Canonical and natural](/F8WNADZFMM/) maps and canonical forms; [almost all, generic and in general](/N1XSAXCQY3/), with the senses of "negligible"; and [sufficiently large, eventually and frequently](/FEQ7WATMHF/) as quantifier statements.
4. **Comparing results.** [Stronger and weaker statements](/KCK4AQ469Q/), [sharp and best possible results](/KWQV0G0VJ3/), and what makes a proof [elementary](/84SZZBACP3/).
5. **Judgements, and where a proof ends.** [Pathological objects](/WMBZPYPN2D/), the words that judge proofs, such as [deep and brute force](/STHWD1K9AN/), and the [end of proof marks](/ET9J6R9T5C/).
