1. ◦ Evaluate each expression with the standard conventions of [mathematical notation](/QKEX4PD029/): (a) $-2^2 + (-2)^2$; (b) $2^{3^2}$ and $(2^3)^2$; (c) $12/3/2$; (d) $\sum_{k=0}^{3} 2^k$.

2. ◦ In each expression, list the free letters and the bound letters. (a) $\sum_{k=1}^{n} k x^k$. (b) $\int_0^x t^2 \, dt$. (c) $\set{y \in \R : y > a}$. (d) $\forall \varepsilon > 0 \; \exists \delta > 0 : \abs{x - a} < \delta \Rightarrow \abs{f(x) - f(a)} < \varepsilon$. Then evaluate (a) at $n = 3$, $x = 2$ and (b) at $x = 3$.

3. ◦ Let $A = \set{n \in \N : n < 4}$. (a) How many elements does $A$ have if $0 \in \N$, and how many if not? (b) Is $\set{1, 2} \subset \set{1, 2}$ true when $\subset$ means "subset", and when it means "proper subset"? (c) Give $\log 1000$, to three decimal places, when $\log$ has base $10$, base $e$ and base $2$. Which [conflicting conventions](/CSFEP56A7D/) are involved?

4. ◦ Let $f \colon \R \to \R$, $f(x) = 3x - 1$. Compute $f^2(2)$ when $f^2$ means $f \circ f$ and when it means $x \mapsto f(x)^2$. Then compute $f^{-1}(5)$ when $f^{-1}$ is the inverse function and when $f^{-1}(5)$ is read as $1/f(5)$, and give the preimage $f^{-1}(\set{5})$.

5. ◦ Decide whether each use of "i.e." is correct, in the sense of [mathematical abbreviations](/4ZNYMYRRY3/), and correct the wrong ones. (a) "$n$ is divisible by $6$, i.e. by both $2$ and $3$." (b) "$x$ is a positive real number, i.e. $x^2 > 0$." (c) "Take the real solutions of $x^2 = 9$, i.e. $x = 3$." (d) "$a$ and $b$ are coprime, i.e. $\gcd(a, b) = 1$."

6. ◦ In each sentence, say whether "trivial" is used in a technical sense or as a judgement of effort, as in [trivial, obvious and clearly](/Y0HXCJRB9B/). (a) "The trivial subgroup of $\Z/6$." (b) "The proof of the lemma is trivial." (c) "The equation $x^3 = 4x$ has the trivial solution $x = 0$." For (a) give the subgroup, and for (c) find the nontrivial real solutions.

7. ◦ For each property $P(n)$ of $n \in \N$, decide whether it holds [for all sufficiently large](/FEQ7WATMHF/) $n$, frequently but not eventually, or neither. When it holds eventually, give the least threshold. (a) $n^2 > 50n$. (b) $n \equiv 2 \pmod 5$. (c) $n < 1000$. (d) The last decimal digit of $2^n$ is $6$.

8. ◦ For each pair, say whether the first condition is strictly [stronger](/KCK4AQ469Q/) than the second, strictly weaker, equivalent to it, or incomparable, and justify with a proof or a counterexample. (a) $x > 2$ and $x^2 > 4$, for real $x$. (b) $4 \mid n$ and $16 \mid n^2$, for integers $n$. (c) "$n$ is a prime greater than $2$" and "$n$ is odd", for integers $n \ge 1$. (d) "$x$ is rational" and "$x^2$ is rational", for real $x$.

9. ◦ (a) Is the bound $x(1 - x) \le \tfrac13$ true for every real $x$? Is the constant $\tfrac13$ [sharp](/KWQV0G0VJ3/)? Find the sharp constant and say whether it is attained. (b) Find the least constant $C$ with $\dfrac{x}{1 + x} \le C$ for every $x \in (0, 1)$, and say whether it is attained.

10. ◦ In the sense of [almost all](/N1XSAXCQY3/), decide whether each statement about $n \in \N$ is true in the cofinite sense, in the density sense, in both or in neither. (a) Almost all $n$ have at least two decimal digits. (b) Almost all $n$ are not powers of $2$. (c) Almost all $n$ satisfy $n \not\equiv 3 \pmod 4$. Then (d): is "almost every real $x$ satisfies $\sin x \ne 0$" true in the countable sense, the measure sense and the topological sense?

11. • Say whether each construction is [canonical](/F8WNADZFMM/), and prove your answer. (a) For sets $A$ and $B$, the map $A \times B \to B \times A$, $(a, b) \mapsto (b, a)$. (b) For a linear map $T \colon V \to V$ on a finite-dimensional real vector space, the determinant of the matrix of $T$ in a chosen basis. (c) For a bilinear form $\beta$ on $V$, the trace of the matrix $(\beta(b_i, b_j))$ in a chosen basis $b_1, \dots, b_n$; test it with $V = \R^2$, $\beta$ the dot product, and the bases $e_1, e_2$ and $2e_1, e_2$. (d) For a subspace $W$ of $V$, the map $V \to V/W$, $v \mapsto v + W$.

12. • Prove that if $P(n)$ and $Q(n)$ each hold for all sufficiently large $n$, then "$P(n)$ and $Q(n)$" holds for all sufficiently large $n$. Give properties $P$ and $Q$ that both hold frequently while "$P(n)$ and $Q(n)$" holds for no $n$. Finally prove that the negation of "eventually $P(n)$" is "frequently not $P(n)$".

13. • Find every $n \in \N$ with $2^n > n^4$. Prove your answer for large $n$ by showing that $2^n > n^4$ and $n \ge 17$ imply $2^{n+1} > (n + 1)^4$.

14. • A student writes: "$n = O(n^2)$ and $n^2 = O(n^2)$, so $n = n^2$." Explain the error, using the reading of $=$ in [equality, definition and equivalence signs](/BCGR3NPPXC/). Then prove that $f = O(g)$ and $g = O(h)$ imply $f = O(h)$, where $f = O(g)$ means that there are $C > 0$ and $N$ with $\abs{f(n)} \le C \abs{g(n)}$ for all $n \ge N$.

15. • Decide which rules define functions, in the sense of [abuse of notation](/JV68YRB5YZ/): the rule must give the same value for every representative of a class. Use representatives $a \in \N$. (a) $\Z/n \to \Z/n$, $[a] \mapsto [a^2]$, for every $n \ge 1$. (b) $\Z/4 \to \Z/5$, $[a] \mapsto [3^a]$. (c) $\Z/4 \to \Z/7$, $[a] \mapsto [2^a]$.

16. • Decide whether almost all natural numbers, in the density sense, are divisible by at least one of $2, 3, 5, 7$. Compute the density of the exceptions and prove that it exists.

17. • The function $g$ below is a standard [pathological](/WMBZPYPN2D/) example. Let $g(x) = 1$ for rational $x$ and $g(x) = 0$ for irrational $x$, and let $h(x) = x \, g(x)$. Prove that $g$ is discontinuous at every real number, and that $h$ is continuous at exactly one point.

18. • Let $T$ be the theorem "if $a$ and $b$ are odd integers, then $a + b$ is even". For each statement below, say whether it comes from $T$ by weakening the hypothesis, by strengthening the conclusion, by an equivalent rewording, or in none of these ways, and whether it is true. (a) If $a$ and $b$ have the same parity, then $a + b$ is even. (b) If $a$ and $b$ are odd, then $4 \mid a + b$. (c) If $a$ and $b$ are odd, then $a + b + 1$ is odd. (d) If $a + b$ is even, then $a$ and $b$ are odd.

19. • Prove that $ab + bc + ca \le a^2 + b^2 + c^2$ for all real $a, b, c$. Show that the constant $1$ in front of $a^2 + b^2 + c^2$ is sharp, and find all cases of equality.

20. • Write "for all sufficiently small $\varepsilon > 0$, $\varepsilon^2 < \varepsilon / 100$" with explicit quantifiers, prove it, and find the largest $\delta$ such that the inequality holds for every $\varepsilon$ with $0 < \varepsilon < \delta$.

21. • Call two words equivalent when one is a rearrangement of the letters of the other. Prove that the rule "write the letters in alphabetical order" is a canonical form for this equivalence. Use it to sort the words LISTEN, SILENT, TINSEL, LISTED and INLETS into classes.

22. ★ Prove that almost all natural numbers contain the digit $7$ in their decimal expansion in the density sense, and that the statement is false in the cofinite sense.

23. ★ Prove that "for every $k \in \N$, $n^k < 2^n$ for all sufficiently large $n$" is true, and that "for all sufficiently large $n$, $n^k < 2^n$ for every $k \in \N$" is false. Which quantifiers have been exchanged?

24. ★ Let $f(x) = x^2 \sin(1/x)$ for $x \ne 0$ and $f(0) = 0$. Prove that $f$ is differentiable at every real number, and that $f'$ is not continuous at $0$.

25. ★ On $\N \times \N$ let $(a, b) \sim (c, d)$ when $a + d = b + c$. Prove that $\sim$ is an equivalence relation, and that $c(a, b) = (a - b, 0)$ for $a \ge b$ and $c(a, b) = (0, b - a)$ for $a < b$ is a canonical form for it. Use the form to decide whether $(3, 8) \sim (10, 15)$ and whether $(7, 2) \sim (2, 7)$.

26. ★ Find the largest constant $C$ such that $(a + b + c)\left(\dfrac1a + \dfrac1b + \dfrac1c\right) \ge C$ for all positive reals $a, b, c$. Prove that it is sharp, and find all cases of equality.
