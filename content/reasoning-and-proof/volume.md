A [mathematical proof](/0B94TKFQJX/) is an argument that shows that a statement follows from axioms, definitions and results proved before. Proof is what separates mathematics from the sciences that test claims by experiment. The polynomial $n^2 + n + 41$ is prime for $n = 0, 1, \dots, 39$, and these forty cases still prove nothing; at $n = 40$ it equals $41^2$. A claim checked in a million cases is a conjecture, and a claim with a correct proof is a theorem for all time. Every later volume of this book consists of definitions, theorems and proofs, so the skills of this volume are used on every page.

This volume treats proof as mathematicians practise it, not as an object of formal logic; that is the subject of [Volume 2](/DYJ0KGTPMR/). It asks four kinds of questions. The first is about language: what does a statement say, and how do "if", "or", "for all" and "there exists" work in mathematical English? The second is about method: which standard patterns of argument exist, and which one fits a given statement? The third is about writing: how does one present a proof so that a reader can check each step? The fourth is about discovery: how does one find a conjecture and a proof before one can write either?

## What the volume teaches

- **Reading statements.** Every statement has [hypotheses and a conclusion](/W48XN2MEM9/), and each hypothesis is there for a reason. A [conditional statement](/FA6YZGR2SS/) and its [quantifiers](/EGTZPA6QH3/) fix what must be proved, and [negating a statement](/K930V33BSC/) correctly is the first step of half the methods.
- **Definitions that do work.** A [definition](/8ZCZ3HW91Z/) can hide a claim. A function defined on representatives must be shown [well-defined](/HNGZEBKVY2/), and a definition with "the" needs existence and uniqueness.
- **A small set of methods.** [Direct proof](/0NTF6DMVAP/), [contrapositive](/0BB5TPGQ1N/), [contradiction](/0J0XBKG64S/), [cases](/0WCP0PVET6/), construction and [counterexample](/0ED72CTBNP/) cover most of what is written. The form of the goal suggests the method, as the map of [proof methods](/G9JFJGY4TQ/) shows.
- **Induction and its relatives.** [Mathematical induction](/0ASJVEFA9J/), the [well-ordering principle](/01BX15HSRR/) and infinite descent are three forms of one idea, and [structural induction](/12MQ2NJ29A/) carries it to trees and formulas.
- **Combinatorial arguments.** The [pigeonhole principle](/0HGVCWQGY3/), [double counting](/0F8YBHF2W3/), [invariants](/8NQ190278Y/) and the [extremal principle](/MD3MRG90W4/) solve problems in every field.
- **Proof as communication.** A proof is written for a reader. [Writing proofs](/8JQHJK94RZ/) and [checking a proof](/4M98ZZNK0E/) are skills to practise, and the [common fallacies](/S2BW0FDHV8/) are worth knowing by name.
- **Discovery is not proof.** [Pólya's four steps](/DYD57G2Y5Z/), [experimental mathematics](/BPEPTN0TT9/) and [Lakatos's proofs and refutations](/79P9CTRN0V/) describe how results are found. Plausible reasoning guides the search but proves nothing.

## The chapters

**Chapter 1.1, Proof, theorems and mathematical method**, explains what a [proof](/0B94TKFQJX/) is, how [deduction differs from induction and abduction](/5V35SGEN1T/), what the labels [theorem, lemma, corollary and proposition](/KK5F0F03ZW/) signal, and what the [hypotheses and conclusion](/W48XN2MEM9/) of a theorem are. It treats the [axiomatic method](/GD2JBXJV5Z/), [conjectures](/WMEMMHZ3BH/), the [functions of proof](/ENY0GKRJS8/) beyond verification, the [methodology of mathematics](/3FJ3XMV9KD/), the uses of examples, abstraction and [classification](/9HQ3TADPA8/) in mathematical practice, and the two temperaments of [problem solvers and theory builders](/Z29HM45R29/).

**Chapter 1.2, The language of mathematical statements**, reads [statements](/S9MHMRAX88/) word by word: connectives, [conditionals](/FA6YZGR2SS/), [quantifiers](/EGTZPA6QH3/), [negation](/K930V33BSC/), [existence and uniqueness](/ACSC75R7R5/) and the ambiguities of mathematical English. It ends with the [Wason selection task](/7GYZJ2MGY9/), a test of how people read a conditional.

**Chapter 1.3, Definitions in mathematics**, sets out the logical form of a [definition](/8ZCZ3HW91Z/), its kinds, the duty to prove that a definition is [well-defined](/HNGZEBKVY2/), [equivalent definitions](/NZ42PS2P1A/), the phrase [up to](/CBC72TX3NQ/), [definite descriptions](/5P955TVSS2/), the [primitive notions](/QMQEJKFEYZ/) that every definition rests on, and why good definitions take the form they do.

**Chapter 1.4, Notation, conventions and jargon**, collects the symbols, the [conflicting conventions](/CSFEP56A7D/), the safe [abuses of notation](/JV68YRB5YZ/), and the informal words of the trade, from [trivial and obvious](/Y0HXCJRB9B/) to [sufficiently large](/FEQ7WATMHF/) and [sharp](/KWQV0G0VJ3/).

**Chapter 1.5, Basic proof methods**, is the core of a transition course. After the map of [proof methods](/G9JFJGY4TQ/) come [direct proof](/0NTF6DMVAP/), [contrapositive](/0BB5TPGQ1N/), [contradiction](/0J0XBKG64S/), [cases](/0WCP0PVET6/), [constructive](/01XM7JEKHY/) and [non-constructive](/JZQVJ9XT63/) existence, uniqueness, [counterexamples](/0ED72CTBNP/), [without loss of generality](/Y7NE0E6PCV/) and the [forward-backward method](/V8ZB838JY5/).

**Chapter 1.6, Induction and well-ordering as proof methods**, covers [induction](/0ASJVEFA9J/), [strong induction](/0KMVFSHFGZ/), [well-ordering](/01BX15HSRR/) and their [equivalence](/J2P9FWBP3Q/), [minimal counterexamples](/JGRCNPKNGG/), [infinite descent](/0EFYCFHYFG/), [strengthening the hypothesis](/F94KJC2RXP/), [structural induction](/12MQ2NJ29A/), and the [errors](/V52HDTMPV5/) that the [horses paradox](/AJ2FDJJGTH/) makes famous.

**Chapter 1.7, Counting, invariant and extremal arguments**, treats the [pigeonhole principle](/0HGVCWQGY3/) in its forms, [double counting](/0F8YBHF2W3/) and [bijective proof](/SS07J9ZAQ7/), [invariants](/8NQ190278Y/) and [monovariants](/TP3GC5J08D/), parity and [colouring](/90Q4PK1CST/), the [extremal principle](/MD3MRG90W4/), averaging, and [diagonalization](/V2H30SA654/).

**Chapter 1.8, Proof patterns for common goals**, matches templates to goals: [set equality by double inclusion](/MKHVRNW68H/), [injectivity and surjectivity](/DD2P85Y8E9/), [equivalence relations](/RGNF33GVHX/), [epsilon-delta proofs](/RXG5JTZ2PZ/), density and open-closed arguments, [bootstrapping](/YKA0ADKYFR/) and [proof by algorithm](/X5HG1RNWQX/).

**Chapter 1.9, Writing and reading proofs**, is about the craft: [writing proofs](/8JQHJK94RZ/), [scratch work](/E97XXFW9MV/), [strategies by logical form](/872K5XB580/), [unpacking definitions](/41JZ39GE3F/), the [level of detail](/VYGBV5D8Y4/), [reading proofs](/40GCSM2G56/) and [checking a proof](/4M98ZZNK0E/).

**Chapter 1.10, Fallacies and errors in proofs**, shows what goes wrong: [division by zero](/V7601AG5G3/), roots and powers out of range, [circular reasoning](/JPHE53PABT/), [affirming the consequent](/J7KFCFQRBT/), [proof by example](/ZX6ERT3SW7/), the [quantifier shift fallacy](/CRW82NARQV/) and [famous incorrect proofs](/1CV4HTA6C8/).

**Chapter 1.11, Heuristics of discovery**, follows Pólya: [the four steps](/DYD57G2Y5Z/), [analogy](/7KDT0R1HF2/), [specialization](/FTBJX4G51S/), [generalization](/8NJ0VN9Z97/), [working backwards](/3QTB9S1THS/), auxiliary problems and elements, and Lakatos's [proofs and refutations](/79P9CTRN0V/).

**Chapter 1.12, Plausible reasoning and experimental mathematics**, explains [plausible reasoning](/8DHD3H1CJ9/), [experimental mathematics](/BPEPTN0TT9/), what [numerical evidence](/JFD3QWCX8Z/) is worth, the [strong law of small numbers](/8AFXPXCE3N/) and [patterns that eventually fail](/A2KSJSQSF6/).

**Chapter 1.13, Rigour and the acceptance of proofs**, asks what [rigour](/E1PE9R8DBB/) is, how [formal and informal proofs](/8VZ77R4R8S/) relate, and how [computer-assisted](/MC18C7BMFT/) and [very long proofs](/RM0S65BHWH/) come to be [accepted](/MJ3EHQJJHX/).

**Chapter 1.14, Teaching reasoning and proof**, is for teachers: [proof schemes](/YVNRVXJR68/), [transition-to-proof courses](/HQDCSJG379/), [student difficulties](/5K2969GSWE/), the [Moore method](/WKM6535VHG/), [concept image and concept definition](/C5S2X0WDJ8/) and [proof assistants in teaching](/KGPHY3F0GC/).

## How to read this volume

If this is your first course on proof, read these entries in this order: [mathematical proof](/0B94TKFQJX/), [deductive, inductive and abductive reasoning](/5V35SGEN1T/), [hypotheses and conclusion](/W48XN2MEM9/), [mathematical statement](/S9MHMRAX88/), [conditional statements](/FA6YZGR2SS/), [quantifiers](/EGTZPA6QH3/), [negating statements](/K930V33BSC/), [mathematical definition](/8ZCZ3HW91Z/), [well-defined](/HNGZEBKVY2/), [direct proof](/0NTF6DMVAP/), [proof by contrapositive](/0BB5TPGQ1N/), [proof by contradiction](/0J0XBKG64S/), [proof by cases](/0WCP0PVET6/), [counterexample](/0ED72CTBNP/), [mathematical induction](/0ASJVEFA9J/), [strong induction](/0KMVFSHFGZ/), [the pigeonhole principle](/0HGVCWQGY3/), [set equality by double inclusion](/MKHVRNW68H/) and [writing proofs](/8JQHJK94RZ/). Do the exercises marked ◦ and • in Chapters 1.2, 1.5, 1.6 and 1.9. That is a complete transition course.

Chapters 1.1 to 1.4 are best read in order. Chapters 1.5 to 1.8 form the core, and 1.9 and 1.10 teach the craft. Chapters 1.11 to 1.14 can be read in any order, and Chapter 1.14 is mainly for teachers.

Each entry stands on its own. Its facts box lists what it needs, with the number of the entry where each prerequisite is explained. Entries in other volumes are cited by number too.

## What you need first

Very little. The examples use school algebra and geometry: even and odd numbers, divisibility, primes, inequalities and triangles. Some examples in Chapters 1.1 to 1.3 and 1.8 use first-year calculus and algebra, such as limits, continuity, vector spaces and groups; a reader who has not met them can pass over those examples. Some entries use [sets](/0BSJ7RQ9BW/) and [functions](/0EPAT51DXQ/) in the plain sense of a first course; their formal theory is elsewhere in the book. Formal logic is not needed, and [Volume 2](/DYJ0KGTPMR/) is there for the reader who wants it.

## The exercises

Each chapter ends with exercises in three bands. Exercises marked ◦ check that you read the entries closely: negate a statement, name the hypothesis of a theorem, find the error in a short argument; each takes a few minutes. Exercises marked • ask you to use a method in a new place, such as proving a small claim by contrapositive or by induction, or building a counterexample; each takes some thought. Exercises marked ★ are harder; some ask for a known result in your own words. Hints for many exercises are in the appendix *Hints to the exercises* at the end of the volume.
