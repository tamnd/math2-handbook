A [mathematical proof](/0B94TKFQJX/) is an argument that shows that a statement follows from axioms, definitions and results proved before, so that it holds in every case and not only in the cases someone checked. The polynomial $n^2 + n + 41$ is prime for $n = 0, 1, \dots, 39$, and these forty cases prove nothing; at $n = 40$ it equals $41^2$. By contrast, writing two even integers as $2a$ and $2b$ gives the sum $2(a + b)$, and that one line covers every pair at once. This volume is about the difference between evidence and proof.

The volume treats proof as mathematicians practise it; proof as a formal object is the subject of [Volume 2](/DYJ0KGTPMR/). Four questions run through the chapters: what a statement says, which pattern of argument fits it, how to write a proof that a reader can check, and how conjectures and proofs are found before either can be written.

## What the volume teaches

- **Reading statements.** Every theorem has [hypotheses and a conclusion](/W48XN2MEM9/). Its [conditionals](/FA6YZGR2SS/) and [quantifiers](/EGTZPA6QH3/) fix what must be proved, and [negating a statement](/K930V33BSC/) correctly is the first step of half the methods.
- **Definitions that carry claims.** A [definition](/8ZCZ3HW91Z/) is read as "if and only if". A rule on representatives must be shown [well defined](/HNGZEBKVY2/), and "the $x$ such that" needs existence and uniqueness.
- **A small set of methods.** [Direct proof](/0NTF6DMVAP/), [contrapositive](/0BB5TPGQ1N/), [contradiction](/0J0XBKG64S/), [cases](/0WCP0PVET6/), construction and [counterexample](/0ED72CTBNP/) cover most of what is written, and the logical form of the goal suggests which to try.
- **Induction and its relatives.** [Mathematical induction](/0ASJVEFA9J/), [strong induction](/0KMVFSHFGZ/) and the [well-ordering principle](/01BX15HSRR/) are three forms of one fact about the natural numbers, and [structural induction](/12MQ2NJ29A/) carries the idea to lists, trees and formulas.
- **General arguments.** The [pigeonhole principle](/0HGVCWQGY3/), [double counting](/0F8YBHF2W3/), [invariants](/8NQ190278Y/) and the [extremal principle](/MD3MRG90W4/) work in every field.
- **Proof as communication.** A proof is written for a reader, and [writing proofs](/8JQHJK94RZ/), [reading proofs](/40GCSM2G56/) and [checking a proof](/4M98ZZNK0E/) are skills to practise.
- **Discovery is not proof.** [Pólya's four steps](/DYD57G2Y5Z/), [plausible reasoning](/8DHD3H1CJ9/) and [experimental mathematics](/BPEPTN0TT9/) guide the search, but a pattern that holds in many cases can still fail.

## The chapters

**Chapter 1.1, Proof, theorems and mathematical method**, explains what a [proof](/0B94TKFQJX/) is, how [deduction differs from induction and abduction](/5V35SGEN1T/), what the labels [theorem, lemma, corollary and proposition](/KK5F0F03ZW/) signal, and what the [hypotheses and conclusion](/W48XN2MEM9/) of a theorem are. It treats the [axiomatic method](/GD2JBXJV5Z/), [conjectures](/WMEMMHZ3BH/), the [functions of proof](/ENY0GKRJS8/) beyond verification, the [methodology of mathematics](/3FJ3XMV9KD/), the uses of examples, abstraction and [classification](/9HQ3TADPA8/) in mathematical practice, and the two temperaments of [problem solvers and theory builders](/Z29HM45R29/).

**Chapter 1.2, The language of mathematical statements**, reads [statements](/S9MHMRAX88/) word by word: connectives, [conditionals](/FA6YZGR2SS/), [quantifiers](/EGTZPA6QH3/), [negation](/K930V33BSC/), [existence and uniqueness](/ACSC75R7R5/) and the ambiguities of mathematical English. It ends with the [Wason selection task](/7GYZJ2MGY9/), a test of how people read a conditional.

**Chapter 1.3, Definitions in mathematics**, sets out the logical form of a [definition](/8ZCZ3HW91Z/), its kinds, the duty to prove that a definition is [well-defined](/HNGZEBKVY2/), [equivalent definitions](/NZ42PS2P1A/), the phrase [up to](/CBC72TX3NQ/), [definite descriptions](/5P955TVSS2/), the [primitive notions](/QMQEJKFEYZ/) that every definition rests on, and why good definitions take the form they do.

**Chapter 1.4, Notation, conventions and jargon**, explains [mathematical notation](/QKEX4PD029/), the [conflicting conventions](/CSFEP56A7D/) for symbols such as $\N$, $\subset$ and $f^{-1}$, safe [abuses of notation](/JV68YRB5YZ/), and the difference between $=$, $:=$, $\equiv$ and $\cong$ in [equality, definition and equivalence signs](/BCGR3NPPXC/). It then reads the jargon as exact claims: [trivial, obvious and clearly](/Y0HXCJRB9B/), [canonical and natural](/F8WNADZFMM/), [sufficiently large](/FEQ7WATMHF/) as a pair of quantifiers, [stronger and weaker statements](/KCK4AQ469Q/) and [sharp results](/KWQV0G0VJ3/).

**Chapter 1.5, Basic proof methods**, is the core of a transition course. After the map of [proof methods](/G9JFJGY4TQ/) come [direct proof](/0NTF6DMVAP/), [proving universal statements](/CX38X989TB/), [contrapositive](/0BB5TPGQ1N/), [contradiction](/0J0XBKG64S/), [cases](/0WCP0PVET6/) and [biconditionals](/TFQ1845T84/). Existence is proved by [construction](/01XM7JEKHY/) or [without a witness](/JZQVJ9XT63/), and [uniqueness](/GQHHNV79PA/). The chapter ends with the [counterexample](/0ED72CTBNP/), [without loss of generality](/Y7NE0E6PCV/), the [forward-backward method](/V8ZB838JY5/) and [proofs of impossibility](/6F55216R53/).

**Chapter 1.6, Induction and well-ordering as proof methods**, covers [mathematical induction](/0ASJVEFA9J/), [strong induction](/0KMVFSHFGZ/), the [well-ordering principle](/01BX15HSRR/) and the [equivalence](/J2P9FWBP3Q/) of the three. The [minimal counterexample](/JGRCNPKNGG/) and [infinite descent](/0EFYCFHYFG/) argue from a smallest failure, and [strengthening the induction hypothesis](/F94KJC2RXP/) rescues a stuck step. Later entries treat [double](/AABX4YPKRR/) and [forward-backward induction](/QC9FKGZH1V/), [structural induction](/12MQ2NJ29A/) and [induction on size](/B2D8PTFNN1/), and the chapter closes with the [errors in induction proofs](/V52HDTMPV5/) that [all horses are the same color](/AJ2FDJJGTH/) makes famous.

**Chapter 1.7, Counting, invariant and extremal arguments**, starts with the [pigeonhole principle](/0HGVCWQGY3/) and its [generalized](/0BY7GN3449/) and [infinite](/0FS3RHF6KE/) forms. It continues with [double counting](/0F8YBHF2W3/) and [combinatorial and bijective proof](/SS07J9ZAQ7/), then [invariants](/8NQ190278Y/), [monovariants](/TP3GC5J08D/), [parity arguments](/H9JT1XNDKQ/) and [coloring proofs](/90Q4PK1CST/). The last entries use the [extremal principle](/MD3MRG90W4/), the [averaging argument](/HXP9VMXV8S/), [counting arguments for existence](/1KVSS0KRZA/), [probabilistic proofs](/6YY8W7HG5M/) and [diagonalization](/V2H30SA654/).

**Chapter 1.8, Proof patterns for common goals**, matches a template to each common goal: [set equality by double inclusion](/MKHVRNW68H/), [injectivity and surjectivity](/DD2P85Y8E9/), [equivalence relations](/RGNF33GVHX/), [equality by two inequalities](/S3QADD13E9/) and [identities](/XP5SDYKJX0/). For analysis it gives [epsilon-delta proofs](/RXG5JTZ2PZ/), the [epsilon over three argument](/VXV7M0QDH6/), [density arguments](/J2J35J3055/), the [open-closed argument](/1JJCDJB7FM/) and [bootstrapping](/YKA0ADKYFR/). **Chapter 1.9, Writing and reading proofs**, is about the craft. [Writing proofs](/8JQHJK94RZ/) states the core habits, [scratch work](/E97XXFW9MV/) separates the search from the final text, and [proof strategies by logical form](/872K5XB580/) and [unpacking definitions](/41JZ39GE3F/) suggest the first move. **Chapter 1.10, Fallacies and errors in proofs**, shows what goes wrong. **Chapter 1.11, Heuristics of discovery**, follows Pólya. After [heuristics in mathematics](/6KCEWEP4WA/) and [the four steps](/DYD57G2Y5Z/) it treats [analogy](/7KDT0R1HF2/), [specialization](/FTBJX4G51S/), [generalization](/8NJ0VN9Z97/), the [inventor's paradox](/FVRKDXBJF6/), [working backwards](/3QTB9S1THS/), [auxiliary problems](/DYSJB93V11/) and [auxiliary elements](/8TKKRAXKFN/). **Chapter 1.12, Plausible reasoning and experimental mathematics**, explains Pólya's [plausible reasoning](/8DHD3H1CJ9/) and [experimental mathematics](/BPEPTN0TT9/). ## How to read this volume

If this is your first course on proof, read these entries in this order: [mathematical proof](/0B94TKFQJX/), [hypotheses and conclusion](/W48XN2MEM9/), [conditional statements](/FA6YZGR2SS/), [quantifiers](/EGTZPA6QH3/), [negating statements](/K930V33BSC/), [mathematical definition](/8ZCZ3HW91Z/), [proof methods](/G9JFJGY4TQ/), [direct proof](/0NTF6DMVAP/), [proving universal statements](/CX38X989TB/), [proof by contrapositive](/0BB5TPGQ1N/), [proof by contradiction](/0J0XBKG64S/), [proof by cases](/0WCP0PVET6/), [counterexample](/0ED72CTBNP/), [mathematical induction](/0ASJVEFA9J/), [strong induction](/0KMVFSHFGZ/), [the pigeonhole principle](/0HGVCWQGY3/), [set equality by double inclusion](/MKHVRNW68H/), [writing proofs](/8JQHJK94RZ/) and [checking a proof](/4M98ZZNK0E/). Do the exercises marked ◦ and • in Chapters 1.2, 1.5, 1.6 and 1.9. That is a complete transition course.

Chapters 1.1 to 1.4 are best read in order. Chapters 1.5 to 1.8 form the core, and Chapters 1.9 and 1.10 teach the craft. Chapters 1.11 to 1.14 can be read in any order after the core. For competitions, go on to Chapters 1.7 and 1.11; before analysis, read the analysis patterns of Chapter 1.8; to teach proof, read Chapter 1.14 with Chapters 1.9 and 1.10.

## What you need first

Very little. Most examples use school algebra and geometry: even and odd numbers, divisibility, primes, inequalities and triangles. Examples that use first-year calculus or algebra, such as limits or groups, say so at the start and can be skipped. Some entries use [sets](/0BSJ7RQ9BW/) and [functions](/0EPAT51DXQ/) in the plain sense of a first course. Chapter 1.7 uses the rules of sum and product and binomial coefficients. Formal logic is not needed.

## The exercises

Each chapter ends with exercises in three bands. Exercises marked ◦ check close reading, such as negating a statement or finding the error in a short argument. Exercises marked • use a method in a new place, such as a proof by contrapositive or a counterexample. Exercises marked ★ are harder, and some ask for a known result in your own words. Many have hints in the appendix *Hints to the exercises*.
