Add three consecutive integers: $4 + 5 + 6 = 15$, $10 + 11 + 12 = 33$, $(-2) + (-1) + 0 = -3$. Each sum is a multiple of $3$. A student's scratch page explains why in one line:

$$n - 1 + n + n + 1 = 3n \quad \text{done}$$

The line holds the whole idea, and as a proof it still fails. It does not say what $n$ is, or that the three integers have been written as $n - 1$, $n$ and $n + 1$. It does not say what is claimed, and the word "done" stands in for the conclusion. A reader who did not do the scratch work has to rebuild all of it. Written for that reader, the proof reads as follows.

> Let three consecutive integers be given, and let $n$ be the middle one, so that the three are $n - 1$, $n$ and $n + 1$, where $n$ is an integer. Their sum is $(n - 1) + n + (n + 1) = 3n$. Since $n$ is an integer, the sum is divisible by $3$. $\square$

Each letter is introduced before it is used, each line is a sentence, and the last sentence is the claim.

Reading runs the other way. Suppose someone claims more: the sum of $k$ consecutive integers is always divisible by $k$, "because the sum is $k$ times the middle term". A careful reader tests the claim on a case the writer may not have pictured. For $k = 4$, the sum $1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10$ is not divisible by $4$. The argument breaks at its first step, since four consecutive integers have no middle term. The claim holds exactly when $k$ is odd, and the argument proves exactly that case. The test did more than refute the claim: it showed which line was wrong and which hypothesis was missing.

The chapter is about these two jobs, writing a proof that a stranger can check, and reading a proof as that stranger. It starts with the plan of a proof, which comes from the logical form of the goal and from the definitions in it. It then turns to the words and layouts that carry a proof, from "let" and "suppose" to two-column tables and numbered structured proofs. It asks how much detail a reader needs and how sentences with formulas should look. The last group runs the same skills backward, as reading, checking and refereeing, and ends with the shape of a research paper.

## What the chapter covers

1. **From the plan to the written proof.** [Writing proofs](/8JQHJK94RZ/) gives the core habits. [Scratch work and the final proof](/E97XXFW9MV/) separates the search for a proof from its write-up. Its examples use the methods of [basic proof methods](/958EF46EYJ/), such as [direct proof](/0NTF6DMVAP/), the [contrapositive](/0BB5TPGQ1N/), [proof by contradiction](/0J0XBKG64S/) and [proof by cases](/0WCP0PVET6/), and the symbols, abbreviations and end-of-proof marks of [notation, conventions and jargon](/Z2WNQ1DNTX/).
